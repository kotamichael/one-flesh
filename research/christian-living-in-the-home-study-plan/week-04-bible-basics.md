# Week 4 — "Bible Basics About the Family" (pp. 43-57)

This is the doctrinal core of the book and the richest cross-reference week in this whole plan —
it's where Adams lays out the foundational theology the rest of the book's practical counsel
stands on.

## What's actually in the chapter (mixed confidence — see below)

CONFIRMED page range (43-57). Content clustered from gracequotes.org page-citations: biblical
covenants (p. 45), marriage as the normal/expected state (p. 47 — possibly ch. 5 material given
page overlap, flagged as uncertain), "becoming one flesh" (p. 48), sexuality/sexual satisfaction
in marriage (p. 50, twice). This suggests a covenant theology of marriage, Gen. 2:24's one-flesh
union, and a doctrinal (not casework) treatment of marital sexuality — but exact verse citations
beyond Gen. 2:24 were not recovered; don't attribute chapter/verse beyond what's stated here.

**The single most important open question for this week, per this project's own standing
discipline**: whether this chapter gives the husband's and wife's roles equal doctrinal depth, or
whether the "basics" lean husband-heavy. Every description located so far lists "wives' roles,
husband's roles... basic theological understandings" as chapter content, in that order, but gives
no indication of relative depth. **This is a live question for your own read, not a settled
finding either direction** — worth tracking deliberately as you go, per the project's rule that

## Cross-references — your physical books first, this is the deep well

**Calvin's commentaries — all four/six volumes are relevant here.** This is the week to have
Genesis (2:18-24, 3:16), Galatians & Ephesians (5:22-33), 1 Corinthians (11:2-16), and the
Catholic Epistles (1 Pet. 3:1-7) open together, since Adams's "basics" chapter is presumably
covering the same doctrinal ground Calvin covers at much greater exegetical depth. Calvin's
signature move on Gen. 3:16 — "She had, indeed, previously been subject to her husband, but that
was a liberal and gentle subjection; now, however, she is cast into servitude" — is the classic
Reformed statement of how creation and fall relate on this question, and a good stress-test for
whatever Adams's own "basics" turn out to assume.

**Zanchi's *Spiritual Marriage*, Ch. 1 ("The Creation of Eve and Her Marriage to Adam").** If
Adams's chapter covers Gen. 2:18-24 as part of its "basics," Zanchi's opening chapter is the
deepest available primary-source treatment of the same material in your library — read them side
by side.

**Perkins' *Christian Oeconomie*.** Perkins's own "basics" move is a definitional one: marriage is
a relation where one is "higher, and beareth rule," the other "lower, and yieldeth subjection" —
grounded in 1 Cor. 11:7 ("the male is man of a superior sex... the female is woman of an inferior
sex, fit to conceive and bear children"), primarily from creation rather than the Fall. Compare
this directly to whatever grounding Adams uses — creation, fall, both, or (per Adams's general
method) something more pragmatic/directive than either.

**Palmer's *The Family*, Part I Chapters II-III** ("Supremacy of the Husband," "Subordination of
the Wife"). Palmer opens ch. II by quoting Col. 3:19 and Eph. 5:25,28,33 directly, then works
through his own numbered points on why love (not bare authority) is the husband's paramount
apostolic duty — good source material if Adams's "basics" touch on the husband's role at all in
this chapter (separate from the dedicated ch. 7).

**Gouge's full six-proof case for headship** (per this project's Strand 2 research): Rom. 13:1's
ordained power, "nature" itself, titles like Lord/Master/Head, the Eph. 5:23 Christ-Church
typology, Gen. 2:18, and 1 Cor. 11:7's head-covering — his fullest single statement of the
creation-grounded case for headship, and a useful checklist against whatever grounds Adams
actually gives.

## Confessional cross-reference

The cross-reference list above (Calvin, Zanchi, Perkins, Palmer, Gouge) is built entirely from the
household-duty tradition's headship case. But this chapter's own *confirmed* content — covenant
theology, Gen. 2:24's one-flesh union, and marital sexuality (see page-citations above) — actually
maps most directly onto **WCF 24 ("Of Marriage and Divorce")**, not onto WLC 123-133.

WCF 24.2 states marriage's purpose in exactly this three-part shape (verbatim, checked directly
against thewestminsterstandard.org's proof-texted edition): "Marriage was ordained for the mutual
help of husband and wife; for the increase of mankind with a legitimate issue, and of the Church
with an holy seed; and for preventing of uncleanness." That's a close match for this chapter's
apparent shape: mutual help (the covenant-relationship material), legitimate issue (procreation
within covenant), and preventing uncleanness (the sexuality/sexual-satisfaction material at p. 50).
Worth naming that match explicitly to the group — this is claim-(a)-level, direct confessional
wording, not an inference, per this project's confessional-citation discipline.

**Don't reach for WLC 123-133 here for the covenant/one-flesh/sexuality material** — that's WCF
24's territory specifically, and the project's own house rule (CLAUDE.md, "Confessional citation")
warns against conflating the two loci. WLC 123-133 only becomes the relevant cross-reference *if*
this chapter's "wives' roles, husband's roles" content (noted above) turns out to be more than a
chapter-list label — i.e., if Adams actually assigns headship/submission duties here rather than
saving that for ch. 6/7. If so, Q129's Col. 3:19 / 1 Pet. 3:7 proofs and Q127's 1 Pet. 3:6 proof
(husband-superior, wife-inferior, per the Assembly's own annexed Scripture proofs) are the specific
citations to use — not the bare Q124/126 "superiors, inferiors, or equals" framework in the
abstract.

## Where the sources disagree — the big one: does headship originate at creation, or only at the Fall?

This is the sharpest fault line in this project's entire bibliography, and this "basics" chapter is
the natural place to bring it into the room, since it's exactly the kind of foundational question a
"basics" chapter has to take some position on (even if only implicitly).

- **Creation-only, sharply**: **Musculus** draws the starkest line of anyone in this bibliography —
  Gen. 2:24 (the pre-Fall "law of wedlock") has *no rule-language at all*; a distinct, explicitly
  post-Fall "law" (Gen. 3:16) is what first introduces "the woman is commanded to be subject."
- **Creation-grounded, fall-confirms**: **Calvin**'s "gentle subjection, then servitude" — headship
  *predates* the Fall but the Fall *intensifies* it. **Richard Steele**, **Perkins**, and **Daniel
  Rogers** lean the same direction. **Palmer** takes the strongest version among the Americans: the
  Fall changes only the *mode of enforcement* ("positive statute"), not the content of headship at
  all.
- **Fall-grounded, explicitly**: **Cleaver & Dod** — "the first subjection of the woman began at
  sin," full stop.
- **Both-together, deliberately**: **Gouge** visibly wrestles with holding both grounds at once —
  Gen. 3:16 as punishment *and* "a law too, for trial of her obedience," plus a separate
  creation-order argument.
- **The most totalizing version — punishment, not mere intensification**: **Dabney** goes further
  than anyone else here: the wife's subordination is itself, in part, **punishment for Eve's sin,
  imputed to all women ever since** — not a pre-existing order intensified, but a penal consequence
  extended by imputation. Track this as Dabney's own distinctive, outlier move, not "the Reformed
  view." **Palmer and Dabney are the same region, the same era, and genuinely disagree** — worth
  naming as the sharpest same-strand tension this project has found.

**Discussion question**: whichever ground Adams uses (or doesn't clearly state) in this chapter,
which of these five positions does it sound closest to? Nouthetic counselors tend to be
pragmatic/directive rather than systematic — he may not tip his hand clearly, which is itself
worth noting.

## Where the sources disagree — mutuality: load-bearing theme, or backdrop?

Every figure this project has researched affirms mutual duty in some form, but weights it very
differently. **Baxter** and **Rogers** foreground mutual love/companionship most heavily — Baxter
opens his household chapters with symmetrical framing ("the first duty of husbands is to love
their wives [and wives their husbands]") before any differentiated-authority language at all.
**Gouge** places 45 sections of *common-mutual* duties textually and logically *before* any
sex-specific duty. **Calvin**'s reciprocal head/body "offices" language (1 Cor. 11) is his own
version of the same instinct. By contrast, **Dabney**'s argument is comparatively thin on
mutuality — polemical-occasional (answering 1870s suffrage debates), not constructive-companionate.
Worth asking: does Adams's "basics" chapter lead with mutuality (the way Gouge and Baxter do) or
with differentiated roles first?

## This week's practice

Doctrinal weeks call for meditation more than task-based homework. **Spend time this week simply
sitting with Gen. 2:24** — not analyzing it, but letting "one flesh" describe your actual marriage.
Where does the union feel real and lived-out; where does it feel more like two separate lives
sharing a house?

**Heart-related, from Gouge**: "Love is as sugar to sweeten the duties of authority... Respect is
as salt to season... subjection." Whatever doctrinal position you land on this week regarding
creation/Fall, let this be the practical test: is authority in your home sweetened by love, is
submission seasoned by respect — or has either curdled into bare duty without the disposition
behind it?
