# Study plan — Jay Adams, *Christian Living in the Home* (church series, one chapter/week)

**Purpose:** prep reading for each week of the church's chapter-by-chapter series, cross-referencing
this project's own historical/exegetical research — with special weight on the four works you now
own physical copies of: **Perkins' *Christian Oeconomie***, **Zanchi's *Spiritual Marriage***,
**Palmer's *The Family***, and **Calvin's Commentaries**.

## How each week's document is built

Each week's file has the same core shape:

- **What this chapter is likely to cover** — based on its title, adjacent confirmed quotes, and
  Adams's general nouthetic method (concrete, directive, Scripture-as-command). Marked honestly
  where this project has direct research on the chapter (6, 7, 9) versus where it's an informed
  guess (the others) — you'll be the one confirming against the actual text.
- **A memory verse (or short couplet of verses), drawn from one coherent passage** — not a
  scattered grab-bag across books. Where Adams's own citation for that chapter is confirmed, it's
  used directly; where it isn't (week 2 especially), that's said plainly rather than forcing a fit.
- **Cross-references from this project**, organized with the physically-owned works flagged
  first and specifically — Perkins, Zanchi, Palmer, Calvin — each with the closest locator this
  project's notes have (chapter/section/page), so you can go straight to the passage. Other
  strand material (Gouge, Bullinger, Calvin's contemporaries, Dabney/Palmer's Southern
  Presbyterian material, Garris) follows where relevant.
- **"Where the sources disagree," on weeks 4, 6, and 7 specifically** — the real fault lines this
  project has tracked across five centuries (creation-vs-Fall grounding, how much weight "helper"
  carries, how "weaker vessel" gets used, Aristotelian vs. purely biblical categories, how much
  mutuality is foregrounded), placed in whichever week they're most relevant to rather than
  gathered in one separate document. Meant as live discussion fuel — "which of these is Adams
  actually doing?" — not trivia.
- **This week's practice** — built primarily from Adams's own homework-style material (his method
  is directive and concrete, not reflection-only, so the practice sections lean that way too where
  his content supports it) plus a "heart-related" prompt drawn from whichever historical author
  has the most relevant affective/dispositional material for that week — not just doctrine stated
  abstractly, per CLAUDE.md's own register rule.
- **Things to look for / discussion questions** — bridges Adams's practical register with the
  deeper exegetical and historical material, usable for personal prep or for leading discussion.
- **A caution, where one exists** — this project doesn't import outside critique of Adams's
  counseling framework as a standing position (see CLAUDE.md), but where a reception note is
  genuinely relevant to leading a class well, it's flagged plainly as a "watch for this," not as
  this project's own verdict on Adams.
- **A "Reading notes" section at the end, to fill in as you go** — one combined place (not a
  separate file) covering both what Adams's chapter actually says (closing this project's own
  research gaps on chapters 1, 2, 3, 5, and 8 especially, which were reconstructed from secondary
  sources) *and* what you found in that week's outside reading (Calvin, Zanchi, Perkins, Palmer,
  and other strand material) — including a direct pointer to which project file to update if
  something's worth feeding back. Week 6 flags this most strongly: Zanchi's Ch. 5 is the single
  most important unread primary source in the whole project, and this is the natural week to
  finally read it.

## Chapters with the richest cross-reference material

Not evenly distributed — chapters 4, 6, 7, and 9 map directly onto this project's core research
(doctrine, the wife's role, the husband's role, and 1 Pet 3's submission-under-hardship material,
respectively) and have the deepest weekly documents. Chapters 2, 3, 5, and 8 are more general
pastoral-counseling or parent-child territory that this project's marriage-roles-focused
bibliography doesn't cover as directly — those weeks say so honestly rather than force a
connection that isn't really there. Chapter 1 sits in between: general theological framing with a
strong direct parallel in Palmer's own opening chapter.
