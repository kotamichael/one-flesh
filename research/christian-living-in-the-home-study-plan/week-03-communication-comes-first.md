# Week 3 — "Communication Comes First"

## What's actually in the chapter (WELL-ATTESTED, built from real verbatim quotes)

The throughline, reconstructed from clustered quotes at pp. 31-41: family conflict — including
sexual difficulty in marriage — is downstream of unaddressed anger and un-pursued reconciliation,
and biblical love is willed action, not feeling.

> "Anger itself is not sinful; every emotion is from God and is good when biblically appropriate.
> But, 'Do not let the sun go down on your anger,' Paul says (Eph. 4:26)." — p. 31

> "There are usually at least two problems involved in any human conflict. There is the issue over
> which the parties differ, and there is also the problem of their attitudes toward one another."
> — p. 39

> "Jesus won't allow the unreconciled condition to continue among believers. In Matthew 5... In
> Matthew 18... There is never a time when you can sit and wait for your brother to come to you...
> It is always your obligation to go." — pp. 36-37

> "Husbands and wives frequently complain that they are having trouble with sex, but the trouble
> usually is not with sex... The real difficulties in bed at night come from the fact that all
> kinds of problems have been carried into bed from the day, problems that should have been
> settled before going to bed." — p. 36

> "Love at first is not feeling. Love first can be expressed as giving. That is at the core of
> love. If one gives, the feeling of love will follow." — p. 41

## Who Adams is arguing against here

Two targets, both consistent with his wider corpus: (1) any account of marital conflict that
locates the problem "in the bedroom" rather than in unresolved relational sin carried there from
elsewhere — a direct application of his general sin-not-sickness method to a specific, sensitive
topic; (2) a sentimental view of love as feeling rather than willed action — a recurring nouthetic
target across evangelical pop-psychology of the era. Not confirmed as named opponents in the text
itself.

## Cross-references — your physical books first

This is genuinely the chapter with the *least* direct historical-strand parallel in this
project's bibliography — "communication" as its own named category is a modern counseling
category; Gouge, Perkins, Zanchi, and Calvin don't organize their material this way. That's worth
saying plainly in discussion rather than forcing a fit. The closest real parallels:

**Gouge's *Of Domesticall Duties*, Treatise II** — his 45 sections of *common-mutual* duties
(love, cohabitation, mutual prayer, care for each other's salvation) that he places *before* any
sex-specific duty. Not "communication" in Adams's sense, but the same underlying instinct that the
relationship itself needs tending before role-specific duties can function well.

**Cleaver & Dod's *A Godly Form of Household Government*** (per Strand 2 research) provides for a
wife's respectful objection to a difficult command — "she may for her excuse gently propound the
same" — a genuine historical precedent for a communication ethic within headship/submission, even
though it's not framed as "communication" the way Adams frames it.

**Calvin's Ephesians commentary** on Eph. 4:26 itself, if your Ephesians volume includes chapter 4
alongside chapter 5 — worth checking what Calvin himself says about the anger text Adams builds
this chapter on, since it's the one piece of Scripture this chapter confirms citing directly.

## This week's practice

**Directly from Adams's own material — this is about as close to his actual assigned homework
style as this project's research reaches**: identify one unresolved conflict, however small,
that's been carried past sundown more than once this week or last. Per Matt. 5 and Matt. 18 as
Adams uses them here, "it is always your obligation to go" — regardless of who was at fault. The
homework is concrete and specific: go, this week, before the next sundown passes on it again.

**Heart-related**: sit with Adams's own claim that "love first can be expressed as giving... if
one gives, the feeling of love will follow" before treating the homework above as mere conflict
management. The point isn't technique; it's that giving precedes and produces the feeling, not the
reverse — a genuinely searching claim about where love actually starts.

## Things to look for / discussion questions

- Adams's claim that sexual difficulty in marriage is usually a downstream symptom of unresolved
  daytime conflict, not a bedroom problem per se — is this too totalizing, or does it match
  ordinary pastoral experience? A good question for a group with real marriages in the room.
- "It is always your obligation to go" (reconciliation) — how does this square with, or
  complicate, headship/submission dynamics? Does Adams say anything about whether this obligation
  falls differently on husband and wife, or is it symmetrical here? Worth noting either way per
  this project's symmetric-treatment discipline (see CLAUDE.md).
- Where does this chapter's "love is willed action" claim sit relative to Eph. 5:25's command to
  husbands specifically ("love your wives") — is Adams here making a general point about love in
  any relationship, or specifically unpacking the husband's Eph. 5 duty? Worth checking against
  the actual text.
