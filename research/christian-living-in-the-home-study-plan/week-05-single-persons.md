# Week 5 — "Single Persons"

## What's actually in the chapter (well-covered by real, clustered quotes)

> "The normal state is marriage, not celibacy. A man and wife — not single persons — were put into
> the garden... God ordained marriage for His purposes." — p. 47 (citing Gen. 2:18, Gen. 2:24
> directly)

> "If she has been given the gift of living as a single person, she must be willing to do so,
> prepare to do so, and look for the work in [the] Lord's vineyard that doubtless He has for
> her... the Lord never calls His children without providing them the help that they need." — p.
> 61

> "A vital Christian, radiating that hidden beauty of the heart, is more attractive to the right
> sort of Christian man... A woman who is developing her domestic abilities, who is reasonably
> attractive, and who is a vital Christian in her own right is an irresistible person." — p. 62

> "How, then, may one know how to choose a mate? There are only two absolutely essential
> requirements: first, that the other person is also a Christian; second, that the two of you not
> only desire to but growingly give evidence of an ability to face, talk over, and solve problems
> together from God's Word in God's way... [other factors] are in no sense essentials. Indeed,
> they are icing on the cake." — p. 64

Scripture engaged: Gen. 2:18, Gen. 2:24 directly; possibly 1 Cor. 7's "gift" language behind the
p. 61 material, though not confirmed as an explicit citation; "hidden beauty of the heart" (p. 62)
plausibly alludes to 1 Pet. 3:3-4's "hidden man of the heart" — also not confirmed as a named
citation.

**Worth noting directly**: pp. 61-64 are addressed specifically to single *women* — her own
initiative, her own character-not-appearance, her own agency in discerning a mate. This is real,
positive content about a woman's own life and calling, in Adams's own words, even though it's
pre-marital rather than an in-marriage duty.

## Who Adams is arguing against here

Two candidates: a view that treats singleness as failure or lesser spiritual status (against
which "the normal state is marriage, not celibacy" pushes, while still making room for those
"given the gift"); and, per one reader's review, a period-bound mate-selection framework — the
material ranking "socio-economic, ethnic, chronological... factors" as mere "icing on the cake"
reads distinctly as a 1972 pastoral response to that era's own social pressures around
who's an acceptable match. Worth naming this as dated cultural context if it comes up in
discussion, rather than treating every specific of pp. 61-64 as timeless principle.

## Cross-references — your physical books

This is the chapter with the least direct parallel in your four physical books — none of Perkins,
Zanchi, Palmer, or Calvin's commentaries treat singleness as its own topic (Zanchi's whole
project, for instance, is about marriage as a positive theological type; he has nothing to say
about the unmarried state as such). Say so plainly rather than forcing a connection.

The one genuine parallel worth pulling: **Katharina Schütz Zell** (per this project's Strand 1
research, not a book you own, but worth knowing about) is this project's clearest primary-source
example of a woman's own claim to competence and calling stated in her own voice, in a marriage —
a useful complement, though not a substitute, to Adams's single-woman material here, since Zell
writes as a wife, not from singleness.

## This week's practice

**For those married in the room**: Adams's own "two absolutely essential requirements" test (a
shared faith, and a growing, demonstrated ability to solve problems together from Scripture) works
just as well retrospectively. This week, ask concretely: where has "icing on the cake" material
(compatibility of temperament, background, preference) quietly become load-bearing in how you
evaluate your marriage day to day, displacing the two things Adams says actually matter?

**For those single in the room**: the p. 61 homework is already concrete in Adams's own words —
"prepare... look for the work in the Lord's vineyard" rather than treating singleness as a waiting
room. Name one specific way to do that this week.

**Heart-related**: "a vital Christian, radiating that hidden beauty of the heart" (p. 62) — worth
sitting with regardless of marital status, married or single, as a corrective to evaluating anyone
(spouse, self, a prospective mate) primarily on external terms.

## Things to look for / discussion questions

- How does the chapter's treatment of a single woman's own initiative and character connect (or
  not) to the wife's-side material coming in ch. 6? Worth flagging as a thread to watch across the
  two weeks.
- Is there a parallel passage addressed to single *men*, with the same specificity as pp. 61-64
  address single women? If not, that's worth naming as an asymmetry in this particular chapter,
  distinct from (and probably less significant than) the husband/wife asymmetry question tracked
  elsewhere in this plan.
- Discussion angle for a mixed group: which of Adams's mate-selection criteria (two "absolutely
  essential," everything else "icing on the cake") still hold up, and which read as dated to 1972?
