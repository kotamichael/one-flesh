# Systematic note — nature, grace, and marriage's typological surplus

**Lane:** systematic-theologian
**Task:** formalize the two-tier claim (general revelation reaches marriage's basic structural
order; special revelation in Christ discloses its mystical/typological content, progressively) for
Series I's opening article, and test it honestly rather than simply defend it.
**Researched on:** 2026-08-21. Confessional text below verified directly against two independent
online transcriptions per citation (converging exactly in each case) rather than quoted from
memory; not yet checked against a physical 1646/1647 printing or the Van Dixhoorn critical edition.
Mark the confessional *text* CONFIRMED on that basis; mark every *systematic move built on it*
according to the contestability scale below, since that's the actual point of this note.

**Confidence idiom for this note** (adapting CONFIRMED/WELL-ATTESTED/UNVERIFIED to systematic
argument, per instructions): **SOUND** = the inferential move is logically tight and its premises

## 1. WCF 7.5–6, quoted precisely, and the verdict on extending it to marriage

**Text (CONFIRMED, two independent transcriptions converging exactly):**

> 7.5. This covenant was differently administered in the time of the law, and in the time of the
> gospel: under the law it was administered by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the
> paschal lamb, and other types and ordinances delivered to the people of the Jews, all
> fore-signifying Christ to come, which were for that time sufficient and efficacious, through the
> operation of the Spirit, to instruct and build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by
> whom they had full remission of sins, and eternal salvation, and is called the Old Testament.
>
> 7.6. Under the gospel, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the ordinances in which this
> covenant is dispensed are the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of
> Baptism and the Lord's Supper; which, though fewer in number, and administered with more
> simplicity and less outward glory, yet in them it is held forth in more fulness, evidence, and
> spiritual efficacy, to all nations, both Jews and Gentiles; and is called the New Testament. There
> are not therefore two covenants of grace differing in substance, but one and the same under
> various dispensations.

**Verdict: this is a legitimate but genuinely extended application, not a direct citation, and the
manifesto/article prose needs to say so explicitly rather than write "per WCF 7.5-6" as if the
Confession is on-topic.**

Where the extension is sound: WCF 7.5-6's *subject* is narrowly and explicitly "this covenant" —
the covenant of grace's own mode of administration through its own ordinances (promises,
prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the paschal lamb; then Word and sacraments). It says nothing
about marriage, and marriage is not one of the ordinances in view — WCF 24 keeps marriage a
creation-ordinance question entirely apart from the covenant-of-grace material in chapter 7, and
this project already guards that separation carefully for other reasons (CLAUDE.md's WCF 24 vs.
WLC 123-133 discipline). So a sentence like "WCF 7.5-6 teaches that marriage was administered
typologically under the Old Testament and more fully under the New" would misstate what the
Confession itself asserts. **STRETCH if phrased that way — don't let this into final prose.**

What legitimately transfers is not the Confession's specific subject matter but its underlying
*hermeneutical and redemptive-historical principle*: one reality (here, the covenant of grace;
there, Christ's own eternal purpose for marriage) really present and really operative under both
administrations, but disclosed with "more fulness, evidence, and efficacy" only once Christ, "the
substance," was historically "exhibited" — not a change in the underlying reality, only in how much
of it could be seen. That principle is not a one-off feature of chapter 7 alone; it recurs
independently at **WLC 34-35** (CONFIRMED, near-verbatim restatement — "when Christ the substance
was exhibited... grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness, evidence, and efficacy") and
at **WCF 8.6** (CONFIRMED — "the virtue, efficacy, and benefits [of Christ's redemption]... were
communicated unto the elect, in all ages successively from the beginning of the world, in and by
those promises, types, and sacrifices, wherein he was revealed... the Lamb slain from the beginning
of the world"). Three independent loci in the Standards converge on the same shape: **type/shadow
now, substance disclosed later, one underlying reality throughout, no discontinuity in what is real
even though there is real discontinuity in what can be known.** That is a general Reformed
commitment about how redemptive history discloses itself, for which WCF 7.5-6 is the cleanest
single statement, not its only instance — which is exactly why it's *available* to be applied,
by extension, to a different instance of the same general shape (marriage's typological content)
without claiming the Confession itself made that further application. **SOUND**, so long as it's
presented as an extension of a recurring confessional pattern, not a citation of on-topic content.

**A refinement worth building into the article, found in the course of checking this rather than
assumed going in**: WCF 7.5-6 is the wrong confessional locus to carry the *hermeneutical warrant*
for reading Gen 2's creation of Eve in light of Eph 5:32's disclosure in the first place — that
warrant is supplied by **WCF 1.9** (CONFIRMED): "The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture
is the Scripture itself: and therefore, when there is a question about the true and full sense of
any Scripture... it must be searched and known by other places that speak more clearly." This is
the Confession's own statement of the *analogia Scripturae* — read the less clear by the more
clear — and it is the actual confessional justification for treating Eph 5:32 as authoritative
commentary on what Gen 2:18-24 was always doing, rather than a later evangelist's opportunistic
overlay onto an unrelated creation account. WCF 7.5-6 then supplies a second and different thing:
not the license to connect the two texts, but the *timing/epistemics* vocabulary for why OT
believers, reading Gen 2 without Eph 5:32 in hand, would not have had this content "in fulness" —
this is a difference of degree, not of two unrelated warrants. **Keep these two loci distinct in
the drafted article** — one answers "may we read Genesis through Ephesians," the other answers
"why wouldn't Moses' contemporaries have seen what Paul later names." Collapsing them into a single
"per WCF 7" footnote would flatten a genuine two-part argument into one under-specified citation.

## 2. The two-tier claim, formalized

**Tier 1 — general revelation / the natural order.** Claim: sexual differentiation as such, and
some real, asymmetric relational ordering between husband and wife (not mere undifferentiated
mutuality), are facts about the created order itself — in principle available to reasoned
reflection on nature, independent of the special revelation that discloses Christ.

*Warrant.* Rom 1:19-20's general-revelation category (God's "eternal power and Godhead" "clearly
seen, being understood by the things that are made") is echoed at the Confession's own opening
line, **WCF 1.1** (CONFIRMED): "the light of nature, and the works of creation and providence, do
so far manifest the goodness, wisdom, and power of God, as to leave men unexcusable." If the
Reformed tradition holds that general revelation discloses *something* true and real about God's
nature and moral order to unaided reason — corrupted in reception (Rom 1:21-23), but real in
content — the same logic extends to a specific creational structure like sexual differentiation and
its ordering, which is, after all, a created datum available to observation the same way any other
feature of "the things that are made" is. This is not speculative for this project — it is already
*practiced*, not just theorized, by figures the historical-researcher lane has already secured:
**Perkins** grounds headship in 1 Cor 11:7's "superior sex" language read through creation-sequence
and Aristotelian rule/subjection categories, without invoking Christ at all
(`research/reading-notes/strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan/household-duty-writers-overview.md`);
**Vermigli** does the same via an explicitly Aristotelian political category, "the Dominion of
Aristocratia," rule by the one who "excelleth in virtue and worthiness"
(`research/reading-notes/strand-1-reformation-continental/five-reformers-overview.md`). Both are
Reformed exegetes working from Scripture, but the *argument-form* they use for the structural claim
is one general reason could in principle run without Scripture — which is exactly Tier 1's claim,
already modeled by this project's own secured sources, not invented for this note. Cross-culturally
observable patriarchal marriage structure among peoples with no access to special revelation (the
"natural marriage among pagans still exhibits some real, if corrupted, order" the brief names) is
the sociological correlate of the same claim — **flagged UNVERIFIED as a specific empirical claim**,
since no lane has done comparative-religion research to confirm it; it functions here as a
plausibility consideration, not a secured finding.

*What Tier 1 does NOT include*: the *rightly ordered* practice of headship — self-giving,
non-tyrannical, Christ-patterned love (Eph 5:25-29) — is not claimed to be generally available.
If anything, the near-universal historical record of corrupted patriarchal marriage (polygamy,
wives-as-property, Lamech) is evidence *for* Tier 1's structural claim (the order is really there,
dimly perceived) and simultaneously evidence that fallen general revelation reliably produces the
"hard patriarchy" distortion this project names as a live error (manifesto, "What is at stake") —
general revelation gets the bare fact of asymmetric order roughly right and its rightly-ordered
content badly wrong, which is exactly what Rom 1's pattern of suppression-and-corruption predicts.

**Tier 2 — special revelation / the Christological mystery.** Claim: that marriage was designed
*from its institution* to prefigure Christ's own future, incarnate, sacrificial union with his
redeemed church is not a fact about the structure of the marital relationship itself, observable by
reflecting on it, but a fact about God's own secret redemptive intent for that structure — and facts
of that kind are knowable only by revelation that discloses them.

*Warrant.* This is the technical logic of a biblical *mystērion* — Paul's own word at Eph 5:32,
pending the exegete lane's confirmation that it carries the same sense there as at Rom 16:25, Eph
3:3-6, and Col 1:26-27: something "kept secret... but now is made manifest" (the exegete lane's
province to confirm the verbal parallel; this note only relies on the general Pauline pattern being
well known, not on a fresh reading of 5:32 itself). The same logic underwrites every case of biblical
typology this project already treats as settled by analogy: the Passover lamb's reference to Christ
(1 Cor 5:7) was not recoverable by examining lambs or the Exodus narrative in isolation; it required
retrospective, revelatory disclosure. Deut 29:29's "the secret things belong unto the LORD" states
the general principle directly: God's own hidden redemptive counsel is not the kind of fact that
observation of the created order, however careful, can reach — only its own disclosure reaches it.
Rom 5:14's naming of Adam as "the figure of him that was to come" is the closest biblical parallel
to what's being claimed for marriage: something in the *original* creation order was, by design,
already oriented toward and only fully legible in light of an antitype not yet given — Adam himself
is a type before Christ arrives to be recognized as the antitype. Marriage-as-type-of-Christ-and-
-Church (Zanchi's whole argument; Eve's creation "a type of the creation of the Church," per
`research/reading-notes/strand-1-reformation-continental/zanchi.md`) is a specific instance of this
same general pattern, not a special case invented for this topic — **this cross-reference (Rom
5:14's Adam-typology as the general form of which marriage's typology is one instance) is this
note's own systematic contribution and has not been independently checked by the exegete lane; flag
it to that lane before treating it as more than a suggestive parallel. CONTESTABLE pending that
check.**

*Why this specifically requires special revelation rather than being in-principle inferable from
creation alone*: the created *structure* (headship, one-flesh union) is, per Tier 1, a feature of
how male and female actually relate as embodied persons — an observable datum, however corruptly
apprehended. The *referent* of the type — that the structure was designed to prefigure a
not-yet-incarnate Christ's not-yet-existing, cross-purchased, Jew-and-Gentile church — is not a
feature of the relationship available to the people living inside it; it is a fact about a plan
external to the structure itself, disclosed only in and through the events and apostolic
interpretation of redemptive history. No amount of philosophical reflection on marriage as such
could have yielded Eph 5:32's content before the incarnation and the apostolic disclosure of it —
this is not a claim that reason is too weak to notice *that* marriage might signify something
transcendent (see §3 below on pagan hieros gamos motifs, which suggest fallen reason does grope
toward this), but that reason cannot land on the *true, specific referent* apart from revelation
naming it. **SOUND** as the core of the claim; the hieros gamos qualification in §3 is where it
gets genuinely complicated.

## 3. The strongest counter-consideration, taken seriously and resolved (with the unresolved edge named)

**The objection, stated as sharply as it should be**: if Tier 1 gives a complete, free-standing
account of marriage's structure — headship, order, telos — fully specifiable without reference to
Christ, and Tier 2 then adds Christological *meaning* on top, the two-tier model has the logical
shape of a ground floor (nature, complete) with an upper story (grace, additional) bolted on. On
that shape, Eph 5:32's mystery becomes a homiletically useful analogy Paul reaches for — a
teaching device for people who already understand marriage on its own terms — rather than
*constitutive* of what marriage actually, objectively is. This would run directly against the
project's own already-secured, three-strand-convergent finding: Zanchi, Palmer, and Adams
independently hold that "St. Paul is not here explaining union with Christ by marriage... but he is
illustrating marriage by union with Christ" (Palmer, confirmed quote, in
`research/reading-notes/strand-5-american-southern-presbyterian/five-figures-overview.md`) — the
direction runs from Christ to marriage, marriage does not run from itself outward to Christ. A
two-tier model that lets Tier 1 stand as metaphysically self-sufficient inverts this directional
claim and should be rejected on the project's own terms, not merely as an abstract worry.

**The resolution this note takes a position on** (per instructions, not surveyed neutrally):
the objection is answered not by collapsing Tier 1 — denying that general revelation reaches any
real structural fact — but by denying that Tier 1's content was ever *metaphysically complete in
itself*, even though it is *epistemically accessible in outline* apart from Christ. The needed move
is to distinguish the **epistemological** two-tier claim (what unaided reason, reflecting on
creation, can track) from an **ontological** two-tier claim (what marriage actually, objectively is)
— and to affirm the first while explicitly denying the second. There is no "plain natural marriage,
complete on its own terms" to which a Christological layer later gets affixed; marriage's own
nature, from Genesis 2 onward, was *already* designed with its Christological telos built in,
whether or not any given observer, era, or culture could perceive that design. Palmer's and
Zanchi's own governing image supplies exactly the right shape for this, better than "layering"
language does: Palmer calls Eph 5 "the hieroglyph over the very portals of marriage, not to be
**deciphered** until the key was furnished in the work of redemption" (confirmed quote, same
source). A hieroglyph is a real, perceivable, even describable inscription prior to the key's
arrival — someone with no access to the key can see shapes, copy them, notice patterns (Tier 1's
proper analogue) — but the inscription was placed there, by its original designer, *for the purpose
of* being read with the key, from the very start. Its meaning is not added later; only its
legibility is. On this model, Tier 1 and Tier 2 are not two separable stories but one designed
object approached at two different points in the history of its own disclosure — which is the
correct way to read WCF 7.5-6's own "one and the same [reality]... under various dispensations"
applied, by the extension defended in §1, to marriage specifically. **This is this note's
constructive position, held with real confidence: keep the epistemological two-tier distinction,
discard any ontological one.**

**Where this note thinks the question is genuinely, honestly unresolved rather than tidily closed**:
the objection's sharpest edge survives in a different form once "hieroglyph" replaces "layering."
If marriage's structure was always designed as Christ-oriented, in what sense is Tier 1's content
*actually* "general-revelation-accessible," rather than simply "special-revelation content not yet
recognized as such by its observers"? The hieroglyph analogy risks proving too much in the other
direction — if the shapes were placed there entirely *for* a key not yet given, is unaided reason
really reaching anything about marriage's *nature*, or only about its incidental, non-essential
surface features (that men and women differ, that some asymmetry of practice recurs), with the
*essential* fact about what marriage actually is (a Christ-type) permanently out of general
revelation's reach even in outline? This note does not think that fully resolves cleanly, and says
so rather than forcing it: the position taken above requires holding that a thing's *structural*
features can be really known (Tier 1) even while its *final, constitutive* meaning is withheld
(Tier 2) — which is coherent (this is exactly how ordinary typology already works: the Passover
lamb was a real, roastable, edible animal, truly known as such, prior to and apart from its
Christological referent) but it does mean Tier 1's "access" is thinner and more provisional than a
casual statement of "general revelation gives the basic order" might suggest to a reader. **Flag
this explicitly in the drafted article rather than smoothing it**: Tier 1 gives real but
structurally partial access — enough to found a natural-law case against, say, radical role-reversal
or the dissolution of marriage's differentiated shape, but not enough to found marriage's own
final, self-interpreting *account of itself*, which remains genuinely Christ-dependent in a way
that is more than additive without collapsing into "general revelation knows nothing."

*A live, unverified complication worth flagging rather than asserting*: pagan "sacred marriage"
(hieros gamos) motifs in comparative religion/mythology are sometimes read as evidence that fallen
reason does grope, unprompted, toward "marriage signifies something transcendent" even absent
special revelation — which would be a further, real instance of Rom 1's own pattern (suppressed
truth surfacing corrupted, as idolatry/fertility cult rather than as the truth about Christ and the
church). If so, this would *strengthen* rather than threaten the two-tier claim: it shows exactly
what general revelation does with a real but unnamed intuition when special revelation is absent —
but this note has not verified any specific hieros gamos scholarship and does not assert it as a
finding. **UNVERIFIED; a live lead for a systematic or historical-researcher pass, not a claim.**

## 4. Testing the framework against the project's own documented historical variation

**Honest verdict up front**: this framework has real, but narrower, explanatory power than the
brief's suggested hypothesis proposes — and using it to explain *why* the Musculus/Oecolampadius
vs. Calvin/Vermigli split exists would be **STRETCH**. That split is not, on inspection, a dispute
about general vs. special revelation, or about nature vs. Christ. It is a dispute entirely internal
to Tier 1's own territory: whether asymmetric headship is *intrinsic* to created sexual
differentiation as such (Calvin: "the order of nature implies that the woman should be the helper of
the man," a real if "gentle" pre-Fall subjection; Vermigli: a "helper" relation carrying real order
even before intensification) or whether it is a *distinct positive institution* God adds only after
and because of the Fall, with no rule-language attaching to the sexes' creation as such (Musculus's
explicit fourth "law," introduced only at Gen 3:16; Oecolampadius's "equal dignity" pre-Fall with
subordination "introduced" after). Both positions on both sides of this split are working entirely
within Scripture's own witness to a pre-redemptive-historical event (creation, then the Fall) — none
of the four figures grounds their view Christologically, in Eph 5:32's mystery, at all. **Directly
testing the brief's own hypothesis**: no, the post-Fall-only positions are not, on the evidence
already secured by the historical-researcher lane, "implicitly Christological" — Musculus's post-Fall
"law" is Gen 3:16, a matter of what happened at the Fall, not a matter of Christ's own headship over
the church disclosed in the gospel. Treating "grounded later in redemptive history" as equivalent to
"grounded Christologically" would be a category error, and this note recommends against making it —
Gen 3:16 (fall/judgment) and Eph 5:32 (Christ/church mystery) are different loci, and keeping them
distinct here is the same discipline CLAUDE.md already requires for WCF 24 vs. WLC 123-133; it
applies again, freshly, at this seam.

**The same fault line recurs, independently, three centuries later**: Strand 5's Dabney (subjection
as, in part, punishment for Eve's sin, imputed to all women since — a *harsher* fall-grounding than
Musculus, since Dabney makes the content itself punitive rather than merely first-appearing
post-Fall) vs. Palmer (headship a creation ordinance in its content, the Fall changing only its
"mode of enforcement... from voluntary harmony to positive statute," never its substance) is the
identical axis restated: is the *content* of subjection creation-original (Calvin/Vermigli/Palmer)
or fall-caused (Musculus/Oecolampadius/Dabney)? Naming this as one recurring fault line, not two
unrelated ones five centuries apart, is itself a small positive finding of this note — it suggests
the creation-vs-fall grounding question is a stable, structural fault line in Reformed exegesis of
Gen 1-3 as such, not an idiosyncratic feature of either strand. And the same verdict applies to
Dabney as to Musculus: his punitive/imputed view is a *stronger* fall-grounding, not a
Christological one — still not evidence for the brief's hypothesis.

**Where the framework does have a real, if more modest, stake in this split**: Tier 1's specific
content is only as rich as whichever side of this debate is exegetically correct — a question this
note explicitly defers to the exegete and historical-researcher lanes rather than adjudicating on
its own systematic authority (per standing rule 4). If Calvin/Vermigli/Palmer are right, Tier 1 =
sexual differentiation *and* real (if "gentle," per Calvin) headship, both creationally given and
both in principle available to general revelation. If Musculus/Oecolampadius/Dabney are right, Tier
1 narrows to sexual differentiation *and* mutual companionship only — full parity in rule at
creation — and headship itself would need to be reclassified as a **third category** this note's
original two tiers don't capture cleanly: a post-Fall, Scripturally-recorded positive institution
(Gen 3:16) that is special revelation but not yet Christological content, sitting between Tier 1 and
Tier 2. **This is a genuine limitation of the two-tier model as originally framed, honestly flagged
rather than absorbed**: a fully adequate systematic account may need to hold the Tier-1/Tier-2
distinction *provisionally*, pending the exegete lane's own resolution (or honest non-resolution) of
Gen 2-3's own internal question, rather than asserting Tier 1's content as settled. **Recommend the
Series I article state this explicitly**: the general-revelation/special-revelation-in-Christ
distinction this article develops does not, by itself, settle whether headship itself is creational
or a Fall-remedy — that is Series II's exegetical question to answer on Gen 1-3's own terms, and this
article's framework should be worded so it survives either outcome, rather than smuggling in a
answer to the creation-vs-fall question under cover of the nature-grace argument.

## 5. Further systematic resources — cited specifically, or flagged as not confidently available

- **Calvin's own "spectacles" image, Institutes 1.6.1** — WELL-ATTESTED from memory as a genuine,
  well-known Calvin passage (Scripture as spectacles correcting the "aged or bleared eye" of natural
  reason so it can rightly read what general revelation already, dimly, presents) but **not
  independently re-verified against the primary text in this pass** — flag before quoting directly.
  If confirmed, this is a strong, on-strand (Calvin is already this project's richest Strand 1
  figure) resource for exactly §3's resolution: general revelation's Tier-1 access is real but
  requires special revelation's own correction to be read rightly, which is a different and more
  precise claim than either "general revelation is blind" or "general revelation is sufficient."
- **Van Til on general/special revelation as non-separable spheres** — his recurring argument that
  general revelation is never rightly read except "through the spectacles of Scripture" (his own
  development of the Calvin image above) is directly on point for §3's resolution. **UNVERIFIED
  as to specific text/citation** — this note is confident the theme is real and Van Tillian but has
  not located or confirmed a specific passage; do not quote him without that check.
- **Bavinck, "grace restores nature"** — a genuinely well-known Bavinck theme (his general
  insistence, across *Reformed Dogmatics*, that special revelation does not compete with or replace
  general revelation but restores/completes what sin has corrupted in it) is relevant to §3's
  concern about Christology being "merely additive" — but note precisely: Bavinck's own formula is
  about **restoration from corruption**, not (like the Thomistic "grace perfects nature") an
  **elevation of nature toward a supernatural end it was already ordered to**. This note's own §3
  resolution leans closer to the Thomistic-shaped "ordered-toward-from-the-start" claim (marriage
  designed as a hieroglyph from creation) than to Bavinck's restoration-from-corruption framing —
  **these are not the same claim, and the drafted article should not cite Bavinck as if his formula
  straightforwardly supports this note's position without noting the difference.** WELL-ATTESTED as
  a general Bavinckian theme; UNVERIFIED/CONTESTABLE as a direct fit for this note's specific
  argument, and flagged as such rather than smoothed over.
- **The *analogia fidei* / *analogia Scripturae*** — already used directly in §1's refinement (WCF
  1.9) rather than merely listed here; this is the operative hermeneutical principle underwriting
  reading Gen 2 through Eph 5:32 at all, and probably belongs in the drafted article's own
  methodological opening, ahead of the WCF 7.5-6 material, since it is logically prior to it.
- **Not recommended for use, flagged rather than silently omitted**: the Eternal Functional
  Subordination (Grudem/Ware vs. Trueman/Goligher) debate over analogizing male headship to the
  Trinity's Father-Son relation is adjacent territory (also a "how much can be inferred about
  creational order from a Christological pattern" question) but the manifesto has already and
  explicitly declined to draw on this analogy (`00-foundations/manifesto.md`, "Relation to
  contemporary treatments": "This project will not adopt the Trinity-analogy argument"). This note
  respects that standing decision and does not import EFS-adjacent reasoning into the nature-grace
  argument above, even though the formal shape of the dispute (how much of a redemptive-historical
  pattern is legitimately read back into a creation ordinance) rhymes with this note's own §1 and
  §3. Worth naming as a **cautionary structural parallel**, not a resource to draw on.
