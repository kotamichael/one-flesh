## Citation-by-citation ruling

### 1. WCF 7.5-6 — text CONFIRMED; note's own STRETCH verdict CONFIRMED, not overdrawn

Independently verified text (opc.org, cross-checked):

> 7.5. This covenant was differently administered in the time of the law, and in the time of the
> gospel: under the law, it was administered by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the
> paschal lamb, and other types and ordinances delivered to the people of the Jews, all
> fore-signifying Christ to come; which were, for that time, sufficient and efficacious, through the
> operation of the Spirit, to instruct and build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by
> whom they had full remission of sins, and eternal salvation; and is called the old testament.
>
> 7.6. Under the gospel, when Christ, the substance, was exhibited, the ordinances in which this
> covenant is dispensed are the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of
> baptism and the Lord's Supper: which, though fewer in number, and administered with more
> simplicity, and less outward glory, yet, in them, it is held forth in more fulness/fullness,
> evidence and spiritual efficacy, to all nations, both Jews and Gentiles; and is called the new
> testament. There are not therefore two covenants of grace, differing in substance, but one and the
> same, under various dispensations.

**CONFIRMED** against the note's quotation — no substantive discrepancy. (One orthographic variant
worth flagging: sources split between "fulness" and "fullness" at 7.6 — see spelling note at the end
of this review.)

**Direct ruling on the note's own verdict**: I agree with the note's self-assessment, and the
chapter's language does **not** reach further than the note credits it. WCF 7.5-6's grammatical
subject across both paragraphs is explicitly "this covenant," which chapter 7's earlier paragraphs
(7.1-4, not reproduced here but confirmed present in the same chapter) establish as the covenant of
grace specifically. Marriage is not named, alluded to, or implied anywhere in 7.5-6; the "types and
ordinances" listed are all covenant-of-grace ordinances (promises, prophecies, sacrifices,
circumcision, the paschal lamb, then Word and sacraments) — marriage is conspicuously absent from a
list that, if the chapter meant to include it, had every opportunity to name it. So a sentence
asserting "WCF 7.5-6 teaches that marriage was administered typologically under the Old Testament
and more fully under the New" would misstate the chapter's actual subject. **STRETCH, correctly
flagged by the note — hold the line here.** Any drafted prose must not write "per WCF 7.5-6" as a
citation for a marriage-specific claim.

### 2. WLC 34-35 — text CONFIRMED, but "near-verbatim restatement" needs to be narrowed

Independently verified text (opc.org, cross-checked):

> Q34. How was the covenant of grace administered under the Old Testament? A. The covenant of grace
> was administered under the Old Testament, by promises, prophecies, sacrifices, circumcision, the
> passover, and other types and ordinances, which did all fore-signify Christ then to come, and were
> for that time sufficient to build up the elect in faith in the promised Messiah, by whom they then
> had full remission of sin, and eternal salvation.
>
> Q35. How is the covenant of grace administered under the New Testament? A. Under the New
> Testament, when Christ the substance was exhibited, the same covenant of grace was and still is to
> be administered in the preaching of the Word, and the administration of the sacraments of baptism
> and the Lord's supper; in which grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness/fullness,
> evidence, and efficacy, to all nations.

**Correction to the note, on a specific point of precision**: the note's own quoted fragment —
"when Christ the substance was exhibited... grace and salvation are held forth in more fulness,
evidence, and efficacy" — is **CONFIRMED accurate, word for word**. But characterizing the *whole*
Q34-35 answer as a "near-verbatim restatement" of WCF 7.5-6 overstates the match once you look past
that one fragment. Real differences: WCF 7.5 names "the paschal lamb," WLC 34 names "the passover"
(a different term, not a synonym choice repeated); WCF 7.5's "sufficient and efficacious, through
the operation of the Spirit, to instruct and build up the elect" becomes WLC 34's shorter
"sufficient to build up the elect" (drops "efficacious, through the operation of the Spirit... to
instruct"); WCF 7.6's summary sentence — "There are not therefore two covenants of grace, differing
in substance, but one and the same, under various dispensations" — has **no counterpart at all** in
WLC 35, which simply ends at "to all nations." WLC 35 also drops "spiritual" before "efficacy."

None of this threatens the note's actual argument — the load-bearing clause ("Christ the substance
exhibited," "more fulness/fullness, evidence, and efficacy") really is shared, close to verbatim,
between the two loci, and that shared clause is exactly what the note needs for its three-loci
convergence claim in §1. But the article should say the two loci **share their key formulaic
language nearly verbatim**, not that WLC 34-35 is a global "near-verbatim restatement" of WCF 7.5-6
— the latter is a global claim the actual texts don't support once you read past the one shared
clause. **CONFIRMED with a precision correction; not a STRETCH, but tighten the wording before it
goes into prose.**

### 3. WCF 8.6 — text CONFIRMED, word for word

Independently verified text (opc.org, cross-checked):

> Although the work of redemption was not actually wrought by Christ till after his incarnation, yet
> the virtue, efficacy, and benefits thereof were communicated unto the elect, in all ages
> successively from the beginning of the world, in and by those promises, types, and sacrifices,
> wherein he was revealed, and signified to be the seed of the woman which should bruise the
> serpent's head; and the Lamb slain from the beginning of the world; being yesterday and today the
> same, and forever.

**CONFIRMED**, exact match to the note's quotation, no discrepancy of any kind. This is the
strongest and cleanest of the three loci for the note's "one reality, differently disclosed" pattern
— it states the "in all ages successively from the beginning of the world" continuity claim more
plainly than 7.5-6 or 34-35 do, and it is entirely about Christ's redemptive work, not about
marriage, which the note correctly treats as another instance of the general pattern rather than a
direct statement about marriage.

### 4. WCF 1.9 — text CONFIRMED; two-job division sound, with one clarification

Independently verified text (opc.org, cross-checked):

> The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself: and therefore, when
> there is a question about the true and full sense of any Scripture (which is not manifold, but
> one), it must be searched and known by other places that speak more clearly.

**CONFIRMED**, exact match (the note's quotation omits the parenthetical "(which is not manifold,
but one)," which doesn't affect the point being made but should be restored if the passage is
quoted directly in the article — partial quotation of a Confession paragraph should either use
ellipsis or reproduce the full sentence, per this project's own citation-precision standard).

**Ruling on the two-job division** (WCF 1.9 = license to read Gen 2:18-24 through Eph 5:32; WCF
7.5-6 = timing/epistemics of why that fuller sense wasn't visible to OT readers): **sound, not
overdrawn — this is the right division and a genuine, useful distinction.** WCF 1.9 states the
Confession's own *analogia Scripturae*: an unclear text's "true and full sense" is to be searched out
by "other places that speak more clearly." Using an inspired apostle's own commentary on Gen 2:24
(Eph 5:32) to establish the "full sense" of Gen 2:18-24 is about as central a case of this principle
as exists in the whole canon — NT commentary clarifying OT text is precisely what 1.9 is for. This
is a materially stronger and more direct fit than the 7.5-6/34-35/8.6 extension: WCF 1.9 is being
applied within its own actual stated principle (Scripture interpreted by Scripture) to an instance of
exactly the kind of case it was written to cover, whereas 7.5-6 etc. are being borrowed *by analogy*
for a subject (marriage) those paragraphs never address. **Rate WCF 1.9's application here as SOUND,
even more securely than the note's own "SOUND" bucket suggests** — it isn't merely "not overdrawn,"
it's the more straightforward move of the two.

One clarification worth adding to the drafted article, not a correction: WCF 1.9 licenses treating
Eph 5:32 as authoritative commentary on Gen 2:18-24's "true and full sense," but it does not, by
itself, explain *why* that fuller sense required the passage of redemptive history to become visible
— 1.9 is a timeless hermeneutical rule, not a redemptive-historical claim. That "why" is supplied
only by the *general disclosure pattern* evidenced at 7.5-6/34-35/8.6 (type now, substance later),
applied by extension — which keeps the note's own distinction (license vs. timing/epistemics) intact
but means the "timing" half of the pairing carries the same extension-by-pattern status flagged as
CONTESTABLE in §1, not a second SOUND confessional citation. The article should be precise that only
one half of this pairing (1.9) is a direct citation; the other half (7.5-6 etc.) is, again, pattern
extension.

## The category-error question — direct ruling

**Does using WCF 7.5-6 / WLC 34-35 / WCF 8.6 this way risk the same kind of error CLAUDE.md already
guards against for WCF 24 vs. WLC 123-133? No — this is a legitimate extension-by-pattern, not a
conflation of the WCF-24/WLC-123-133 kind, provided one specific labeling discipline is enforced as
a hard requirement rather than a suggestion.**

Here is the distinction, stated directly. The WCF 24 / WLC 123-133 error this project already
guards against is a **substitution error**: citing a locus as if it were dispositive on a subject
that a *different* locus actually addresses (e.g., reaching for WCF 24 to settle a headship question
that WCF 24 never raises, when WLC 123-133 — a different chapter, on a different subject, entirely
un-consulted — is where the Standards actually speak to it). The reader is misled into thinking the
cited text is on-topic when it isn't, and meanwhile the locus that *is* on-topic goes unconsulted.

What the nature-grace note is doing is different in kind. It is not claiming WCF 7.5-6 (or 34-35, or
8.6) is dispositive, or even on-topic, for marriage — the note says the opposite, explicitly and
repeatedly ("STRETCH... don't let this into final prose," "not a citation of on-topic content").
It is instead taking a **general hermeneutical/redemptive-historical principle** that the Standards
happen to state clearly in the course of addressing a different, named subject (the covenant of
grace's mode of administration), and applying that *principle* — not that subject's content — to a
different case (marriage's typological disclosure) that the Standards never address at all, under
any locus. There is no "correct locus" being bypassed here the way WLC 123-133 is bypassed when a
draft reaches for WCF 24 instead — no chapter of the Standards addresses marriage's typological
content, full stop, so there is no substitution happening, only an argument that a pattern secured
elsewhere in the Standards is general enough in form to license extending it to a topic those same
Standards are silent on.

That makes this a **claim-(c) move under this lane's own standing taxonomy** — "a further
development beyond anything the Standards address... fine to include, but must never be presented
as (a)" — not a claim-(a) citation, and definitely not a conflation of two on-topic loci. The
distinction holds *as long as*, and only as long as, the drafted article maintains the note's own
discipline with total consistency: every sentence that draws on this pattern must be worded so a
reader cannot mistake it for "the Confession states this about marriage." Concretely, that means:
banning any footnote or parenthetical of the form "(WCF 7.5-6)" attached directly to a marriage-typology
claim without an explicit intervening clause — something like "by extension of the pattern the
Confession states about the covenant of grace at WCF 7.5-6, which does not itself address marriage."
The moment that discipline slips — the moment a footnote reads as if 7.5-6 is being cited *for*
marriage rather than *analogized to* marriage — the error becomes real and becomes exactly the kind
of category error this project's house rules exist to prevent. **Ruling: the move is legitimate as
designed; the risk is entirely in execution, not in the underlying logic, and this gate should be
treated as conditional on that labeling discipline surviving into the actual article draft, not as a
one-time pass.**

## Spelling/orthography note (house-rule discipline, not a substantive finding)

Independent sources split between "fulness" (single *l*, older orthography) and "fullness" (double
*l*, modernized) at WCF 7.6 and WLC 35. This project's own house rule requires preserving original
spelling in direct quotation of pre-19th-century sources. Neither of my independently fetched sources
is a facsimile or critical edition (Van Dixhoorn) — both are modern web transcriptions of uncertain
typesetting fidelity — so I cannot certify which spelling matches the actual 1646/1647 print run
without consulting a facsimile or the Van Dixhoorn critical edition. **UNVERIFIED as to which
spelling is period-original; flag for whoever finalizes direct quotation in the article, and default
to whichever spelling the project settles on as its canonical WCF source text for consistency across
the whole project rather than deciding it locally in this one article.**
