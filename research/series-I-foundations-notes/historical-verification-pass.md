## 1. Does Zanchi himself make the progressive-disclosure (OT-shadow/NT-substance) move about marriage specifically?

**Short answer: not confirmed either way. One genuine, primary-source negative finding secured; the
place most likely to contain a positive answer remains unread and is flagged, not guessed at.**

### 1a. *De Operibus Dei*, Tomus III, Caput II "De Matrimonio" (cols ~820-832) — now read directly, and it is NOT where this argument lives

This closes an open question flagged in the existing Zanchi file
(`research/reading-notes/strand-1-reformation-continental/zanchi.md`, open question 2) as
inaccessible due to unusable OCR. A different, better-OCR'd scan of the same 1619-printing volume
was located and is usable: Internet Archive item `bub_gb_MGtNtpCmVVgC` ("OPERVM THEOLOGICORVM D.
HIERONYMI ZANCHII, Tomus Tertius, DE OPERIBVS DEI INTRA SPATIVM SEX DIERVM CREATIS"), full text at
`https://ia801006.us.archive.org/16/items/bub_gb_MGtNtpCmVVgC/bub_gb_MGtNtpCmVVgC_djvu.txt`. This
is a different, legible OCR pass of the volume the existing file's open question 2 already
correctly identified by column range but could not read (the previously-tried archive.org item
`bub_gb_yqST190e63EC` is confirmed, independently, to be Tomus Octavus — the correspondence
volume — not Tomus III; that identification in the existing Zanchi file stands).

**CONFIRMED, direct full-text search of the chapter** (running from "CAP. II. DE MATRIMONIO" to
the start of "CAPVT III... DE DIVORTIIS," roughly 950 lines of dense Latin OCR): the chapter
contains **zero occurrences** of "Christus," "ecclesia," "figura," "typus," "umbra" (shadow), or
"mysterium"/"myfterium." Its actual content, confirmed by reading through it, is a scholastic
*quaestiones*/canon-law-style treatment of marriage as a natural and legal institution: the
definition of matrimony, whether/when it may lawfully be contracted, impediments, betrothal
(*sponsalia*) and consent, marriage ages, marriage between a believer and an unbeliever
(*matrimonium inter fidelem et infidelem*), and — immediately following, in the next chapter — a
dedicated treatment of divorce. **This is a genuinely different genre and purpose from *De
Spirituali Connubio*, and it does not engage the Christ/church typology at all, let alone make a
timing/epistemics claim about it.** This is a real, useful negative finding: it rules out the one
part of Zanchi's corpus this project could point to with a precise citation (cols 820-832) as *not*
the place to look for the progressive-disclosure argument, closing that specific lead rather than
leaving it open indefinitely.

### 1b. *De Spirituali Connubio* itself — where the argument, if it exists, would have to live — remains substantially unread by me this pass

I obtained and read the same 44-page publisher's sample already flagged as available in the
existing Zanchi file (`research/reading-notes/strand-1-reformation-continental/zanchi.md`, "Wife's
side" section) — this time extracting its actual text via `pdfplumber` rather than treating it as
opaque binary. The sample covers **O'Banion's translator introduction and the dedicatory epistle
only** — it does not reach Chapter 1 ("The Creation of Eve and Her Marriage to Adam") in full, and
does not reach Chapter 4 ("The Final Causes of the Spiritual Union") at all, which is the chapter
title most likely, on its face, to contain a purpose/timing argument.

Within what I *could* read, one passage from O'Banion's introduction is worth naming and then
setting aside carefully, because it is easy to mistake for an answer to this question and it is
not: "Sometimes this commitment led him to discern scriptural allegories, shadows, and mysteries
that pointed to Christ but that would not have been apparent to Calvin" (O'Banion, "Introduction,"
xxxv). **This is a contrast between two different sixteenth-century interpreters' exegetical
methods (Zanchi's more allegorical, rule-of-faith-driven reading vs. Calvin's grammatical-historical
one), not a redemptive-historical claim about OT believers' access versus NT believers' access.**
Citing this sentence as evidence Zanchi makes the timing/epistemics move would be a real
misreading of what it says — flagging this explicitly so a later pass doesn't repeat the mistake.

I found no other passage in the accessible material (front matter, dedicatory epistle, or the
already-existing raw reading notes in
`research/christian-living-in-the-home-study-plan/week-01-christ-centered-home.md`, which cover
Ch. 1's own outline in some detail — slumber as a type of Christ's death, the rib as type of
Christ's deity, the church built from Christ's side as Eve was built from Adam's rib, etc.) that
states or implies an explicit claim about OT patriarchs having only partial or shadowed access to
this typology compared to fuller post-incarnation access. What is visible in Ch. 1's outline is
Zanchi *asserting* the correspondences point-by-point, in the timeless present ("the church from
Christ's side," "divinity given," etc.) — not framing them as content that was hidden from Adam,
Abraham, or Moses and only later disclosed. But this is a reading of an outline of notes taken from
a chapter, not the chapter's own full text, and Chapters 3-4 (which more directly concern the
"doctrine of spiritual marriage" and "final causes") are entirely unread by anyone on this project
to date.

I also checked available secondary sources — a review at Modern Reformation, a Reformed Forum
podcast episode description with O'Banion — neither discusses this specific question one way or
the other. Andrew Leslie's 2023 *Journal of Reformed Theology* article on Zanchi's *De Operibus
Dei* (already flagged in the existing Zanchi file) discusses a related but distinct claim —
Christ's own mediatorial agency *in the original creation* of Adam and Eve (a prelapsarian
Christology-of-creation argument) — which is not the same claim as "OT believers had only shadowed
access to this truth." I could not obtain the article's full text (Brill blocked access) to check
whether it also touches the timing/epistemics question; the abstract alone does not settle it.

**Verdict, stated as the task requires: genuinely UNVERIFIED, not merely unconfirmed by omission.**
Zanchi's general typological claim (marriage designed as a type of Christ and the church) is
already CONFIRMED by this project's existing research. The narrower, specific claim this task asked
about — that Zanchi *also* argues OT believers had comparatively shadowed/partial access to that
typology, as against fuller NT access — is **not confirmed, and not disconfirmed**: the one place I
could check with precision (the *De Operibus Dei* "De Matrimonio" chapter) doesn't contain the
typology at all, and the place where it would have to be checked (*De Spirituali Connubio*
Chapters 1, 3, and especially 4) remains substantially unread. Recommend against writing "Zanchi
argues marriage was known only in shadow before Christ" into any draft on the strength of what this
project currently has — that would smooth an unlocated claim into confident prose, exactly what
house rule warns against. The project owner's physical copy (already purchased, per the existing
Zanchi file's open-questions list) is the next real step, not another round of remote-access
attempts.

## 2. Calvin's *Commentary on Ephesians*, 5:31-32 — full finding

**Filed directly into**
`research/christian-living-in-the-home-study-plan/week-01-christ-centered-home.md`'s previously-empty
Calvin section — see that file for the full reading notes with exact quotations. Summary here:

**CONFIRMED** (fetched and searched the raw CCEL HTML directly — `ccel.org/ccel/calvin/calcom41.iv.vi.vi.html`,
the Calvin Translation Society/Pringle translation — not relying on an AI-summarized pass alone):

- **On directionality**: Calvin states plainly that "the spiritual union between Christ and his
  church is so treated as to illustrate the common law of marriage, to which the quotation from
  Moses relates" — Christ's union illustrating/explaining marriage, not the reverse. This is a
  fourth independent voice (alongside Palmer, Zanchi, and Adams, per this project's existing
  three-strand convergence finding) converging on the same directional claim, and has been added to
  the week-01 file's synthesis section as such.
- **On the "mystery" itself, quoted in full**: "This is a great mystery; by which he means, that no
  language can explain fully what it implies. It is to no purpose that men fret themselves to
  comprehend, by the judgment of the flesh, the manner and character of this union... For my own
  part, I am overwhelmed by the depth of this mystery, and am not ashamed to join Paul in
  acknowledging at once my ignorance and my admiration." He closes by using the verse polemically
  against Rome's sacramental reading of *mysterium*/*sacramentum* (marriage as one of seven
  sacraments).
- **On timing/progressive disclosure — checked directly and confirmed absent.** A full-text search
  of Calvin's entire comment on Eph 5:22-33 for disclosure/timing language ("hidden," "shadow,"
  "foreshadow," "before Christ," "not yet," "in old time," "dispensation," and near-synonyms)
  returns **zero hits**. Calvin does not treat "great mystery" the way Paul's dominant usage
  elsewhere works (Rom 16:25, Eph 3:4-9, Col 1:26-27 — "hidden in past ages, now disclosed," per the
  exegete pass's own survey). For Calvin, the phrase marks the union's *permanent, intrinsic
  incomprehensibility* — a claim about the thing's *nature*, not about *when in redemptive history*
  it became knowable. In the exegete pass's own terms, this is a clean instance of "reading (B)"
  (the weaker/analogical reading of Eph 5:32's mystery), not "reading (A)" (the strong/typological,
  hidden-then-disclosed reading Zanchi and Palmer independently take).

**Why this matters for Series I, stated plainly**: the project's own working hypothesis (and its
best-secured historical support, Zanchi and Palmer) leans on reading (A). This pass confirms that
Calvin — the single most prominent Strand 1 voice — does not supply reading (A) at this text; he
supplies reading (B). This is not fatal to the project's argument (Zanchi and Palmer's reading (A)
remains available and, per the exegete pass, textually well-supported), but the drafted article
should not imply Calvin agrees with Zanchi/Palmer on *how* the mystery-disclosure mechanism works,
even though he agrees with them on *directionality*. Keep the two claims (directionality vs.
timing/disclosure) visibly separate, per the same discipline the exegete and systematic passes
already applied elsewhere.

## 3. The two flagged-UNVERIFIED quotes from the systematic-theologian pass

### 3a. Calvin, *Institutes* 1.6.1, the "spectacles"/glasses image — CONFIRMED

Verified directly against the CCEL Beveridge-translation text (cross-checked against an independent
web citation of the same passage; both converge exactly):

> "For as the aged, or those whose sight is defective, when any book, however fair, is set before
> them, though they perceive that there is something written, are scarcely able to make out two
> consecutive words, but, when aided by glasses, begin to read distinctly, so Scripture, gathering
> together the impressions of Deity, which, till then, lay confused in our minds, dissipates the
> darkness, and shows us the true God clearly." (*Institutes* 1.6.1, Beveridge translation)

The chapter itself is titled (Beveridge) "The Need of Scripture, as a Guide and Teacher, in coming
to God as a Creator," and opens: "Therefore, though the effulgence which is presented to every eye,
both in the heavens and on the earth, leaves the ingratitude of man without excuse, since God, in
order to bring the whole human race under the same condemnation, holds forth to all, without
exception, a mirror of his Deity in his works, another and better help must be given to guide us
properly to God as a Creator." **CONFIRMED, both sentences, at the location the systematic pass
cited from memory.** Note for house style: this is the common modern-English Beveridge rendering,
not old-spelling — the original English-language Institutes translations (Norton, 1561) would need
separate sourcing if period English is wanted per house rule 7.

### 3b. A citable Van Til passage on general revelation being rightly read only "through the spectacles of Scripture" — located, with an important precision about whose words are whose

**CONFIRMED as a real, citable, located passage — but the wording needs to be reported precisely,
because it is Van Til's own sentence characterizing Bavinck's return to Calvin's position, not an
isolated free-floating Van Til quote, and Van Til goes on to make the same substantive claim
independently and repeatedly in his own voice elsewhere in the same book.**

Source: Cornelius Van Til, *An Introduction to Systematic Theology: Prolegomena and the Doctrines
of Revelation, Scripture, and God*, 2nd ed., ed. William Edgar (Phillipsburg, NJ: P&R Publishing,
2007). Full text obtained and searched directly via Internet Archive
(`an-introduction-to-systematic-theology-prolegomena-and-the-cornelius-van-til`, OCR'd djvu text).
Page numbers are not recoverable from this OCR pass (no pagination markers survived digitization) —
cite by chapter/section until a physical or paginated-PDF copy is checked.

**Passage 1 — Chapter 5, "Christian Epistemology (the Positions of Herman Bavinck and Valentine
Hepp)," section A ("The Position of Herman Bavinck")**, Van Til's own sentence: "[Bavinck] wants to
return to the position of Calvin for whom Scripture was the eyeglass through which the Christian
should read the book of nature." (Note: "eyeglass," singular, not "spectacles" — the systematic
pass's memory of the specific word was slightly off; the image is the same one.)

**Passage 2 — same chapter, later**, Van Til quoting Bavinck directly and then explicitly adopting
the claim as his own: "'Even the Christian would not be able to read nature aright if it were not
for the light of Scripture.' If we follow this lead of Bavinck we cannot go far wrong... The
Christian knows that he would interpret nature wrongly, due to the sin that is within him, unless
he be enlightened by Scripture and guided by the Holy Spirit." **This last sentence is Van Til's
own constructive statement, not a quotation of anyone else** — the clearest single sentence in this
book expressing exactly the theme the systematic pass wanted sourced.

**Passage 3 — Chapter 10, "Special Revelation," section A ("The Necessity of Special Revelation")**
— Van Til quotes the opening of *Institutes* 1.6.1 directly (the same passage confirmed in §3a
above, word-for-word matching the Beveridge translation, confirming which English edition Van Til
used), under Calvin's own chapter title ("The Need of Scripture, as a Guide and Teacher, in coming
to God as a Creator"), and then adds his own gloss, in his own voice: "No one, on the basis of
present general revelation alone, actually knows God aright as the Creator... He therefore never
reads the 'book of nature' aright even with respect to 'natural' things." This is Van Til's own
argument, built directly on and citing *Institutes* 1.6.1, making the identical claim requested —
again without using the specific word "spectacles," but unmistakably the same image and argument
Calvin supplies it for.

**Verdict**: the *theme* is real, Van Til-affirmed, and now specifically citable at three located
points in one identified book — an improvement on the prior UNVERIFIED status. The one caveat
worth stating for house precision: the literal word "spectacles" does not appear in this particular
book (Van Til's own word, when he uses one, is "eyeglass"); if the drafted article wants to quote
Van Til using the word "spectacles" specifically, that would require checking a different Van Til
title (*Common Grace and the Gospel* and the *Introduction to Systematic Theology*'s earlier,
differently-edited printings are the next places to check) — recommend either using "eyeglass"
in any direct Van Til quotation, or attributing "spectacles" to Calvin and Bavinck's formulation of
Calvin (both well-attested) while citing Van Til only for the substantive claim, not the specific
word.

### 4b. Two genuine primary-text fragments recovered this pass — neither answers the question, both
### worth keeping on record

**(i) A period-English quotation from the 1592/1594 translation itself**, recovered via a secondary
blog (Eric Parker, "Zanchi: The Logic of Union with Christ," *Epistolê*, July 28, 2016) that quotes at
length from "An excellent and learned treatise, of the spirituall mariage betvveene Christ and the
church, and every faithfull man" (old spelling preserved as quoted by Parker). **Flag on the date**:
Parker's post dates this translation to 1594, while every other bibliographic source checked this
pass and in the prior pass (O'Banion's own 2005 scholarly article, the O'Banion-family "Zanchi
Bibliography" page, PRDL) gives **1592**, Cambridge, John Legat, as the English printing date — this
discrepancy is unresolved and flagged rather than silently harmonized; it may be a simple error in
Parker's post, or there may be a second printing not yet identified.

The passage itself is **not on this task's question**. It concerns the logical *order of cognition*
by which a believer comes to know and be united to Christ — flesh before deity, because "the will
followeth knowledge... we do first and sooner apprehend & know Christ propounded in the word of God
as he is man, then as he is God" — argued polemically against both "the Zwinglians" (who Zanchi says
hold the faithful united only to Christ's divinity) and "the Lutherans" (whose view of Christ's body
as invisible/unlike-ours Zanchi rejects as incapable of nourishing us), and grounded in the logic of
the sacraments ("this whole doctrine is very lively to be seene in the Sacraments, as it were in most
cleere looking glasses"). This is a real, quotable, period-English primary-source find for the
project's files generally — and it is a concrete example of Zanchi arguing from a stated interlocutor
(Zwinglians and Lutherans both named, per house rule on surfacing what a source argues against) — but
it is a different topic (epistemic order of union with Christ's two natures) from this task's
question (OT-vs-NT disclosure of marriage's typological meaning), and should not be cited as bearing
on the latter.

**(ii) A French-translation excerpt of Ch. 2 ("The Doctrine of Carnal Marriage"), not Ch. 4**,
recovered via a French-language post (Josué Isaac, "Le mariage analysé selon les quatre causes
aristotéliciennes — Zanchi," *Par la foi*, March 18, 2022), which translates and lightly modernizes
pp. 36–41 of Simon Goulart's 1594 French translation (*Excellent traité, du mariage spirituel entre
Jésus Christ et son Eglise*, Geneva). This is Zanchi's own application of the four Aristotelian
causes (material, efficient, formal, final) to **carnal** marriage specifically — matching Ch. 2's
title and location in O'Banion's table of contents, not Ch. 4. Within its "final causes" section,
Zanchi names three ends of carnal marriage in his own voice (companionship/governance — citing Gen.
2:18 and Prov. 31; procreation as "the nursery of the Church" — citing Gen. 1:28 and Matt. 16:18; and,
after the fall, a remedy against illicit unions — citing 1 Cor. 7), and then adds, distancing this
fourth point from his own core structure: **"Certains ajoutent une quatrième fin, à savoir que le
mariage charnel devait être le mystère et sacrement du mariage spirituel"** ("Some add a fourth end,
namely that carnal marriage was to be the mystery and sacrament of spiritual marriage," §4.10,
Goulart trans., modernized by the blog's author — this is a French-to-French modernization of a 1594
translation of Zanchi's Latin, so it is three steps removed from Zanchi's own words and should be
cited with that chain made explicit, not as if it were Zanchi's Latin directly).

This is a genuinely interesting texture point for the project's existing Ch. 2 notes (already partly
documented in `zanchi.md` via the "crosses and troubles"/"joint property" quote from the same

### 5a. The Chapter 4/5 discrepancy — resolved, with a genuine nuance the OCR search alone missed

**Verdict: there IS a real structural break in the 1591 print corresponding to O'Banion's Chapter 5,
but it is not headed "CAPUT QUINTUM."** A plain keyword search for "caput" (the Latin word for
"chapter") across the whole file correctly returns zero hits for a fifth chapter heading — but that
search alone is misleading, because chapters 1-3 and chapters 4-5 use two visibly different heading
conventions in this particular printing, confirmed by looking at the actual page images:

- **Ch. 1-3** are headed with the full formula "**CAPVT PRIMVM.**" / "**CAPVT SECVNDVM.**" /
  "**TERTIVM CAPVT**" (word order reversed on the third, oddly, but still using the word *caput*),
  each followed by an italic one- or two-sentence argument-summary before the numbered theses begin.
  Confirmed by direct visual inspection of the PDF page image for p. 3 (CAPVT PRIMVM).
- **What corresponds to Ch. 4** ("Caussae finales spiritualis coniugii," p. 101 in the original
  pagination, PDF page 112) is headed only with the bare numeral "**IIII.**" (rendered by the OCR as
  the garbled "III I.") directly above the title "CAVSSAE FINALES SPIRITVALIS CONIVGII" — no word
  "CAPUT" appears on the page at all. Confirmed by rendering and viewing the actual page image, not
  just the OCR text.
- **What corresponds to O'Banion's Ch. 5** ("The Duties of the Husband and of the Wife," p. 107 in
  the original pagination, PDF page 118) is headed with only the bare numeral "**V.**" — no title, no
  word "CAPUT," nothing else on the line — followed immediately by the sentence "**SEquitur quinto
  loco de officiis viri & vxoris, quibus tum matrimonium ipsum integrum illibatumque conseruatur, tum
  coniuges feliciter inter se viuunt**" ("There follows, in the fifth place, [a treatment] concerning
  the duties of the husband and the wife, by which both the marriage itself is kept whole and
  unblemished, and the spouses live happily with one another"). Confirmed by rendering and viewing
  this page image directly as well.

So: **this is a real division in the original 1591 print, not a modern editorial split of one long
chapter for readability** — O'Banion's Chapter 5 boundary lines up exactly with a real break in the
Latin, marked by the bare numeral "V." immediately after the final-causes material closes ("hæc de
caussis finalibus" — "thus concerning the final causes"). The likely explanation for why chapters 4-5
drop the word "CAPUT" while 1-3 keep it: this looks like a printer's-convention shift partway through
the book (chapters 1-3 use the fuller display-heading style; 4 and 5, and everything after, are set
with a plainer bare-numeral heading) rather than any textual corruption or an artifact of this
particular scan — both bare-numeral headings ("IIII." and "V.") appear in exactly the same visual
position and typographic weight as the "CAPVT..." headings before them, just without the word itself.
**Report this plainly rather than picking a side with more confidence than the evidence supports**:
it could equally be read as (a) Zanchi/the printer simply treating "IIII" and "V" as self-evident
continuations needing no fresh "CAPUT" label once the numbering was established, or (b) the whole
back portion of the book (final causes → duties → what follows, see below) being conceived by the
printer as one continuous fourth "part" with internal numbered sections, which O'Banion's modern
edition then re-divides into separate "chapters" for readability *at the "V." boundary specifically*
because that boundary already existed in the original, even without a "CAPUT" label. Either way, the
substantive point holds: **the officiis-viri-et-uxoris material is not "folded into Chapter 4" as the
prior pass's negative keyword search suggested — it has its own clearly marked division**, and
O'Banion's chapter-5 title is a fair description of a real break, not an invention.

**One further structural finding, not previously on record**: after the "V." officiis section closes
(~p. 114 of the original), the text continues, still without any "CAPUT" heading, into a titled
section "**CONSEQUENTIA CONNVBIUM SPIRITVALE**" ("The Consequences of the Spiritual Marriage" —
what the union brings/yields), using its own internal "THESIS I, II..." numbering, and running for
roughly 20 more original pages to "FINIS" (p. 133). **This substantial final portion of the treatise
is not captured anywhere in the existing `zanchi.md` table of contents** (which lists only Ch. 1-5
per O'Banion's front matter) — it covers the goods/blessings that follow from the spiritual marriage
(distinguishing goods pertaining to the age to come vs. the present age, and within the present age,
goods pertaining to the body/temporal life vs. the spiritual life). Flagging this as a genuinely new
finding about the book's full extent, not previously on record, though outside this task's specific
question — a future pass on "what does Zanchi say the union yields/gives" should start here.

### 5b. Wife's-side and husband's-side duties in the "V." (officiis) section — now CONFIRMED, read
### directly, applying the project's own symmetry rule

This was previously the single largest documented gap in `zanchi.md` (open question 1, "Wife's side"
section: "I could not access its text"). It is now read in full. Following Zanchi's own structure
(his own numbered theses, not an imposed grid):

**Common to both** (thesis II): "*mutuam fidem, mutuumque amorem*" — "mutual faithfulness, and mutual
love."

**Proper to the husband** (thesis III): "*ut tanquam caput sapienter imperet, iustè dominetur, vxorem
regat, foueat, nutriat, tueatur, seruet tanquam propriam carnem, multaq; ei, tanquam infirmiori
vasculo, condonet*" — "that as head he should command wisely, rule justly, govern his wife, cherish,
nourish, protect, and keep her as his own flesh, and forgive/overlook much in her, as in a weaker
vessel."

**Proper to the wife** (thesis IV): "*ut virum tanquam suum caput, dominum, & seruatorem metuat,
reuereatur, obseruet, subiecta ei maneat, ab eo eiusq; ore pendeat, sinat se regi, ei non responset,
sed simpliciter obediat in domino, modesta, lenis & quieta*" — "that she should fear her husband as
her head, lord, and protector, revere him, attend to him, remain subject to him, hang on him and his
word, allow herself to be ruled, not answer back, but simply obey in the Lord, being modest, gentle,
and quiet" — citing Eph. 5, 1 Tim. 2, and 1 Pet. 3 explicitly as his warrant (thesis IV names all
three texts together).

The chapter then spends theses V-XXI describing how Christ perfectly fulfills the husband's office
(the positive pattern, not directed at the wife), before theses XXII-XXXII turn to explaining the
Church's duty toward Christ — and this material is built explicitly on wife-specific texts and
categories, even though it is phrased in terms of "the Church," not "the wife" as such. Reporting
this distinction plainly rather than collapsing it: theses III-IV above are Zanchi's direct,
non-allegorical statement of what is proper to husband and wife respectively; theses XXII-XXXII are
the Church-application, built on the same wife-pattern. Within that application, thesis XXVII cites
1 Pet. 3 directly ("*Sicut Sara obediuit Abrahæ, dominum eum vocans*" — "as Sarah obeyed Abraham,
calling him lord") to explain wherein the wife's subjection chiefly consists (obedience and
reverence); thesis XXVIII names the positive virtue as "*humilitas animi*" ("humility of mind"); and
— **this is the wife's-side warning content the project's rule 5a asks every pass to check for, now
CONFIRMED rather than absent** — thesis XXVIII states this humility is what keeps "*bona vxor*" ("a
good wife") from "*responsat[ing] viro*" (answering back to her husband) or desiring "*in illum...
dominari, aut illi imperare*" ("to dominate over him, or to command him"), and thesis XXIX grounds
this directly in 1 Tim. 2:11-12, quoted: "*Mulier cum silentio discat, in omni subiectione: Mulieri
enim docere non permitto, neque autoritatem vsurpare in viros, sed esse in silentio*" — "Let the
woman learn in silence, with all subjection; for I permit not a woman to teach, nor to usurp authority
over the man, but to be in silence." **Zanchi thus explicitly names the temptation to usurp authority
and to talk back/refuse silence as the characteristic fault against which a wife's humility stands** —
squarely the kind of wife-specific warning content rule 5a asks for, now directly sourced rather than
inferred.

**Honest asymmetry to flag, not smoothed over**: within this same stretch of text, **no parallel
explicit warning against the husband's own characteristic sins (tyranny, harshness, abdication) is
stated** — thesis III's "*multaq; ei... condonet*" (let him forgive/overlook much in her) is a duty of
forbearance, not a warning against a husband's own vice, and theses V-XXI (Christ's perfect fulfillment
of the husband's office) are wholly positive/exemplary, not cautionary. This may simply be because the
chapter, at least in the material read this pass, reaches the husband's proper duties (III) and then
moves immediately into how Christ exemplifies them perfectly (V-XXI), without pausing — the way it
does for the wife (XXVIII-XXIX) — to name the husband's corresponding besetting fault. Report this as
what the text does and does not say, not as evidence Zanchi thought husbands need no such warning;
the material simply doesn't reach that point in this chapter, at least not in what's covered here.
Recommend this be flagged as a genuine open question for whoever reads the remaining ~20 pages of
"Consequentia connubii spiritualis" (see 5a above) or any other Zanchi text, rather than assumed either
way.

### 5c. The core question: Zanchi's direct commentary on Eph 5:32 (*Commentarius in Ephesios*,
### "EXPLICATIO CAP. V vs. 21-33," on v. 32 specifically) — read and translated in full

**CONFIRMED, direct quotation, full context read** (Latin preserved alongside translation, per house
rule 7 — this is a post-Reformation printed edition, and the translation below is mine, checked
against the full surrounding argument, not lifted from a secondary source):

> "*V. 32. Mysterium hoc magnum est. Ego verò dico de Christo & de Ecclesia.*"
> "Verse 32. This is a great mystery. But I speak concerning Christ and the Church."

Zanchi first explains Paul's own move (quoting Gen. 2 on Adam/Eve, then applying it to Christ/Church):
things like Eve's being drawn from Adam and Adam's "a man shall leave father and mother" statement
"*considerari haec possunt bifariam*" — "can be considered in two ways." First, "*quatenus sunt res
gestae & ad connubium carnale pertinent. Hoc modo non voco has res mysterium*" — "insofar as they are
historical events pertaining to carnal marriage — in this way I do not call these things a mystery."
Second, "*quatenus illud connubium Adae & Evae fuit typus connubii Christi cum Ecclesia. Hac ratione
mysterium magnum*" — "insofar as that marriage of Adam and Eve was a *type* of the marriage of Christ
with the Church. In this respect, a great mystery."

Then his technical definition of the word itself:

> "*Mysterium vox Graeca est, ut omnibus liquet, à verbo μύω, quod est abscondo, occulto: significat
> rem occultam & secretam. Quidam volunt ab Hebraeo deduci a athar, quod est celare, latere,
> abscondere... Significat rem per se arcanam non simpliciter sed religiosam, quae ab humana ratione
> abscondita est & non potest percipi sine relatione divina & Spiritus Sancti lumine.*"
>
> "'Mystery' is a Greek word, as is clear to all, from the verb μύω, which means 'I hide, I conceal':
> it signifies a hidden and secret thing. Some wish it derived from the Hebrew *athar* [סתר], meaning
> to conceal, to lie hidden, to hide away... It signifies a thing that is in itself secret — not merely
> [any secret], but a *religious* one — which **is hidden from human reason and cannot be perceived
> without divine relation/revelation and the light of the Holy Spirit**."

And applying this to marriage specifically:

> "*Talis autem res fuit eductio Evae ex latere Adae dormientis. Item conjunctio Evae cum Adamo in
> matrimonium. Res in se fuit visibilis & sub sensum cadens, sed aliam occultam repraesentabat
> eductionem & creationem Ecclesiae ex latere Christi in cruce mortui, & unionem Ecclesiae cum
> Christo.*"
>
> "Now such a thing was the drawing-out of Eve from the side of the sleeping Adam; likewise the
> joining of Eve to Adam in marriage. The thing was in itself visible and fell under the senses, but
> it represented another, hidden thing: the drawing-out and creation of the Church from the side of
> Christ dead on the cross, and the union of the Church with Christ."

He then turns polemical, against Rome — confirming this project's house rule on naming the
interlocutor: "*non ita malè vertit vetus interpres Sacramentum: quamquam ea voce postea abusi
Pontificii statuerunt Matrimonium inter sacramenta*" — "the old translator [the Vulgate/Jerome] did
not render it too badly as 'sacramentum' — although the Papists (*Pontificii*) later abused that word
to establish Matrimony among the sacraments." He closes by saying it would have been better simply to
keep the word "mystery," and that Paul himself refutes "the ignorance of these men" (Rome) because
Paul "expressly says that he is not speaking about the marriage between a man and a woman... but about
Christ and the Church, that is, about the spiritual marriage between Christ and the Church."

**Critical assessment, exactly as the task asked, stated as plainly as the text allows**: this is a
**general, standing epistemological claim about the category "mystery" as such** — that a
mystery (of the religious kind) is, by definition, inaccessible to unaided human reason at any point
and requires divine revelation and the Spirit's illumination to be perceived. Zanchi is doing word
study here (Greek and conjectured Hebrew etymology), not redemptive-historical periodization. He does
**not** say, anywhere in this passage, that this mystery was hidden from OT believers and is now known
with greater fulness by NT believers — there is no "*ante Christum*"/"*post Christum*" language, no
"in former ages," nothing about Adam, Abraham, Moses, or the patriarchs' access to it, at all. The
"occultam" ("hidden") in "*aliam occultam repraesentabat*" describes the relationship between a
visible sign and its hidden spiritual referent (a general type/antitype, sign/signified structure —
exactly parallel to how he defines "sacrament" one sentence later: "a visible sign of an invisible
thing or grace"), not a claim about *when in history* that referent became knowable. **This is
CONFIRMED, direct-text, strong primary-source support for the general claim that marriage's
Christological meaning is a real μυστήριον in Zanchi's own technical sense — but it does NOT supply
the more specific OT-shadow/NT-fuller-disclosure timing claim.** State this distinction with exactly
this precision in any draft; collapsing "hidden from reason, revealed by the Spirit" into "hidden from
OT believers, revealed to NT believers" would misrepresent what this passage actually argues.

### 5d. The Eph 3 passage (line ~14443 in the extracted commentary text) — read in full context, and
### a further, important piece of evidence about what Zanchi's toolkit *does* contain

Read the fuller surrounding context (not just the single sentence flagged), including a numbered list
a few lines earlier (Zanchi's own six-point characterization of "the doctrine of the Gospel" that
Paul preached in this chapter) that turns out to matter for the honest assessment:

> "*[Doctrina] licèt non innotuerit superioribus temporibus, sed nunc primùm sit revelata: fuit tamen
> jam inde ab aeterno in Deo secreta, suo tempore revelanda. Et hoc vers. 3. Cùm eam vocat mysterium,
> ante non revelatum erat igitur, sed ignotum filiis hominum, non autem Deo. Item si nunc revelatum
> est mysterium, ante igitur erat, sed absconditum, v. 5.*"
>
> "[The doctrine], although it was not made known in former times but is now first revealed, was
> nevertheless hidden in God from eternity, to be revealed in its own [appointed] time. And this is
> why, in v. 3, when he calls it a mystery: **before, it was not revealed, but unknown to the sons of
> men — though not to God**. Likewise, if the mystery is now revealed, then before it existed, but was
> **hidden**, v. 5."

**This is exactly the clean OT-hidden/NT-revealed timing statement the project has been looking for
— but it is about the Eph 3 mystery of Gentile inclusion, not about marriage.** This confirms Zanchi
does possess and use this precise dispensational-timing move elsewhere in the very same commentary —
which makes its *absence* at Eph 5:32 (§5c above) more likely to be a real, meaningful choice on his
part rather than an accident of what happened to survive in the text.

The specific sentence flagged in the task, read in its fuller context, is more complicated than a
clean "before Christ hidden, after Christ revealed to all" claim:

> "*Praecipuè verò illa hujus mysterii pars, quam explicat vers. 6... hoc demùm fuit, & superioribus
> aetatibus ante Christum & post Christum, Judaeis ferè omnibus mysterium magnum & absconditum. Ergo,
> quòd nunc nobis notum sit acceptum feramus revelationi Spiritus Sancti.*"
>
> "But especially that part of this mystery which he explains in v. 6 [that Gentiles, remaining
> Gentiles, could become one body with the circumcised Jews] — this, in the end, was, **both in the
> former ages before Christ and after Christ, a great and hidden mystery to almost all the Jews**.
> Therefore, that it is now known to us, let us ascribe it to the revelation of the Holy Spirit."

As the task anticipated: this is a claim about **persistent Jewish non-reception, before and after
Christ alike** — not a clean "OT: hidden / NT: revealed" claim. It sits alongside, and in some tension
with, the cleaner dispensational-timing statement quoted just above it (from the six-point list) about
the same overall Eph 3 mystery. **This is entirely about the Eph 3 Gentile-inclusion mystery and must
not be cited as if it were about marriage** — flagging this explicitly per the task's own caution, and
per this project's standing discipline against conflating strands/texts.

### 5e. Final, precise verdict on the core question

**Neither confirmed nor complicated in the direction originally hoped — a clear, nuanced, and now
well-founded result:**

1. **CONFIRMED**: Zanchi, commenting directly on Eph 5:32 itself, defines *mysterium* as a thing
   "hidden from human reason" and "unable to be perceived without... the light of the Holy Spirit,"
   and applies this to marriage's typological meaning (Eve's creation representing "another hidden
   thing," the Church's creation from Christ's side). This is a real, strong, newly-secured
   primary-source finding — upgraded from UNVERIFIED to CONFIRMED — for the **general** claim that
   marriage's Christological meaning is a genuine μυστήριον, inaccessible to unaided reason, known
   only by revelation.
2. **CONFIRMED-ABSENT** (not merely unconfirmed): nowhere in this passage, nowhere in the "adumbrata"
   sentence in *De Spirituali Connubio* Ch. 1 (the only other hidden/shadow-vocabulary hit in the
   entire treatise, confirmed by a full-file search), and nowhere in the newly-read Ch. 4-5 material,
   does Zanchi tie this general epistemic claim to an **OT-believers-had-it-in-shadow /
   NT-believers-have-it-with-more-fulness** redemptive-historical timing argument specifically about
   marriage. He has that exact move available and uses it elsewhere in the same commentary, for a
   different mystery (Eph 3's Gentile inclusion) — which makes the omission at Eph 5:32 look like a
   real feature of how he reads this particular text, not an accident of incomplete access.
3. **Recommendation for the Series I draft**: cite Zanchi, with the Eph 5:32 commentary quotation
   above, as strong confirmed support for "marriage's Christological meaning is a real biblical
   mystery, inaccessible to unaided reason, known only by revelation" — and do **not** cite him as a
   second historical voice (alongside whatever WCF 7.5-6 argument the systematic-theologian pass is
   building) for the specific claim that OT believers had only a shadowed view of marriage's typology
   compared to NT believers. That second claim remains without primary-source support from Zanchi
   after this genuinely thorough pass — a real, useful, honest negative finding, not a disappointing
   one.
