# Reformed-orthodox / Puritan household-duty writers — overview

**Strand:** strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan

**Researched on:** 2026-08-06/07 (background research agent — EEBO-TCP/Bodleian OTA, Internet Archive, secondary scholarship where primary access was blocked)

**Note:** this pass explicitly asked for the wife's-side content with the same weight as the husband's side, per the project's standing discipline. The results below are notably better-balanced than the strand-6 contemporary material — several of these figures (Gouge, Richard Steele, Perkins) have real, concrete, positive content on a wife's own competence and authority, not just "she submits." Preserve that balance in any later draft.

## 1. Girolamo Zanchi — *De Spirituali... Connubio* (1591; ET *The Spiritual Marriage between Christ and His Church*, RHB 2021)

WELL-ATTESTED/CONFIRMED: a dogmatic/typological exposition of Eph 5:22-33, not a household manual — the outlier in genre among this group. Grounds headship in Genesis 2 (Eve's creation as helper), then reads Eph 5 as elevating that creational pattern into Christology: "For our entire salvation consists in this spiritual and divine marriage." Reads the wife's helper-role typologically — as the church helps Christ "by begetting and raising Christ's children." No "weaker vessel" language or Prov 31 material located. UNVERIFIED whether Zanchi treats 1 Cor 11 or Col 3:18-19 at length.

## 2. Thomas Cartwright — largely UNVERIFIED for this topic

No dedicated household-duties treatise found (confirmed via Chilton Powell's standard scholarly survey of the genre, which mentions Cartwright only re: church-government controversy). What is confirmed is narrower: his divorce/remarriage casuistry against Whitgift (innocent party in proven adultery may remarry), and a Latin Proverbs commentary (1617) that would necessarily include Prov 31 but wasn't accessible this pass — flagged as the single most promising unexplored lead. Treat Cartwright as a contextual/ecclesiological figure for now, not a headship-exegesis source.

## 3. William Perkins — *Christian Oeconomie* (1609, posthumous)

CONFIRMED, direct quotes. Definitional move: marriage is a relation where one is "higher, and beareth rule," the other "lower, and yieldeth subjection" — stated as definitional before any fall-argument. Grounds this in creation/sex-distinction via 1 Cor 11:7 ("the male is man of a superior sex... the female is woman of an inferior sex, fit to conceive and bear children"), not primarily the Fall. Restrains headship explicitly: "though the husband be the wife's head, yet it seemeth he hath no power nor liberty granted him" to be cruel. **Positive wife-content**: extended direct use of Prov 31 as the wife's *proper* domestic industry and competence, not decorative praise — "the heart of her husband trusteth her... she seeketh wool and flax, and laboureth cheerfully." Frames the household as "the school wherein the first principles... of government and subjection are learned" (the classic Puritan "little commonwealth" move). "Weaker vessel" phrasing not confirmed in the sections reached (large-file truncation, not necessarily absent).

## 4. William Gouge — *Of Domesticall Duties* (1622) — the anchor text of this strand

CONFIRMED, extensive direct quotes (modern critical edition: *Building a Godly Home*, ed. Beeke & Brown, RHB 2013-14).

**Structure matters**: Treatise I is a 133-section exposition of Eph 5:22-6:9; Treatise II covers 45 sections of *common-mutual* duties (love, cohabitation, mutual prayer, care for each other's salvation) *before* any sex-specific duty — mutuality is textually and logically prior in Gouge's own ordering.

**Headship, non-degrading**: "Though the man be as the head, yet is the woman as the heart." The husband "ought to make her a joint governor of the family with himself, and refer the ordering of many things to her discretion" — direct textual evidence against a flattened "husband decides everything" reading. "Love is as sugar to sweeten the duties of authority... Respect is as salt to season... subjection" — every authority-duty is paired with a love-duty. Most marital misery, per Gouge's dedicatory epistle, comes from "an husband's abuse of his authority," not wives' insubordination.

**Positive wife-content — genuinely rich, this is Gouge's real distinctive**: Treatise III has an enumerated sub-section, §18 "Of the cases wherein a wife hath power to order things of the house without her husband's consent," and §21 on what she "may dispose without her husband's consent." §20 itemizes which expenditures need consent (children, servants, cattle, guests, journeys) rather than issuing a blanket rule. §31 works through a real casuistic question — "Where then is the preferment of the wife above servants and children, if she have not a property [in the goods]?" — distinguishing the wife's *dignity/rank* from mere property right. The dedicatory epistle explicitly carves out her "proper goods" and "extraordinary cases" from any consent-requirement. **This is exactly the casuistic specificity the pastoral-practical lane should draw on** — Gouge works disputed edge cases the way a counselor handles real marriages, not the way a system-builder states doctrine.

"Weaker vessel" (1 Pet 3:7) confirmed via secondary citation, used to argue husbandly forbearance, invoking Abraham/Sarah as the model.

Creation vs. Fall: Gouge visibly wrestles with the fall-objection to a wife's subjection ("That was a punishment inflicted on her for her transgression?") as a live question — his full resolution wasn't fully recoverable this pass (large-file truncation). Flagged for follow-up.

## 5. Robert Cleaver & John Dod — *A Godly Form of Household Government* (1598)

CONFIRMED, direct quotes. Notably the most explicitly **fall-grounded** of this group: cites Gen 3:16 directly as instituting subjection — "the first subjection of the woman began at sin." Also gestures to Gen 2:23-24 companionate unity, so it's both/and, but the Fall is the foregrounded proof-text, more starkly than Perkins or Gouge. Defines headship pastorally: "defender, teacher, and comforter," not merely governmental. **Positive wife-content, notably candid**: explicitly concedes real cases of superior female judgment — "some women are wiser and more discreet than their husbands, as Abigail... Yet still... [she rests] in acknowledging their husbands to be their heads" — subordinating real competence formally rather than denying it exists. A wife facing a difficult command "may for her excuse gently propound the same" — respectful objection is provided for, not suppressed.

## 6. Richard Baxter — *A Christian Directory* (1673)

CONFIRMED but exegetically thinner in the material reached — a real, flagged research gap, not evidence of absence (EEBO 403 blocked full access to the relevant pages). Opens with symmetrical framing: "the first duty of husbands is to love their wives [and wives their husbands]" rather than leading with differentiated authority language. Leading emphasis is *mutual spiritual edification* — helping each other "in the knowledge and worship and obedience of God" — more than scholastic proof-texting of headship. Wife's duty stated as "voluntary subjection... God appointed them their heads," with wives to "learn from their husbands as appointed teachers." Follow-up needed on his specific handling of Eph 5:22-24 and 1 Pet 3:7.

## 7. Daniel Rogers — *Matrimoniall Honour* (1642)

CONFIRMED, direct quotes — a useful addition beyond the original research brief. Grounds headship distinctively in the *imago Dei* itself: "the man doth especially resemble the image of God, and in that respect is the wife's head" — arguably the most purely creation-theology-grounded headship argument found in this whole strand (distinct from Perkins's 1 Cor 11:7 "superior sex" language and Cleaver/Dod's Fall emphasis). Notable companionate-marriage argument: shared religion is "fittest to level and equal" worldly "disproportions... in years, education, disposition, breed, estate" between spouses — an explicit leveling move alongside, not replacing, formal headship. The genre's most sustained positive theology of mutual conjugal *love* as an end in itself.

## 8. Richard Steele (1629-1692) — "The Duties of Husbands and Wives" (Eph 5:33 sermon)

CONFIRMED, direct quotes — and a real citation-accuracy catch: apuritansmind.com's hosting of this genuine Puritan sermon mislabels the author with **Sir Richard Steele the Whig essayist's dates (1672-1729)** rather than the actual divine's (1629-1692) — correct this before citing. (Also distinct from David Steele, 1803-1887, the American/Irish Covenanter — three different "Steele"s, don't conflate any of them.)

Content: holds creation-order and fall-confirmation together most explicitly and cleanly of any figure surveyed — "she was made after the man (1 Tim 2:13), from the man (1 Cor 11:8), and for the man (1 Cor 11:9)" stacked with "even after the fall the divine order stands: 'he shall rule over you' (Gen 3:16)" as confirmation, not origination. Vivid weaker-vessel language: "wives are like crystal glasses, easily broken if not tenderly handled." **Positive wife-content, strong**: "the house is her proper place; she is its beauty; there is her business and safety" (citing Prov 31:11 directly), plus an explicit *efficiency-based* argument for her household authority — husbands have "more important things to do than direct the household servants."

## Cross-cutting findings for the project

1. **Creation vs. Fall is a real, live fault line, not a settled consensus** — Perkins and Rogers lean creation-order; Cleaver/Dod lean explicitly on Gen 3:16; Richard Steele holds both together deliberately; Gouge visibly wrestles with the tension. Represent this range; don't flatten it.
2. **Mutuality-of-duty is universal across every figure but weighted differently** — Baxter and Rogers foreground mutual love/companionship most; Gouge and Cleaver/Dod foreground mutual duty-performance.
3. **Gouge is uniquely casuistic** — his itemized edge-cases are the strongest available historical material for the pastoral-practical lane's "how does this actually work day to day" question.
4. **Positive wife-content is real and recoverable in this strand** (Perkins, Gouge, Cleaver/Dod's Abigail concession, Richard Steele) — this strand, unlike the contemporary strand-6 material so far, does not have the imbalance problem CLAUDE.md flags. Use it as the model for what a properly balanced reading note looks like.
5. **Cartwright is the weak link evidentially** — no household-duties treatise located; his 1617 Proverbs commentary (unaccessed) is the most promising lead if the project wants to pursue him further.
