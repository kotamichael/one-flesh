# John Angell James — household-duty genre, 19th century

**Full citation:** *The Family Monitor, or A Help to Domestic Happiness* (Birmingham, 1828); *The Marriage Ring, or How to Make a Happy Home* (later work, specifically on marriage).

**Strand:** strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan (placed here as the genre's later continuation — see note below on fit)

**Researched on:** 2026-08-07 (direct WebSearch, brief pass — needs a full research treatment before use in a draft)

## A note on fit

James (1785-1859) is English **Nonconformist/Congregationalist**, not Presbyterian, and writes two centuries after Gouge/Perkins/Cleaver & Dod rather than within the Reformed-scholastic period proper. He doesn't cleanly belong to any of the project's five confessional-historical strands as originally scoped. He's included anyway, per the project owner's direction, because *The Family Monitor* and *The Marriage Ring* are direct continuations of the same household-duty genre Gouge anchors — worth reading alongside that strand as its 19th-century evangelical afterlife, with the denominational/period difference flagged rather than smoothed over.

## What the source covers (WELL-ATTESTED, bibliographic/structural level only — not yet read in depth)

*The Family Monitor* has seven chapters; three address husband/wife duties specifically: "the domestic constitution," "the mutual duties of husbands and wives," "the special duties of husbands and wives" (plus a chapter of "remarks on the formation of the marriage union"), before turning to parent/child and master/servant material — a structure recognizably continuous with Gouge's eight-treatise division of the same territory. Secondary description characterizes his emphasis as "mutual love, duty, and godliness."
