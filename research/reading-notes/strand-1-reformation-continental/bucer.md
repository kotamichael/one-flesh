# Martin Bucer (1491-1551)

**Full citation:** see "Best available primary sources" below — multiple works, no single citation.

**Strand:** strand-1-reformation-continental

**Researched on:** 2026-08-10 (bibliography-building pass; not yet a full close-reading — see below)

**Pass type:** EXPAND (bibliography-building). This note catalogs and locates the best available primary
sources on Bucer's marriage/headship/divorce views and pulls a few seed quotes; it is deliberately not
exhaustive close-reading. A dedicated DEEPEN pass should follow, prioritized per the list at the end.

## Best available primary sources

### 1. Marriage-law and marriage-court writings (Strasbourg reform) — the strongest primary source for Lead #1

**Martin Bucers Deutsche Schriften, Band 10: Schriften zu Ehe und Eherecht** (Gütersloh: Gerd Mohn, 2001),
ed. Robert Stupperich, Wilhelm H. Neuser, Gottfried Seebaß, and Christoph Strohm, with Stephen E.
Buckwalter and Hans Schulz. This is the dedicated critical-edition volume of Bucer's German writings on
marriage and marriage law — CONFIRMED to exist and to be freely digitized (viewed directly): 19 numbered
items spanning 1524-1543, including Strasbourg-preachers' marriage opinions (1524, 1527), Bucer's own
consultations on divorce, leprosy, mental illness, and bigamy (1526-1541), formal marriage-law reform
recommendations he drafted for other cities (Ulm, Augsburg, 1531-1537), the substantial "Von der Ehe und
Ehescheidung" ("On Marriage and Divorce," 1533), a "Scriptum maius vom Ehegericht" ("Major Writing on the
Marriage Court," 1534-1537 — this is the direct primary-source document for the Strasbourg *Ehegericht*
marriage-court reform the task asked about), and a 1543 marriage sermon.

- **Language:** German (old-spelling original; no English translation located for this volume).
- **Access:** freely digitized page-by-page with OCR text and downloadable PDF (standard ~209MB, high-res
  ~549MB) at the Heidelberg Academy of Sciences' digital library: https://digi.hadw-bw.de/view/bds10/0048
- **Flag:** old-spelling German only. This is the actual primary-source location for "Bucer's role in
  Strasbourg's marriage-law reforms" — not yet directly read this pass; a deepen pass with German-reading
  capacity (or a located secondary paraphrase of specific items) is needed to extract content.

### 2. *De Regno Christi* (1550) — the mature political-theological work, Book II, chs. ~22-46 on marriage/divorce

**Critical edition (Latin):** *Martini Buceri Opera Latina*, vol. XV: *De Regno Christi: Libri Duo, 1550*,
ed. François Wendel (Paris: Presses Universitaires de France / Gütersloh: C. Bertelsmann, 1955), lxx + 340
pp., with a companion vol. XVbis containing a critical edition of the 1558 French translation. CONFIRMED
to exist via multiple library/review records (Cambridge Core, Journal of Ecclesiastical History review).
Digitized copy located at Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/deregnochristi0000buce (Latin
text with French introduction/notes; access mode — full-view vs. borrow-only — not confirmed this pass).
The broader 5-volume *Opera Latina* set is also at https://archive.org/details/martinibuceriope0001buce.

**Standard modern English translation:** Wilhelm Pauck, ed./trans., in *Melanchthon and Bucer*, Library of
Christian Classics vol. XIX (London: SCM Press / Philadelphia: Westminster Press, 1969).

**Important caveat, WELL-ATTESTED from two independent searches (not yet directly verified against the
book's own table of contents):** the Pauck/LCC English translation of *De Regno Christi* is **abridged**
and specifically **omits the marriage-and-divorce chapters** (reported as chs. 22-46 of Book II — "more
than a quarter of the whole work," per one secondary characterization). **This means the standard,
easiest-to-obtain modern English translation of Bucer's most mature work is not usable for this project's
actual subject matter.** This needs direct verification against a physical/scanned copy of the LCC volume
before being treated as fully settled, but it was reported consistently and independently (a Puritan Board
discussion thread and a separate summary both state the same omission).

### 3. No freestanding marriage/divorce treatise exists separately from #2 — the "Bucer on divorce" material English readers know is Milton's own translation/digest of the *De Regno Christi* chapters

Bucer did not, so far as this pass located, write a dedicated standalone tract on marriage/divorce distinct
from Book II of *De Regno Christi* (item #2) and the marriage-law consultations of item #1. What
English-language readers cite as "Bucer's divorce treatise" is:

**John Milton, trans./ed., *The Judgment of Martin Bucer Concerning Divorce* (London, 1644)** — Milton's
own 17th-century English translation and selective digest of the marriage/divorce chapters of *De Regno
Christi* Book II, published as the second of his four "divorce tracts" to buttress his own controversial
divorce arguments before Parliament.

- **Access, freely available, modernized-spelling scholarly transcription:** Dartmouth Milton Reading Room,
  https://milton.host.dartmouth.edu/reading_room/bucer/text.shtml — this is the cleanest accessible text
  located this pass and is the source of the direct quotes below.
- **Access, old-spelling facsimile-derived text (1820s reprint edition), bundled with Milton's other divorce
  tracts:** Internet Archive, https://archive.org/details/doctrinediscipli00miltrich (large combined
  volume; the Bucer section is deep into the file and was not cleanly isolated this pass).
- **Access, EEBO-TCP:** University of Michigan, https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A69646.0001.001 — returned
  HTTP 403 to automated fetch this pass; may require library/campus access.
- **Critical caveat — this is a mediated source, not a neutral rendering of Bucer:** per Wikipedia's article
  on the work (WELL-ATTESTED, not yet cross-checked against a scholarly edition), Milton "omits many
  sections that did not support Milton's purpose," specifically excluding, e.g., Bucer's material on "the
  joys of single life." Milton was translating selectively and polemically, in service of his own divorce
  arguments. Quotable as period reception and as a reasonably reliable guide to Bucer's core
  divorce-argument structure, but **not a substitute for the Wendel Latin critical edition (item #2) if a
  future pass needs Bucer's full, unfiltered argument** — flag this every time Milton's text is cited as if
  it were simply "Bucer."

**Best secondary source for locating and contextualizing all of the above — the standard modern monograph
on this exact topic:** H. J. Selderhuis, *Marriage and Divorce in the Thought of Martin Bucer*, trans. John
Vriend and Lyle D. Bierma, Sixteenth Century Essays & Studies vol. 48 (Kirksville, MO: Thomas Jefferson
University Press, 1999) — translation of *Huwelijk en Echtscheiding bij Martin Bucer* (Leiden: J.J. Groen
en Zoon, 1994). CONFIRMED to exist via multiple library/review records (Journal of Ecclesiastical History,
Church History, PhilPapers). This is the correct secondary source to consult for **locating** primary
material and for scholarly context — per this project's standing rules, it should not be the sole basis for
characterizing what Bucer himself held without checking the primary texts above.

### 4. Correspondence — *Correspondance de Martin Bucer* / Bucers Briefwechsel

A multivolume critical edition of Bucer's letters, begun by Jean Rott (Strasbourg; d. 1998) and continued
since by the Bucer-Forschungsstelle at Erlangen (Reinhold Friedrich, Berndt Hamm, Wolfgang Simon, with
Matthieu Arnold and Christian Krieger), published in the Brill series *Studies in Medieval and Reformation
Traditions*. CONFIRMED to exist and to be an ongoing, still-being-published critical edition (vol. IX,
covering September 1532-June 1533, appeared from Brill in 2013). A HathiTrust catalog record exists
(https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/000771852) but full open digitization was not confirmed this pass —
this looks like library/subscription access (Brill), not freely downloadable.

- **Specific lead for the deepen pass:** Oecolampadius died November 1531, and Wibrandis Rosenblatt (his
  widow) married Capito in August 1532 with Bucer's active brokering (see below) — so **volume VII (Oct.
  1531-March 1532) and volume VIII (whatever its exact range is, immediately preceding vol. IX's
  Sept.1532 start) are precisely the volumes most likely to contain any direct correspondence on this
  episode**, if it survives. Not yet obtained or read this pass.

### 5. On Lead #1 specifically — "pressure on Oecolampadius to marry": this appears to be a misidentification, correction needed

This pass found **no evidence** that Bucer pressured *Oecolampadius* to marry. What is WELL-ATTESTED
(multiple independent popular/scholarly-adjacent sources — Wikipedia's Wibrandis Rosenblatt article,
Desiring God, Christian History Institute — consistent but not yet traced to a primary letter with a page
citation, so treat as WELL-ATTESTED rather than CONFIRMED) is a different, adjacent chain of events:

- Oecolampadius married the widow Wibrandis Rosenblatt in 1528 on his own initiative; no source located
  this pass shows Bucer's involvement in that specific marriage.
- After Oecolampadius died (November 1531), it was **Capito**, not Oecolampadius, whom Bucer pressed to
  marry the newly widowed Wibrandis. One search result attributed to Bucer the line "My choice for Capito
  is the widow of Oecolampadius... he writes me that he has been very touched by the sight of the widow
  Wibrandis and the orphaned children" — **UNVERIFIED as an exact quotation**: this came through a
  secondary web summary without a clean citation to the letter itself or a scholarly edition/page number,
  and must be checked against the *Correspondance*/Briefwechsel (item #4) or Selderhuis (item #3) before
  it is quoted as Bucer's own words. Capito and Wibrandis married August 1532.
  Bucer here is closer to a marriage-broker between two other people than a party "pressuring" a would-be
  bachelor colleague — a real but different dynamic than the task's lead suggested.
- After Capito died (1541) and Bucer's own wife Elisabeth Silbereisen died in the same 1541-42 plague, it
  was **Elisabeth's own dying request** that Bucer and the twice-widowed Wibrandis marry — which they did
  in March/April 1542. So the "Bride of the Reformation" narrative (Wibrandis's four reformer-marriages:
  Keller, Oecolampadius, Capito, Bucer) is real and WELL-ATTESTED, but the specific claim "Bucer pressured
  Oecolampadius to marry" does not check out as stated. **Flag this to the project owner as a correction to
  the original lead, not a confirmation of it.**

### 6. Biographical note, not yet a located text: Bucer's own early marriage

Bucer himself broke his Dominican vows and married a former nun, Elisabeth Silbereisen, in summer 1522 —
among the earliest reformers to marry after Luther (following, per one secondary source, only Karlstadt and
a then-secret Zwingli). His reputed "first book," *Das ym selbs niemant sonder anderen leben soll* ("That
no one should live for himself but for his neighbor," 1523) was found in search results but its content
was **not confirmed this pass to be specifically about clerical marriage or marriage doctrine** — do not
assume that from the title alone; a deepen pass should check its actual content before citing it here.

### 7. A related but distinct episode not to conflate with divorce theology: the Philip of Hesse bigamy affair (1539-40)

WELL-ATTESTED via secondary scholarship (a 1906 *American Journal of Theology* article by John Alfred
Faulkner, archived at https://archive.org/stream/jstor-3154607/3154607_djvu.txt, plus Selderhuis's
monograph, item #3): Bucer personally carried Landgrave Philip of Hesse's request for permission to take a
*second* wife (Margarethe von der Saale) while his first marriage continued, to Luther and Melanchthon in
Wittenberg (secret counsel given December 1539); the bigamous marriage occurred March 1540 with Bucer and
Melanchthon present. **This is a bigamy question (a second wife added, first marriage intact), not a
divorce question (dissolution of a first marriage) — keep these two distinct** even though both feed
Bucer's reputation for unusual permissiveness on marriage-law questions. Do not cite this affair as
evidence for Bucer's divorce views specifically; it belongs in the project's account, if at all, as a
separate data point about his casuistry on marriage law broadly.

## Seed direct quotes (from item #3, Milton's translation of the *De Regno Christi* divorce chapters)

These are quoted from Milton's 1644 English translation via the Dartmouth Milton Reading Room text — **this
is Milton's own period English rendering of Bucer's Latin, not a modern critical translation**, and per the
caveat above, Milton's selection was polemically motivated. Treat as WELL-ATTESTED-to-CONFIRMED for wording
(verified consistent across two independent fetches of the source page) but flag that the underlying Latin
has not been checked against the Wendel critical edition this pass.

On the purpose/ends of marriage:

> "the full and proper and main end of mariage, is the communicating of all duties, both divine and humane, each to other, with utmost benevolence and affection."
> — Milton's translation, *Judgment of Martin Bucer Concerning Divorce*, ch. XXXVIII/XXXIX region (chapter
> numbering inconsistent across two fetches of the source page this pass — verify exact chapter number
> against a clean copy before final citation)

On the husband's headship duty:

> "That the husband beare himself as the head and preserver of his wife, instructing her to all godlines and integritie of life."
> — same source, same chapter region

On the wife's own duty and role (researched with the same specificity as the husband's side, per this
project's standing rule):

> "the wife be subject to her husband, and study to be truly a meet help to him, as first to all godlines so to every other use of life."
> — same source, ch. XXI ("The means of preserving mariage holy and pure")

> "the wife also be to her husband a help, according to her place."
> — same source, ch. XXXIX region

**Note on the wife's-side material specifically:** this particular source (the divorce chapters of *De
Regno Christi*) is a juridical/legal-theological argument about what makes a marriage valid or voidable,
not a household-duty manual like Bullinger's *Christen State* or the later Puritan genre — so its wife's-
side content is genuinely thinner and more instrumental ("a meet help... to godliness") than what Strand 2
writers (Gouge, Cleaver & Dod) will offer on the wife's own positive competence and governance. This is a
real, honest genre-limitation of this particular text, not a research gap to smooth over: a deepen pass
should check whether Bucer's other writings (the marriage-law consultations in item #1, or any exposition
of Prov 31/Titus 2/1 Pet 3 if one exists) have more developed wife's-side material than this juridical
treatise does. One additional line surfaced in an earlier, less rigorous fetch pass — "the meet help of an
honest and good man, the mother of an honest off-spring and family, the glory of the man, ev'n as the man
is the glory of Christ" (attributed to ch. XL) — but this was **not** reproduced in the follow-up verbatim-
quote fetch, so mark it UNVERIFIED pending direct confirmation, not CONFIRMED.

On Bucer's reasoning for divorce beyond adultery (context, not a headship/submission quote, but load-
bearing for understanding why the marriage material matters to him):

> "between those who either through obstinacy, or helpless inability, cannot or will not perform these repeated duties...there can be no true Matrimony."
> — same source; exact chapter not re-verified verbatim this pass, treat as WELL-ATTESTED not CONFIRMED

Bucer's summary of marriage's necessary properties, per Milton's digest (paraphrase, not a direct quote —
marked as such): cohabitation, mutual love "in the Lord," proper headship/help roles, and conjugal
benevolence — the absence of any one, through "obstinacy or helpless inability," dissolves the covenant on
his view, which is the structural core of his unusually permissive divorce argument.

## Exegetical grounds (if stated)

Not established this pass. The headship/help language quoted above ("head and preserver... a help,
according to her place") echoes Eph 5:22-33 and Gen 2:18 without this pass having located Bucer's own
verse-by-verse exegesis of either text, or of 1 Cor 11, Col 3:18-19, or 1 Pet 3:1-7. **This is the single
biggest open item for the deepen pass** — everything located so far is either (a) the juridical/divorce
argument (item #2/#3) or (b) marriage-law consultation documents not yet read (item #1); neither has been
confirmed to contain sustained exegesis of the core headship/submission texts this project centers on.

## Bearing on the project

Provisionally: Bucer clearly affirms both headship ("head and preserver," instructing the wife "to all
godlines") and the wife's own active duty ("study to be truly a meet help") in the same breath the other
Strand-1 figures use — consistent with, not divergent from, the strand's shared creation-order-plus-mutual-
duty pattern seen in Calvin, Bullinger, and the others (see `five-reformers-overview.md`). His genuinely
distinctive contribution to this strand is not on headship/submission per se but on **divorce** — he is, per
Selderhuis and the Milton-tract tradition, the most permissive figure in this strand on that specific
question, grounding it in a positive definition of marriage (mutual love and duty-sharing) whose absence,
he argues, voids the marriage bond itself. This is worth naming as a genuine outlier position within
Strand 1, not smoothed into "the Reformers held X on divorce" — no comparison yet made this pass to
Calvin's more restrictive Genevan ordinance (see the Calvin section of `five-reformers-overview.md`, which
notes Calvin's own civil-legal integration); a deepen pass should set the two side by side explicitly.
