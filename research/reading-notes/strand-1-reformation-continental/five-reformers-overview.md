# Reformation-era continental theologians — overview

**Strand:** strand-1-reformation-continental

**Researched on:** 2026-08-07 (background research agent — CCEL, Internet Archive OCR of period English translations, PRDL, secondary scholarship including John Witte Jr. and Mickey Mattox)

**Method note:** several sources are unmodernized 16th/17th-century English translations with garbled OCR (u/v, long-s, misreadings); the agent hand-decoded and cross-checked against printed page markers, but exact spelling should be re-verified against a clean edition before final publication quotation.

## 1. Johannes Oecolampadius (1482-1531)

Primary work: *In Genesim Enarratio* (1536, posthumous lecture-commentary on Gen 1-3); modern critical translation: Mickey Mattox, *An Exposition of Genesis* (Marquette, 2013). Secondary anchor: Mattox, "Eve in Early Reformation Exegesis" (2015).

WELL-ATTESTED: shows "a new emphasis on Eve's goodness and original equality with Adam, as well as a parallel insistence on her subordination to her husband after the Fall" — subordination-language reserved for **after** the Fall, not before, though a teleological "made for him" ordering is asserted even pre-Fall: CONFIRMED quote — "God also wished that Adam... would know that she was made for him," said of the naming of animals before Eve's creation. Married late (age ~45) and reluctantly, per Erasmus's own criticism of the match.

**Gap, stated plainly**: no located exegesis of Eph 5, 1 Cor 11, or 1 Pet 3 — his confirmed commentary corpus doesn't include these books.

## 2. Wolfgang Musculus (1497-1563)

Primary work: *Loci Communes* (1560); English trans. John Man, *Common Places of Christian Religion* (1563/1578). CONFIRMED, OCR-decoded and cross-checked against page markers: Musculus organizes marriage under a taxonomy of divinely-given "laws" — the pre-Fall "law of wedlock" (Gen 2:24, leaving/cleaving/one-flesh, **no rule-language**) is structurally separate from a fourth, explicitly **post-Fall** "law" (Gen 3:16) that first introduces "the woman is commanded to be subject and obedient unto her husband," paired reciprocally with the man's law to "provide necessaries... by honest travail." This is the sharpest creation/fall bifurcation found in this whole strand — no pre-Fall rule-language at all, unlike Calvin's "gentle subjection" reading. On Eph 5:31-32, reads "this great mystery" as referring to the Christ/Church relation, not conferring sacramental status on human marriage (part of his broader anti-sacramental-marriage argument). Gap: 1 Cor 11 and 1 Pet 3 not located (likely present in uncleanly-OCR'd Decalogue material).

## 3. Peter Martyr Vermigli (1499-1562)

Primary works: *In Primum Librum Mosis Commentarii* (1569, Genesis, from 1542-47 Strasbourg lectures — WELL-ATTESTED to cover the target texts, not directly quoted this pass); *Loci Communes* (compiled by Masson, trans. Marten, 1583). CONFIRMED, from the *Loci*'s "What dominion the husband hath over the wife" section: Vermigli explicitly surveys a **live patristic dispute** rather than asserting one line — reports Chrysostom **denied** pre-Fall subjection (no subjection-language before Gen 3:16), against an alternative reading (which Vermigli seems to favor) that some order existed via the "helper" relation pre-Fall, intensified after. His own constructive move is distinctive among this whole strand: he classes marital rule through **Aristotelian political categories** — "the Dominion of Aristocratia" (rule by "the chief, which excelleth in virtue and worthiness"), concluding "the husband is [as] head to his wife, because in virtue and wisdom he ought to excel her," while conceding "this [does] not always come to pass" in practice. Explicitly rules out "violence" and requires the ruling head not exercise "tyranny," citing Eph 5's Christ-love pattern. Gap: 1 Cor 11 (he lectured on 1 Corinthians at Strasbourg/Oxford) not directly located this pass.

## 4. Heinrich Bullinger (1504-1575)

Primary works: *Der christlich Eestand* (1540), English trans. Coverdale, *The Christen State of Matrimony* (1541) — a full pastoral/vernacular treatise, one of the most reprinted marriage manuals of the Reformation, especially in England; also *Sermonum Decades Quinque* ("The Decades"). WELL-ATTESTED (not independently verified against a critical edition this pass): defines marriage as an "alliance... coupled and brought into one by mutual consent... to live in chastity and train up their children in the fear of the Lord," and makes a notable anti-misogynist move on the wife's dignity: "a wife is not called an impediment or a necessary evil... but she is the help or arm of man." **Gap, stated plainly**: no located exegesis of Gen 3:16, Eph 5, 1 Cor 11, or 1 Pet 3 specifically — cannot report how he grounds headship (creation vs. fall) with primary-text confidence; his flagship work is pastoral/practical genre (courtship, household economy) rather than technical exegesis, and its influence was through sheer circulation (many English editions), not doctrinal precision.

## 5. John Calvin (1509-1564)

Primary works consulted directly (CCEL): *Commentary on Genesis* (1554, on 1:26-28, 2:18-24, 3:16); *Commentary on Galatians and Ephesians* (1548, on 5:22-33); *Commentary on 1 Corinthians* (1546, on 11:3-12); *Commentaries on the Catholic Epistles* (1551, on 1 Pet 3:1-7). Secondary: John Witte Jr., "John Calvin on Marriage and Family Life"; Witte & Kingdon, *Sex, Marriage, and Family in John Calvin's Geneva*.

CONFIRMED, direct quotes across all five target texts — the richest and most fully-textured figure in this strand.

- **Gen 2:18-24**: "The order of nature implies that the woman should be the helper of the man." Places a *voluntary* submission-disposition within creation itself — Eve to "submit herself willingly to her husband, as being taken out of him."
- **Gen 3:16 — Calvin's signature both/and formula**: "She had, indeed, previously been subject to her husband, but that was a liberal and gentle subjection; now, however, she is cast into servitude." Headship *predates* the Fall (gentle) but the Fall *intensifies* it (harsh) — continuity, not origination. This is the classic "Calvin position" this project should cite precisely, and note it differs from Musculus's sharper bifurcation above.
- **Eph 5:22-33**: grounds headship in Christ's own appointment — "Christ has appointed the same relation to exist between a husband and a wife, as between himself and his church" — with an explicit reciprocal duty: "Let husbands imitate Christ... he scrupled not to die for his church."
- **1 Cor 11:3-12**: distinguishes spiritual equality ("in Christ there is neither male nor female") from functional order; his strongest mutuality formula in the whole strand: "Let the man perform to the woman the office of the head... and let the woman perform to the man the office of the body." Also: "The male sex is but the half of the human race... they... acknowledge themselves to be debtors to the weaker sex," and headship must be exercised "with moderation."
- **1 Pet 3:1-7**: wife's submission is evangelistically purposed ("that they might by their probity allure their husbands to the faith"); "weaker vessel" should evoke protective honor, not contempt; explicit spiritual-equality grounding — "heirs together of the grace of life" means shared graces "in common." Marital peace as precondition for effective prayer.
- **Legal/civil integration (via Witte)**: marriage grounded "in the order of creation... order and law of nature"; husband and wife have "common dignity before God" but "distinct duties" and "different authorities" — and, notably, on divorce specifically: "though in other matters the husband is superior [to the wife], in matters of the marriage bed, the wife has an equal right." Calvin alone among the five integrates this exegesis into an operative civil-legal reform program (Geneva's 1546 Marriage Ordinance).

## Synthesis — real differences across this strand, not a flattened consensus

1. **Where does headship's authority originate?** Calvin: both, continuity-with-intensification (gentle pre-Fall, servile post-Fall). Vermigli: reports the live dispute, leans toward a similar continuity view via the "helper" relation. Musculus: sharp bifurcation — *no* rule-language pre-Fall, rule introduced as a distinct post-Fall "law." Oecolampadius: original equality pre-Fall, subordination introduced after, though a "made for him" teleology is asserted even pre-Fall. Bullinger: unconfirmed.
2. **Genre varies sharply**: Calvin is exegetical-juridical (feeding real Genevan law); Vermigli is exegetical-scholastic (importing Aristotelian categories explicitly); Musculus is exegetical-systematic (a taxonomy of "laws"); Bullinger is pastoral-practical (lay household manual); Oecolampadius is exegetical-historical (a running lecture-commentary).
3. **Mutuality** is affirmed by all four with substantive material, differently expressed: Calvin's reciprocal head/body "offices" and "heirs together"; Musculus's paired laws (obedience/provision); Vermigli's Aristotelian requirement that rule serve the ruled and exclude "violence"/"tyranny"; Oecolampadius's "partners of equal dignity."
4. **"Weaker vessel"** confirmed only for Calvin in this strand — do not assume the other four share his specific gloss.
5. **Thinness is real and figure-specific**: Calvin is abundant and cleanly quotable; Oecolampadius has one dedicated, well-scholarshiped work but almost no coverage of the other four texts; Musculus and Vermigli have substantial primary material but labor-intensive to extract, with 1 Cor 11 and 1 Pet 3 eluding this pass for both; Bullinger has the most historically influential work of the set but the least fine-grained exegetical detail located.
