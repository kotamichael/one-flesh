# Reformation-era continental theologians — overview

**Strand:** strand-1-reformation-continental

**Researched on:** 2026-08-07 (background research agent — CCEL, Internet Archive OCR of period English translations, PRDL, secondary scholarship including John Witte Jr. and Mickey Mattox); follow-up pass 2026-08-08 (closing specific gaps); **bibliography-upgrade pass 2026-08-08 (this pass — surveying modern critical editions, dedicated translation series, and correspondence collections not yet used, per project-owner request to upgrade sourcing quality rather than re-confirm existing content)**.

**Method note:** several sources are unmodernized 16th/17th-century English translations with garbled OCR (u/v, long-s, misreadings); the agent hand-decoded and cross-checked against printed page markers, but exact spelling should be re-verified against a clean edition before final publication quotation.

**Bibliography-upgrade pass — headline findings, in one place:** (1) **Musculus** — the biggest find: two dedicated exegetical commentaries (1 & 2 Corinthians, 1559; Galatians/Ephesians, 1561) exist separately from the *Loci Communes* already used, and are the right place to look for his 1 Cor 11 and Eph 5 material — no English translation of either exists yet. (2) **Calvin** — modern critical Latin (COR) now covers Ephesians and the Catholic Epistles/1 Peter, and the Torrance/Eerdmans CNTC English series is a real upgrade over CCEL for 1 Corinthians, Ephesians, and 1 Peter specifically; Genesis and the Latin text of 1 Corinthians remain un-upgraded; a named, unread letters lead (the Falais correspondence) surfaced. (3) **Bullinger** — a 2009 German critical edition of his marriage writings (plus a previously-unknown 1527 marriage tract) exists, and his correspondence is digitized and searchable, but neither has an English translation or was read for content this pass. (4) **Vermigli** — the Genesis translation remains unpublished and the 1 Corinthians translation's "in press" status from 2020 is now stale and possibly stalled; a new, currently-irrelevant Loci Communes retranslation series is underway and worth monitoring. (5) **Oecolampadius** — confirmed no better source exists; the 2013 Mattox translation is already the best available.

## 1. Johannes Oecolampadius (1482-1531)

Primary work: *In Genesim Enarratio* (1536, posthumous lecture-commentary on Gen 1-3); modern critical translation: Mickey Mattox, *An Exposition of Genesis* (Marquette, 2013). Secondary anchor: Mattox, "Eve in Early Reformation Exegesis" (2015).

WELL-ATTESTED: shows "a new emphasis on Eve's goodness and original equality with Adam, as well as a parallel insistence on her subordination to her husband after the Fall" — subordination-language reserved for **after** the Fall, not before, though a teleological "made for him" ordering is asserted even pre-Fall: CONFIRMED quote — "God also wished that Adam... would know that she was made for him," said of the naming of animals before Eve's creation. Married late (age ~45) and reluctantly, per Erasmus's own criticism of the match.

**Gap, now confirmed as permanent rather than merely unlocated (2026-08-08 follow-up, via PRDL's full author catalog, 193 titles/229 vols)**: Oecolampadius's confirmed exegetical corpus is Genesis, Job, Psalms, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Daniel, the minor prophets, Matthew, Romans, Colossians, Hebrews, and 1 John — **1 Corinthians and 1 Peter are simply not in it**. He never lectured on or published commentary on either book. This is a fact about his output, not a research failure — there is no text to locate for Eph 5, 1 Cor 11, or 1 Pet 3 from him, ever. His Genesis material (above) is the full extent of what can be drawn from him on this topic.

**Bibliography upgrade pass, 2026-08-08 — honest negative finding.** Checked whether Mickey Mattox (Marquette; now Hillsdale College) has produced any further Oecolampadius translation since the 2013 *An Exposition of Genesis*, and whether anyone else has. His current CV (as of August 2025) shows no additional Oecolampadius translation project. No other translator or press has taken up further Oecolampadius work found via search. **The Mattox 2013 critical translation (Reformation Texts with Translation series, Marquette University Press) remains the best source available for this figure, and there is nothing better to upgrade to.** Combined with the corpus fact above, this closes Oecolampadius as a research target for this project — not because of a research limitation, but because the source material genuinely does not extend further.

## 2. Wolfgang Musculus (1497-1563)

Primary work: *Loci Communes* (1560); English trans. John Man, *Common Places of Christian Religion* (1563/1578). CONFIRMED, OCR-decoded and cross-checked against page markers: Musculus organizes marriage under a taxonomy of divinely-given "laws" — the pre-Fall "law of wedlock" (Gen 2:24, leaving/cleaving/one-flesh, **no rule-language**) is structurally separate from a fourth, explicitly **post-Fall** "law" (Gen 3:16) that first introduces "the woman is commanded to be subject and obedient unto her husband," paired reciprocally with the man's law to "provide necessaries... by honest travail." This is the sharpest creation/fall bifurcation found in this whole strand — no pre-Fall rule-language at all, unlike Calvin's "gentle subjection" reading. On Eph 5:31-32, reads "this great mystery" as referring to the Christ/Church relation, not conferring sacramental status on human marriage (part of his broader anti-sacramental-marriage argument).

**1 Cor 11 / 1 Pet 3, 2026-08-08 follow-up: still not located, but honestly reported as UNVERIFIED rather than a confirmed negative.** A search of the full *Common Places* volume found the marriage section heading (~p.339) but couldn't extract its full body text this pass; word-search for "weaker vessel," "covering," "helper," "subjection," "Titus" all returned zero hits, but this is judged untrustworthy given known long-s/OCR fragility in this exact scan and the fact that the *first* pass already located substantive marriage material from the very same source. **Do not treat this as a confirmed absence — it needs a clean-text edition or a manual page-339-onward read to close properly**, which the project doesn't yet have.

**Bibliography upgrade pass, 2026-08-08 — the real fix for the 1 Cor 11 gap is a different primary source, not a better edition of the** ***Loci Communes*.** Two prior passes searched only the *Loci Communes* (a systematic *topical* work) for 1 Cor 11 and 1 Pet 3 material and came up empty/unreliable. That was the wrong text to search. Musculus wrote **dedicated, separate exegetical commentaries** covering exactly these books, distinct from the *Loci* and never checked by this project:

- ***In ambas apostoli Pauli ad Corinthios epistolas commentarii*** (Basel, 1559; a 1566 Basel edition is also in library catalogs) — his dedicated verse-by-verse commentary on **both** epistles to the Corinthians, so it covers 1 Cor 11:2-16 directly. CONFIRMED to exist and be digitized (a period Latin scan is available via Google Play Books; also documented in CERL and library catalogs). A dedicated secondary treatment exists — Scott M. Manetsch (Trinity Evangelical Divinity School), "(Re)constructing the Pastoral Office: Wolfgang Musculus's Commentaries on 1 & 2 Corinthians," in *On the Writing of New Testament Commentaries: Festschrift for Grant R. Osborne* (Brill, 2013) — access was blocked (403) this pass, so its content on 1 Cor 11 specifically is UNVERIFIED, but its existence (author now confirmed via a separate search, not just the Brill listing) confirms the commentary is real and has scholarly attention. No English translation or modern critical edition located — Latin only.
- ***In Epistolas Apostoli Pauli ad Galatas et Ephesios commentarii*** (Basel, 1561; reprinted 1569) — his dedicated commentary on Galatians **and Ephesians**, so it covers Eph 5:22-33 directly and at far greater length than the *Loci*'s brief 5:31-32 aside (already found and reported above). CONFIRMED to exist via library/bibliographic record; not yet located as a clean digitized text or translation this pass.
- Musculus's full confirmed exegetical output, cross-checked across sources: Matthew (1544), John (1545), Psalms (1550), the Decalogue (1553), Genesis (1554), Romans (1555), Isaiah (1557), **1 & 2 Corinthians (1559)**, **Galatians/Ephesians (1561)**, and Philippians/Colossians/1&2 Thessalonians/1 Timothy (1564). **No 1 Peter commentary exists in his corpus** — this parallels the confirmed Oecolampadius negative above and should be treated the same way: a fact about his output, not an unlocated text. Do not keep searching for Musculus on 1 Pet 3; there is nothing to find.

**Recommendation for the next pass**: acquire or digitize the 1559 Corinthians commentary and the 1561 Galatians/Ephesians commentary directly (both Latin, no known translation) rather than re-attempting the *Loci Communes* OCR. This reframes the open gap from "OCR failure on a known source" to "right source not yet obtained" — a more tractable problem.

## 3. Peter Martyr Vermigli (1499-1562)

Primary works: *In Primum Librum Mosis Commentarii* (1569, Genesis, from 1542-47 Strasbourg lectures — WELL-ATTESTED to cover the target texts, not directly quoted this pass); *Loci Communes* (compiled by Masson, trans. Marten, 1583). CONFIRMED, from the *Loci*'s "What dominion the husband hath over the wife" section: Vermigli explicitly surveys a **live patristic dispute** rather than asserting one line — reports Chrysostom **denied** pre-Fall subjection (no subjection-language before Gen 3:16), against an alternative reading (which Vermigli seems to favor) that some order existed via the "helper" relation pre-Fall, intensified after. His own constructive move is distinctive among this whole strand: he classes marital rule through **Aristotelian political categories** — "the Dominion of Aristocratia" (rule by "the chief, which excelleth in virtue and worthiness"), concluding "the husband is [as] head to his wife, because in virtue and wisdom he ought to excel her," while conceding "this [does] not always come to pass" in practice. Explicitly rules out "violence" and requires the ruling head not exercise "tyranny," citing Eph 5's Christ-love pattern. Gap, 2026-08-08 follow-up, now precisely specified rather than vaguely open: his own Genesis commentary (*In Primum Librum Mosis Commentarii*, 1569, Latin, from the 1542-44 Strasbourg lectures, reaching through Gen 42:25 so it does cover 3:16 in principle) has no accessible English translation yet located — a Peter Martyr Library volume (ed. Donnelly) was announced by the Davenant Institute in 2017 but its publication status is UNVERIFIED as of this pass. An English translation of his 1 Corinthians commentary by Jon Balserak, under contract with Truman State University Press, is CONFIRMED to exist as a translation project (status "Accepted/In press" per a University of Bristol research-output record, 2020) but no accessible excerpt was located. Secondary scholarship confirming both are real, substantive texts: *A Companion to Peter Martyr Vermigli* (Brill, 2009) contains dedicated chapters on his Genesis exegesis (Campi) and his 1 Corinthians exegesis (Balserak), neither accessible this pass. **A caution against a false lead**: Vermigli's Oxford lectures on 1 Corinthians are the documented source of his published *Oxford Treatise and Disputation on the Eucharist* (1549) — but that addresses 1 Cor 11:17-34 (the Lord's Supper), not 11:2-16 (head covering/headship). Do not cite the Eucharist treatise as if it closes this gap. **Recommend to the project owner**: acquiring these two specific published/in-progress translations directly is the efficient next step, not further web search.

**Bibliography upgrade pass, 2026-08-08 — current status checked directly, mixed results.**
- **Genesis commentary (Donnelly translation)**: STILL UNPUBLISHED. The Davenant Institute's own current Peter Martyr Vermigli Library page lists nine completed volumes in the reprint series (Early Writings; Dialogue on the Two Natures in Christ; Sacred Prayers; Philosophical Works; Life, Letters, and Sermons; Commentary on Lamentations; the Oxford Eucharist Treatise; Predestination and Justification; Commentary on Aristotle's Nicomachean Ethics) and explicitly notes the original series "reached ten volumes before running out of funding" — the Genesis commentary is not among the nine listed and its status remains unresolved as of this pass. Volume 1, *On Original Sin*, is published (2nd ed. available as of this pass) as "Common Places I." ## 4. Heinrich Bullinger (1504-1575)

Primary works: *Der christlich Eestand* (1540), English trans. Coverdale, *The Christen State of Matrimony* (1552 imprint of the 1541 translation) — old-spelling original, OCR imperfect; also *The Decades*, Second Decade, Tenth Sermon (Parker Society critical edition, Cambridge 1849-52, modernized spelling — a different register from the *Christen State* text, noted explicitly). WELL-ATTESTED: defines marriage as an "alliance... coupled and brought into one by mutual consent... to live in chastity and train up their children in the fear of the Lord," and makes a notable anti-misogynist move on the wife's dignity: "a wife is not called an impediment or a necessary evil... but she is the help or arm of man."

**Gap substantially closed, 2026-08-08 follow-up — both primary sources now directly searched and quoted:**
- **Gen 3:16**: CONFIRMED but thin — *Christen State* paraphrases the curse in passing ("I wyl surely increase thy sorrow... to thy husband shalt thou have respect") without developing hierarchical implications from it, treating it as a fallen-world hardship Christ mitigates rather than as his ground for headship. Not discussed at all in the Decades sermon.
- **Eph 5:22-33**: a genuine negative finding on citation, but CONFIRMED close functional paraphrase without a verse-tag: *Christen State* cites "Ephe.v." for 5:3 (fornication), not 5:22-33; but the Decades sermon paraphrases the headship logic directly without naming the text: "Let the husband be the head of the wife, to wit, her adviser and counsellor, her ruler and guide, her sweet yokefellow and admonisher," paired with "Let the wife be obedient unto her husband, even as we see the members obey the head."
- **1 Cor 11 and Col 3:18-19**: checked directly in both works — zero matches for either. Bullinger appears simply not to engage 1 Cor 11 or Col 3:18-19 by name in either of his two major marriage-relevant works.
- **1 Pet 3:1-7**: CONFIRMED, direct old-spelling quote, explicitly tagged "i. Pet. iii.": "Let the wiues be in subieccion to their husbandes, that euen they which beleue not the worde, maye... be wonne by the good conuersacion." But "weaker vessel" (3:7) and Sarah/1 Pet 3:6 are checked and absent from both works.
- **Wife's-side content, checked with equal specificity**: genuinely positive in *Christen State* — the headship statement is embedded in a mutuality frame: Eve was made "out of [Adam's] side, as one that is set next unto [him], **to be his help & companion**," immediately following "the husband is the head and master of the wife." Also "one helping and comforting the other" and maternal child-rearing as a recurring positive theme. The Decades sermon, by contrast, is genuinely thin here — its content skews toward warnings against adultery/intemperance rather than the wife's competence. **Flag this asymmetry explicitly if citing Bullinger on the wife's side**: the positive material comes disproportionately from *Christen State*, not the Decades.

**Bottom line**: no longer a total gap. Headship is CONFIRMED (grounded in creation/the rib, echoing 1 Cor 11:8-9/Eph 5:23 logic without chapter-tagging either); 1 Pet 3:1 is CONFIRMED by direct citation; 1 Cor 11, Col 3:18-19, "weaker vessel," and any developed Gen 3:16 exegesis remain genuine, now-checked negative findings.

**Bibliography upgrade pass, 2026-08-08 — a real upgrade exists, but not in English.**
- **Modern critical edition of *Der christlich Eestand*, CONFIRMED to exist**: Detlef Roth, ed., *Heinrich Bullinger, Werke, 3. Abteilung: Theologische Schriften, Band 5: Pastoraltheologische Schriften* (Theologischer Verlag Zürich, 2009). This is a genuine scholarly critical edition — not a reprint — covering **three** of Bullinger's pastoral-theological writings together: the 1527 *Vollkommne underrichtung des christenlichen eestands* (an earlier, shorter marriage tract predating *Der christlich Eestand* and not previously known to this project), the 1540 *Der christlich Eestand* itself, and the 1535 *Bericht der Kranken* (a guide for visiting the sick, not marriage-relevant). It includes a full critical apparatus and a CD-ROM for text search. **This is a better primary text than the OCR'd 1552 Coverdale English print used in this project's existing notes** — but it is in early modern German, not English, and no English translation of the Roth critical edition itself has been located. **Practical implication**: if a future pass wants finer-grained, textually reliable Bullinger material (rather than the OCR-fragile English translation), Roth 2009 is where to go, but it requires a German-reading researcher or translator; it does not replace the Coverdale text as this project's accessible English source. Also worth flagging as newly discovered: the existence of the 1527 tract as a second, earlier marriage-relevant primary work by Bullinger, not previously identified by this project.
- **Correspondence, CONFIRMED to exist and be searchable, content UNVERIFIED**: the Bullinger Digital project (Universität Zürich / Heinrich Bullinger Stiftung, ongoing since the 1960s as a card index, online since 2013) has digitized and made searchable Bullinger's correspondence — roughly 2,000 letters he wrote and up to 12,000 he received survive, with several thousand now edited/transcribed and an advanced search interface (filterable by correspondent, date, place). This is a real, substantial, and so-far-untapped resource that plausibly contains marriage counsel to specific correspondents (parallel to what was found for Calvin below), but **no specific marriage-counsel letter was located or confirmed this pass** — this is a lead for future research, not a finding. It also requires Latin/Early New High German reading.
- **Net assessment**: existing English-language sourcing (Coverdale's *Christen State*, the Parker Society *Decades*) remains the right choice for accessible English quotation, but the project should note both of these as documented, real upgrade paths for a future pass with more time or a German-reading collaborator, rather than treating current sourcing as the ceiling.

## 5. John Calvin (1509-1564)

Primary works consulted directly (CCEL): *Commentary on Genesis* (1554, on 1:26-28, 2:18-24, 3:16); *Commentary on Galatians and Ephesians* (1548, on 5:22-33); *Commentary on 1 Corinthians* (1546, on 11:3-12); *Commentaries on the Catholic Epistles* (1551, on 1 Pet 3:1-7). Secondary: John Witte Jr., "John Calvin on Marriage and Family Life"; Witte & Kingdon, *Sex, Marriage, and Family in John Calvin's Geneva*.

CONFIRMED, direct quotes across all five target texts — the richest and most fully-textured figure in this strand.

- **Gen 2:18-24**: "The order of nature implies that the woman should be the helper of the man." Places a *voluntary* submission-disposition within creation itself — Eve to "submit herself willingly to her husband, as being taken out of him."
- **Gen 3:16 — Calvin's signature both/and formula**: "She had, indeed, previously been subject to her husband, but that was a liberal and gentle subjection; now, however, she is cast into servitude." Headship *predates* the Fall (gentle) but the Fall *intensifies* it (harsh) — continuity, not origination. This is the classic "Calvin position" this project should cite precisely, and note it differs from Musculus's sharper bifurcation above.
- **Eph 5:22-33**: grounds headship in Christ's own appointment — "Christ has appointed the same relation to exist between a husband and a wife, as between himself and his church" — with an explicit reciprocal duty: "Let husbands imitate Christ... he scrupled not to die for his church."
- **1 Cor 11:3-12**: distinguishes spiritual equality ("in Christ there is neither male nor female") from functional order; his strongest mutuality formula in the whole strand: "Let the man perform to the woman the office of the head... and let the woman perform to the man the office of the body." Also: "The male sex is but the half of the human race... they... acknowledge themselves to be debtors to the weaker sex," and headship must be exercised "with moderation."
- **1 Pet 3:1-7**: wife's submission is evangelistically purposed ("that they might by their probity allure their husbands to the faith"); "weaker vessel" should evoke protective honor, not contempt; explicit spiritual-equality grounding — "heirs together of the grace of life" means shared graces "in common." Marital peace as precondition for effective prayer.
- **Legal/civil integration (via Witte)**: marriage grounded "in the order of creation... order and law of nature"; husband and wife have "common dignity before God" but "distinct duties" and "different authorities" — and, notably, on divorce specifically: "though in other matters the husband is superior [to the wife], in matters of the marriage bed, the wife has an equal right." Calvin alone among the five integrates this exegesis into an operative civil-legal reform program (Geneva's 1546 Marriage Ordinance).

**Bibliography upgrade pass, 2026-08-08 — genuine upgrades for two of four target texts; Genesis and 1 Corinthians remain best-served by the existing CCEL/CTS text; and a promising unread letters lead surfaced.**

## Synthesis — real differences across this strand, not a flattened consensus

1. **Where does headship's authority originate?** Calvin: both, continuity-with-intensification (gentle pre-Fall, servile post-Fall). Vermigli: reports the live dispute, leans toward a similar continuity view via the "helper" relation. Musculus: sharp bifurcation — *no* rule-language pre-Fall, rule introduced as a distinct post-Fall "law." Oecolampadius: original equality pre-Fall, subordination introduced after, though a "made for him" teleology is asserted even pre-Fall. Bullinger: thin but now attested — creation/the rib grounds headship in *Christen State*, with no developed treatment of Gen 3:16's hierarchical implications located in either primary source checked.
2. **Genre varies sharply**: Calvin is exegetical-juridical (feeding real Genevan law); Vermigli is exegetical-scholastic (importing Aristotelian categories explicitly); Musculus is exegetical-systematic (a taxonomy of "laws"); Bullinger is pastoral-practical (lay household manual); Oecolampadius is exegetical-historical (a running lecture-commentary).
3. **Mutuality** is affirmed by all four with substantive material, differently expressed: Calvin's reciprocal head/body "offices" and "heirs together"; Musculus's paired laws (obedience/provision); Vermigli's Aristotelian requirement that rule serve the ruled and exclude "violence"/"tyranny"; Oecolampadius's "partners of equal dignity."
4. **"Weaker vessel"** confirmed only for Calvin in this strand — do not assume the other four share his specific gloss.
5. **Thinness is real and figure-specific**: Calvin is abundant and cleanly quotable; Oecolampadius has one dedicated, well-scholarshiped work but almost no coverage of the other four texts; Musculus and Vermigli have substantial primary material but labor-intensive to extract, with 1 Cor 11 and 1 Pet 3 eluding this pass for both; Bullinger has the most historically influential work of the set but the least fine-grained exegetical detail located.
