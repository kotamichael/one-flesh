# George W. Knight III, J. Ligon Duncan III, Richard D. Phillips — and why Grudem is excluded

**Strand:** strand-6-contemporary-interlocutors

**Researched on:** 2026-08-07 (background research agent, WebSearch/WebFetch — sermon transcripts, Wikipedia, reviews, seminary bios; full book text of Knight's and Phillips's works not directly accessed)

**Purpose of this pass:** the project owner asked for more academically substantial confessional-Presbyterian complementarian voices than Kevin DeYoung's popular-level treatment supplies.

## George W. Knight III (1931-2021)

CONFIRMED bibliography: *The New Testament Teaching on the Role Relationship of Men and Women* (Baker, 1977), revised as *The Role Relationship of Men and Women: New Testament Teaching* (Moody, 1985); *The Pastoral Epistles* (NIGTC, Eerdmans, 1992). OPC-ordained; also served RPCES/PCA pulpits; taught at Covenant Theological Seminary, Knox Theological Seminary, Greenville Presbyterian Theological Seminary; PhD, Free University of Amsterdam; President, CBMW (1993-95) and ETS (1995).

WELL-ATTESTED: Knight coined "role relationship"/"role subordination" language — men and women ontologically equal, functionally role-differentiated, with headship grounded in creation (1 Tim 2:13, Adam formed first) and therefore transcultural. His word-study of *authenteō* (1 Tim 2:12) across its known Greek occurrences argued the "authority" in view is "a positive concept... in no way regarded as having any overtone of misuse of position or power" — legitimate authority, not domineering usurpation.

**A significant liability, worth flagging directly**: per Kevin Giles's critical history (*The Rise and Fall of the Complementarian Doctrine of the Trinity*) and multiple corroborating sources, Knight (1977) was the first writer to analogize male headship/female submission in marriage to the Father-Son relation in 1 Cor 11:3, describing the Son as "eternally" or "permanently" subordinate to the Father in role while equal in being. This became, by 2016, the seed of the Eternal Functional/Relational Subordination of the Son (EFS/ERAS) controversy (Grudem, Bruce Ware defending it; Carl Trueman, Liam Goligher, and other confessional Reformed critics — not only egalitarians — opposing it as a threat to Nicene Trinitarian orthodoxy). **Any future use of Knight on 1 Cor 11:3 must flag this controversy explicitly rather than adopt the Trinity-analogy uncritically** — this project does not draw on that argument.

**Research-coverage note, not a verdict on the source**: no substantive positive material from Knight on the wife's own dignity, competence, or calling was located in the secondary literature accessed (reviews, obituaries, seminary bios) — this may reflect what's easily searchable about a technical 1977/1985/1992 corpus rather than the book's actual content. Someone would need to read the primary text directly (especially his treatment of 1 Cor 11:11-12's mutuality clause, and 1 Pet 3) before this gap can be reported as more than a research-coverage limitation.

## J. Ligon Duncan III

CONFIRMED: PCA minister, Chancellor/CEO of Reformed Theological Seminary; former Chairman of the Board and President of CBMW, now Senior Fellow. Key sources: "Male Authority and Female Equality: In the Beginning" (Genesis 1-3 essay/sermon, First Presbyterian Church, Jackson, MS, 2003); "God's New Family: An Exposition of Ephesians" sermon series (installments on Eph 5:22-33); "The Pastoral Statements of Confessional Complementarianism" (CBMW pre-conference, Together for the Gospel).

Direct quotes (CONFIRMED, cross-checked across two independently mirrored transcript sources): "God has created men and women equal in their essential dignity and human personhood, but different and complementary in function with male headship." "Adam's headship in marriage was established by God before the Fall, and was not the result of sin." On limits: "Your husband tells you to sin, you're a Christian, the answer is 'No!'" On the character of headship: "The husband is not a tyrannical monarch. He is not a totalitarian ruler" — he explicitly distinguishes "male headship" from "male domination," defined as "the bold assertion of man's will over woman's will heedless of her spiritual equality, her best interests, and her values."

Positive wife-side content, genuinely present: grounds dignity in shared image-bearing ("Men and women are equal in the sense that they bear God's image equally"); affirms women's calling to fulfilling ministry; in the Ephesians submission sermon, cites Prov 31 approvingly ("she opens her mouth in wisdom... teaches with kindness") and states directly: "A submissive wife offers suggestions and advice and counsel, and even correction to her husband." Note: his separate sermon specifically on "Love Your Wife" (Eph 5:25ff.) is weighted toward the husband's sacrificial obligation with the wife's equal worth left more implicit — even within one figure's corpus, balance varies sermon to sermon.

## Richard D. Phillips

CONFIRMED: Senior Minister, Second Presbyterian Church (PCA), Greenville, SC; co-editor, Reformed Expository Commentary series; Council member, The Gospel Coalition. Primary relevant work: *The Masculine Mandate: God's Calling to Men* (Reformation Trust, 2010) — a book about manhood generally (marriage, fatherhood, work, friendship, church), not a marriage-roles treatise specifically.

**A citation the project owner named could not be verified**: *Women, Ministry, and the Gospel: Exploring a Complementarian Vision* (with Wellum and Chapell) — extensive searching (title search, author-combination search, publisher catalogs) did not locate this book under that title with those editors. It may be a conflation with a different title (e.g., Husbands & Larsen's *Women, Ministry and the Gospel: Exploring New Paradigms*, IVP 2007 — a different book, different editors, no Phillips involvement) — **do not cite this title until re-verified**.

WELL-ATTESTED (via reviews, primary text not independently cross-checked): headship does not mean the husband "does everything or decides everything" — he "typically initiates and always leads shared discussions," while "the wife's opinion is vitally important" and "a godly couple should be a close-knit team." Grounds marital dysfunction in the Fall, reading Gen 3:16's "desire" as a controlling/contrary desire (following the Susan Foh line common in Reformed complementarianism), remedied only through gospel-wrought sanctification.

This project's own research pass did not turn up material from Phillips developing the wife's own competence and calling in comparable depth to Duncan's Prov 31 material above — noted here as a limit of what this pass located, not as a characterization of the book's balance or intent, which was written to address men specifically.

## Wayne Grudem — excluded from this bibliography

CONFIRMED: Grudem is a confessing credobaptist ("Reformed Baptist" by his own description, in "A Baptist Because of the Bible," in *Why I Am a Baptist*, ed. Nettles & Moore, 2001) — not Presbyterian, and therefore outside this project's confessional-Presbyterian scope regardless of his broader Calvinistic soteriology or his elder-plurality polity views. Worth a footnote, not inclusion: his *Evangelical Feminism and Biblical Truth* (2004) and his role in the 2016 EFS/ERAS controversy are the point where Knight's 1977 Trinity-analogy argument became mainstream and was then publicly contested.

## Bearing on the project

Gives the contemporary bibliography real academic range: Duncan is the best-attested for balance between headship and the wife's own dignity/voice; Knight supplies technical exegetical depth (creation order, *authenteō*) alongside a clearly-flagged theological hazard (the Trinity analogy) this project explicitly declines to adopt; Phillips supplies a pastoral, man-addressed account whose wife-side content this pass simply didn't locate — a research gap to close, not a conclusion to draw.

## Follow-up pass, 2026-08-08

### Knight on 1 Cor 11:11-12 and 1 Pet 3 — CONFIRMED via a Knight-authored primary text

Located a full-text essay by George W. Knight III himself, "The Role of Women in the Church," reproduced at bible-researcher.com/knight1.html. This is not the 1977/1985 book itself, but it is Knight's own words on the identical argument (the essay's biographical note at the end matches Knight's academic credentials), and it directly answers the question the first pass couldn't: does Knight develop anything positive on the wife's own dignity, beyond role-subordination?

CONFIRMED direct quotes:

> "They were created essentially equal: as holy bearers of God's image before the fall, after it as sinners equally in need of redemption, and... as spiritually equal receivers of God's salvation." — Knight, "The Role of Women in the Church"

> "Nothing in the Apostles' teaching indicates that men are intrinsically superior to women, even in marriage or the life of the church." — same

> "However, in the Lord, neither is woman independent of man, nor is man independent of woman" [1 Cor 11:11-12] — same

On 1 Pet 3: "Peter speaks... teaching that the wife is an heir of the gracious gift of life with her husband, or a fellow heir of the grace of life" — Knight cites this as 1 Peter 3:7 in the section heading, but the in-text citation on the page itself reads "(I Peter 5:7)," an apparent transcription error (reproduced consistently across two independent fetches of the same page, so it is a feature of the source text, not this pass's error) — **verify chapter/verse directly against Knight's own text before quoting**, and flag/correct if citing.

Immediately following, on the husband's side: "Husbands are not called to require their wives to submit to them, but must rather themselves submit to God, and graciously, lovingly, and tenderly lead and guide their wives and families" (1 Pet 3:7; Eph 5:23-33); "Husbands are to be neither harsh nor bitter" (Col 3:19).

**Honest assessment, not padding**: this closes the "found nothing at all" gap — Knight does affirm essential equality, non-superiority, and the 1 Cor 11:11-12 mutuality clause in his own words, more than the first pass located. But it does not amount to developed material on the wife's own competence, initiative, or calling in the way Duncan's Prov 31 material or the wife-specific texts (Titus 2:3-5, 1 Pet 3:1-6) invite — Knight's essay stays at the level of "essentially equal in being, distinct in role," not "here is what she brings/does." The research-coverage gap on Knight's positive material for the wife's *active* side (as opposed to her equal standing) is narrowed but not closed; the 1977/1985 book itself is still unread.

### Phillips, *The Masculine Mandate* — re-checked, gap confirmed as a scope limit

Re-checked three independent reviews (9Marks/Jonathan Leeman, Themelios/Gospel Coalition, and a personal-blog review) specifically for any quoted material on the wife's own competence/gifts/initiative beyond what the first pass found. None surfaced anything new. The three reviews' quoted excerpts remain husband-facing: "he typically initiates and always leads... the wife's opinion is vitally important, and a godly couple should be a close-knit team"; she is "a suitable helper who is equal in worth as the man, yet she is different"; "[she] is good for our physical, emotional, spiritual, and sexual well-being."

UNVERIFIED (honestly, not padded): still no developed wife-competence material located in *The Masculine Mandate* specifically. But this may be a scope limit of the book, not a balance failure by Phillips generally — it is explicitly a book addressed to men about being a man (work, fatherhood, friendship, marriage), not a marriage-roles treatise. **New lead for a future pass**: Phillips has written a full-length *Ephesians* commentary (*Ephesians: A Mentor Expository Commentary*, Christian Focus, 2016) and a two-volume *Genesis* Reformed Expository Commentary (P&R, 2023) — both would engage Eph 5:22-33 and Gen 2's "helper" language directly and at much greater length than a book written to men only. Neither has been checked by any pass so far; either is a far more promising primary source for Phillips's treatment of the wife's own role than *The Masculine Mandate*.
