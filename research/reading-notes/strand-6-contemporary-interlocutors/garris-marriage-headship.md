# Zachary Garris — marriage, headship, and submission

**Full citation:** *Masculine Christianity* (2020, independent); *Honor Thy Fathers: Recovering the Anti-Feminist Theology of the Reformers*; editor, *Dabney on Fire: A Theology of Parenting, Education, Feminism, and Government*; ongoing material at knowingscripture.com and teachdiligently.com.

**Strand:** strand-6-contemporary-interlocutors

**Researched on:** 2026-08-06 (via background research agent, WebSearch/WebFetch)

## Who he is

CONFIRMED: Pastor, Bryce Avenue Presbyterian Church (PCA), White Rock, NM. M.Div., Reformed Theological Seminary (Jackson); J.D., Wayne State University Law School. Founder of KnowingScripture.com and TeachDiligently.com.

## What he actually argues

CONFIRMED: Garris holds male headship in marriage is a creation-order reality, not a fall-consequence — Genesis 3:16 "intensifies this rule through sin's effects, not creating it" (per a sympathetic review's paraphrase of his argument). On Eph 5:22-33 / Col 3:18-19 / 1 Pet 3:1-7 directly (knowingscripture.com): "Headship means rule, which explains why passages call for the wife to 'submit'... Submission implies authority and rule." He states it is "not his responsibility to submit, but a wife's to submit to her husband," and that "it is contrary to nature for a wife to direct and set parameters for how her husband can lead" — citing 1 Pet 3:1,6 (Sarah's obedience) as the model.

On 1 Tim 2 / 1 Cor 14:34-35, per the *Between Two Cultures* review: women are barred from "teaching men and exercising authority over men" for two reasons — the creation order and greater deceivability — and Garris takes 1 Cor 14:34-35 to prohibit "all public speech in worship," a harder line than mainstream complementarianism.

On 1 Tim 3:4-5 ("The Husband 'Rules' His Home," knowingscripture.com): Garris argues modern translations (NASB/ESV/NIV/CSB/NET) wrongly render Greek *proistēmi* as "manage" rather than "rule" — "most of the major modern English Bible translations made a huge mistake in substituting the word 'manage' for the husband's 'rule.'" His model: "the husband is *ruler* of the home and the wife *keeper* and *manager*."

Practical emphasis (American Reformer, 2023, "The Leaven of Egalitarianism"): headship must be actually exercised, not merely titular — "If a husband and wife cannot recall a single instance of even a 'tie-breaking vote,' one must question how this qualifies as male headship."

## Exegetical grounds

Creation-order grounding (1 Cor 11, Gen 2) is primary; Gen 3:16 read as intensification, not institution. Notable documented gap: the sympathetic *Between Two Cultures* review of *Masculine Christianity* states Garris "references [Eph 5] in passing here and there, but does no serious exegesis of this passage" — his argument leans on the submission/rule side of Eph 5 and comparatively little on the husband's self-sacrificial-love clause (vv.25-29). Worth checking directly against the primary text before repeating this critique as settled.

## Follow-up pass, 2026-08-08 — the Eph 5:25-29 gap, closer to primary text

The same reviewer (Between Two Cultures) who wrote the "does no serious exegesis of this passage" line also posted a companion 16-page, chapter-by-chapter reading summary of the book (PDF, betweentwocultures.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/masculine-christianity_summary.pdf) — a detailed outline with page citations and direct quotes for each of the book's 12 chapters. This is not the book itself, but it is the closest available approximation short of buying and reading it, and it independently corroborates the review's claim rather than merely repeating it:

- Ch. 7, "Masculine Authority Starts in the Home," §1 "Male Headship in the Home," reduces to one line: "Wives are to submit (Col 3:18-19; 1 Pt. 3:1; Eph. 5:22)" — citing only the submission half of Eph 5:22-33, with no corresponding subsection on vv. 25-29.
- Ch. 12, "Leaving a Manly Legacy," §1 "A Call to Men," includes as one bullet among several: "Love your wife and lead her in the way of godliness" — a single line, not a worked exegetical treatment.
- By contrast, every other major text Garris engages (1 Cor 11:2-16, 1 Tim 2:8-15, 1 Cor 14:34-35, 1 Tim 3:1-7, Titus 1:5-9) gets its own numbered subsection with multi-point argumentation in the outline.

WELL-ATTESTED (upgraded from the prior pass): the claim that Garris's book is comparatively thin on Eph 5:25-29 relative to his treatment of the wife's submission clause is corroborated by two independent pieces of evidence from the same source (the review's explicit statement and the separate chapter outline's structure), not merely asserted once. This is still short of CONFIRMED — the book itself has not been read directly — but it is no longer merely "a reviewer's characterization." Fair framing for a draft: "Garris's chapter structure in *Masculine Christianity* treats the wife's submission (Eph 5:22, Col 3:18-19, 1 Pet 3:1) as its own subsection with sustained argument; the husband's parallel duty to love sacrificially (Eph 5:25-29) appears only in a single practical exhortation ('love your wife and lead her in the way of godliness'), not as a worked exegetical passage — a real asymmetry in the book's own proportions, corroborated by an independent chapter-by-chapter outline, though the book itself has not been directly read by this project."

The same outline also confirms the wife's-side content in the book is thin in the way CLAUDE.md's house rule flags generally: her "positive" material (ch. 5, §§4-5) consists of being "fit" for man, bearing children, and household management (Prov 31:14-24, contrasted via *oikodespoteō* vs. the husband's *proistēmi*) — real but narrower than, e.g., Duncan's "she opens her mouth in wisdom... offers suggestions and advice and counsel, and even correction to her husband" (see the Knight-Duncan-Phillips note). Worth flagging in any draft that engages Garris on the wife's role: don't let this book stand in for the fuller positive material this project owes the wife's side; supplement from elsewhere in the contemporary bibliography.
