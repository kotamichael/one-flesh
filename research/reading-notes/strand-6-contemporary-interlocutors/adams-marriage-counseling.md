## Who he is

CONFIRMED: Presbyterian pastor/seminary professor (1929-2020), founder of the nouthetic/biblical counseling movement; founded CCEF and the Institute for Nouthetic Studies. Author of 100+ books.

## What he actually argues — husband's side (well-covered by this research pass)

Adams reduces headship to direct, actionable counseling directives rather than abstract theological proposition. From *Christian Living in the Home* (P&R, 1972, p.101), chapter "Loving Leadership": "You are to be the head of your home, including your wife, just as Christ is the head of the church... You are the head of your home. If there is little or no love in that home, it is your fault. God holds you responsible to introduce love."

On decisional authority (classic Adams formulation, widely quoted): "Somebody must have the final say... the buck stops not with the wife, but with the husband."

Follow-up should specifically search Adams's counseling material for what he tells *wives* to actually do (not just "submit") — his casework likely addresses a wife's own initiative, competence, and household governance directly, given his generally concrete, directive style.

## Wife's side — follow-up pass (2026-08-08)

**The book has a parallel wife-addressed chapter the first pass missed.** *Christian Living in the Home* (P&R, 1972) is structured as mirror-image chapters: ch. 6, **"A Word to the Wives,"** parallels ch. 7, "Loving Leadership" (to husbands) — CONFIRMED via multiple independent bookseller/reader-review sources reproducing the same chapter list (1. Christ-centered home; 2. Hope and help for your family; 3. Communication comes first; 4. Bible basics about the family; 5. Single persons; 6. **A Word to the Wives**; 7. Loving Leadership; 8. Discipline with dignity; 9. How to live with an unbelieving husband). Adams gave the wife's side its own chapter-length direct address, on par with the husband's — the earlier note's "thin" finding was a research-coverage gap, not a feature of the book's structure.

**What's actually in it** (I could not obtain the primary chapter text directly — the book is gated behind Internet Archive's controlled-lending system, which this pass could not get past — so the following is WELL-ATTESTED via independent reader accounts, not CONFIRMED against the primary page):
- Adams addresses wives personally and directly, in the second person, e.g. (reviewer-quoted exact wording, UNVERIFIED against the primary page but consistently reproduced): "...so that won't let you off the hook; not for one moment," and "Yet by comparison to what Jesus Christ has required of their husbands, Christian Wives have an easy lot" — i.e., Adams's own comparative point is that the husband's charge (Christlike, sacrificial headship) is the heavier one.
- Independent reviewers describe an extended illustration involving bread-baking/a cookbook in this chapter, and one reviewer (Laura McCarter) judged the chapter itself "biblically faithful."

**The "buck stops with the husband" quote is very likely spoken TO wives, not about them.** Gracequotes.org's citation (a secondary compilation, not the primary book — WELL-ATTESTED not CONFIRMED) gives **p. 83** for "Somebody must have the final say... it stops not with the wife, but with the husband," distinct from **p. 101** for the "you are the head of your home... it is your fault" quote. Since ch. 7 ("Loving Leadership," husband-addressed) opens around p. 101, p. 83 falls inside ch. 6 ("A Word to the Wives"). That means this oft-cited line was most plausibly delivered *as part of Adams's case to the wife* for why the final say rests with her husband — not a husband-directed exhortation. **This matters for house rule 2**: if the project cites this quote, it should be framed as "Adams tells wives X" rather than "Adams tells husbands X," pending confirmation against the actual page (still not independently confirmed — flag as `[VERIFY: page 83 context]`).

**"What Is She to Do?"** (*Journal of Biblical Counseling* 10:2, Winter 1990; also hosted at nouthetic.org) — CONFIRMED to exist and address a wife specifically. Its subject is a wife in a difficult/abusive marriage. Content actually surfaced by this pass (CONFIRMED as currently appearing on the nouthetic.org page, via direct fetch):
- She should not "fear their threat, nor be upset" (citing 1 Pet 3:14).
- She should continue "fulfilling the marriage vow, which included the promise 'for better or for worse.'"
- "A soft answer turns away wrath" (Prov 15:1) — offered as counsel toward de-escalating speech.
- In acute danger, "it would not be wrong for the wife to put one night's clothing in a bag and head with her children to a neighbor's house" — explicitly framed by Adams as *not* separation, a temporary safety measure only.
- If the husband is a Christian, she is directed to appeal to church elders for intervention.
- Adams states he finds "no biblical warrant" for divorce or permanent separation on grounds of abuse.

  This is genuinely concrete, directive "what to do" content in Adams's characteristic nouthetic style, applied to a hard pastoral case. Per the project's standing rule not to import outside critique of Adams's counseling framework as a standing concern, this note reports his actual content and citation rather than adjudicating the abuse-counseling debate; if the project engages that debate on its merits at some point, it should do so as its own dedicated, primary-source exegetical/pastoral question (does the biblical case Adams makes from 1 Pet 3 and the marriage vow actually support his conclusions), not by importing a secondhand critique.

## Method note

His nouthetic method (per *Competent to Counsel*) treats Scripture as giving directly actionable commands: identify the counselee's sin against the biblical role-pattern, ask "What does God's Word command?", prescribe concrete behavioral steps. This method should apply just as concretely to a wife's own duties as to a husband's — the thinness above is a research gap, not a feature of Adams's method.
