# Shawn Mathis, and additional contemporary confessional-Presbyterian voices

**Strand:** strand-6-contemporary-interlocutors

**Researched on:** 2026-08-06/07 (via background research agents, WebSearch/WebFetch)

## Shawn Mathis

CONFIRMED: Rev. Shawn C. Mathis — OPC (Orthodox Presbyterian Church), pastor of Providence OPC, Denver, CO. Site: pastormathis.com. His substantial published output (*Uniting Church and Family*; *Is the Church Pro-Gay?*; an 8-part series on the family-integrated-church movement) is on family-integrated-church debates and cultural/apologetic engagement with the gay-marriage question — not, historically, on marriage-roles doctrine as such.

**A genuinely on-topic, very recent (2026) find, CONFIRMED:** three 2026 blog posts responding to a live OPC controversy (see the Garris note): "Initial Thoughts on an Embarrassing Essay" (March 4), "A Simple Response to 'In Defense of Union, Not Patriarchy'" (March 12), and "Genesis, Patriarchy & 'In Defense of Union, Not Patriarchy'" (March 19), all at pastormathis.com. In the third, responding to OPC minister Aaron Mize's *Ordained Servant* article, Mathis argues for creation-order male headship: the *order* of creation is theologically meaningful; Adam's formation, his naming of the animals and of Eve, establish a "dominion pattern" prior to the fall; Eve's formation from Adam's side signals a subordinate-helper role; and he appeals to the Adam/Christ headship typology (1 Cor 15:47-48, Rom 5) to argue male headship is "intrinsic to mankind" and creational, not merely a fallen-world corruption (contra Mize's claim that patriarchy is "a feature of the fallen world Christ overturns").

His marriage-roles-specific writing is thin except for this live, dated, exegetically-argued 2026 skirmish — worth citing precisely because it's contemporaneous and directly exegetical.

## Additional confessional-Presbyterian voices identified (not originally in the bibliography)

**C.R. Wiley** (PCA, Presbyterian Church of Manchester, CT) — CONFIRMED. *Man of the House* (Front Porch Republic, 2017); *The Household and the War for the Cosmos* (Canon Press, 2019), which per secondary description explicitly addresses "biblical commands for a husband to love his wife and a wife to submit to her husband" within a broader covenantal-household/"little commonwealth" framework — a useful complement to Garris/Adams, since Wiley's angle is household-economics/social-order rather than marriage counseling or exegetical polemic.

**Douglas Wilson** (CREC, Christ Church, Moscow, ID — not a mainline confessional-Presbyterian body, but Reformed/covenantal and frequently in intramural conversation with PCA/OPC circles) — CONFIRMED. *Reforming Marriage* (1995) treats headship as ontological/creational rather than a mere imperative — the husband "finds himself in a position of inescapable leadership and cannot successfully refuse to lead" — and distinguishes the paired commands precisely: husbands commanded to *love* sacrificially, wives commanded to *respect*. *For a Glory and a Covering* (2012) is a fuller practical-theology treatment. Note: Wilson is considerably more polarizing within confessional Presbyterian circles than Garris or Wiley (controversies over slavery historiography) — handle carefully, per house rules on charity and accurate representation.

**Kevin DeYoung** (PCA, Christ Covenant Church; Moderator, PCA 52nd General Assembly, 2025) — CONFIRMED, but flagged as popular-level rather than a heavyweight academic treatment. *Men and Women in the Church* (Crossway, 2021) works through 1 Cor 11, Eph 5:22-33, 1 Tim 2:8-15; direct quote: "The wife submits as to the Lord, and the husband loves as Christ to the church." A more moderate "mere complementarian" position by comparison with Garris/Wilson/Wiley, useful for showing internal PCA range — but a dedicated research pass on heavier academic Presbyterian complementarian scholarship (George W. Knight III, J. Ligon Duncan, Richard D. Phillips) is underway separately, per the project owner's request for more scholarly depth than DeYoung provides alone.

**Rachel Green Miller** — flagged but NOT deeply verified in this pass. *Beyond Authority and Submission* (P&R, 2019), reported as a Reformed (OPC-adjacent) critic of the "patriarchy movement" strand Garris/Wilson represent. Worth a dedicated follow-up if the project wants an internal Reformed critic's perspective — this would materially help the project's stated non-goal of avoiding "hard patriarchy" overreach.
