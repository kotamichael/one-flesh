## Shawn Mathis — now read directly, not summarized

CONFIRMED: Rev. Shawn C. Mathis — OPC, pastor of Providence OPC, Denver, CO. His three March 2026 posts (pastormathis.com) were fetched and read in full.

**Post 1, "Initial Thoughts on an Embarrassing Essay" (March 4):** sharply critical of Mize's essay's quality — "a rapid-firing of buzzwords, with undefined terms, hidden assumptions and hasty conclusions." Names what he thinks Mize omits: "nothing about Paul's argument based on the creational order of Adam and Eve nor Peter's commendation of Sarah in calling Abraham 'Lord.'" Calls Mize's typological reading of Gen 3:16 (as being about Christ/church rather than describing male/female relations) "torturous reasoning." Frames this as part of a trajectory from prior "crypto-feminism" to overt feminism in the OPC — his own inference about institutional direction, not a report of settled OPC doctrine; present it as his opinion if cited.

**Post 2, "A Simple Response..." (March 12):** central complaint is that 1 Tim 2:12-14 is "strikingly missing from Mize's article" — Mathis argues this text grounds patriarchy in creation (Adam's prior formation, Eve's deception), not the Fall. Argues κεφαλή covers both organic and governing senses, so Mize's organic/hierarchical distinction "doesn't hold weight on lexical grounds alone." Stays in 1 Tim 2/1 Cor 11; doesn't engage Eph 5 or 1 Pet 3 directly in this post.

**Post 3, "Genesis, Patriarchy..." (March 19) — the fullest exegetical piece:** "The order of things is meaningful, depending upon the nature of the case and the circumstances." "Adam was formed of the earth and Eve was not is not a coincidence." On Eve's *ezer* role: she "was created from Adam's side to be a 'helpmate'... her earthly telos is to be his aid" — **this is Mathis's own paraphrase/assertion, not a derived exegetical argument about *ezer kenegdo*; don't treat it as more rigorous than it is.** His clearest typological point: "Paul's long discourse contrasting the first and Second Adam only makes sense if the Genesis recording of the manner of man's creation is significant... Adam and Christ are contrasted, not Eve and Christ... presenting Adam as superior to Eve, representing the human race as its head."

**A caution worth flagging on Mathis's own rhetoric, not just Mize's**: responding to Mize's point that *'ezer* "is most often used of God himself as Israel's helper... [and] conveys strength and partnership, not subordination," Mathis calls the comparison "blasphemous." That's a strong charge against what is actually a standard, common move in the complementarian/egalitarian *'ezer* debate — this looks like the same "confident overstatement" this project's house rules warn against, just occurring on Mathis's side of an exchange rather than a critic's. This project's own exegetical lane should handle *'ezer* on its semantic merits, not adopt Mathis's rhetorical framing.

**Wife's-side finding, stated plainly**: across all three posts, Mathis never offers a positive, independent account of the wife's own competence, dignity, or calling beyond being formed "to be his aid." If this project cites Mathis on headship, don't lean on him for the wife's-side material — his silence here should be noted, not papered over.

## Aaron Mize — represented on his own terms, not only through Mathis's paraphrase

**Important sourcing complication**: Mize's original *Ordained Servant* article was pulled from opc.org after publication (confirmed via the OPC's own April 2026 editor's letter, opc.org/os.html?article_id=1250) and no Wayback Machine snapshot exists. Every Mize quote below is reproduced secondhand by his critics (cross-checked across two independent, mutually-hostile-to-Mize sources to raise confidence) — this is WELL-ATTESTED, not CONFIRMED. **Try to obtain a saved/cached copy of the original before treating any Mize quote as fully locked down.** Notably, the editor's own letter discloses the published article itself had a real sourcing error — quotation marks were "inadvertently add[ed] to a passage summarizing Geerhardus Vos's work" that wasn't actually a Vos quotation — a real-world instance of exactly the paraphrase/quotation conflation this project's own house rules exist to prevent.

Mize's actual argument, on its own terms rather than Mathis's characterization of it: he distinguishes Christ's *kingship* (a rule Mize associates with fallen/political categories) from Christ's *headship* (a union/organic category modeled on body-and-head unity, Eph 5 read through 1 Cor 12's body-of-Christ language), arguing Eph 5 borrows the second category for marriage, not the first: "Christ's headship is organic and mystical, not hierarchical or official"; "Christ's mediatorial rule in the church is based in his Davidic kingship, not conjugal headship." **This is a specific, arguable reading of what "head" means in Eph 5 — represent it as that, not flatten it to "Mize denies wives submit."** His central thesis: "Scripture never presents patriarchy as the created or redeemed norm. It is a feature of the fallen world Christ overturns, not a structure he institutes." On *'ezer*, a standard move in the wider complementarian/egalitarian debate, not an outlier invented for this essay: "[the word] is most often used of God himself as Israel's helper… It conveys strength and partnership, not subordination." His sharpest pastoral-practical claim, worth this project's pastoral-practical lane taking seriously on its own terms even where the exegetical conclusion differs: "If the husband is divinely entitled to his wife's obedience, then there is either no practicable definition of abuse, or it is non-existent altogether."

What Mize says positively about the wife's role: **not found in any reproduced material from either side** — UNVERIFIED, don't assume either way. Given his argument is about de-emphasizing hierarchy in favor of mutuality categories, he may have more to say about non-hierarchical wifely/husbandly roles than survives in these hostile secondhand summaries — flag as an open question pending the primary text.

**Tension, stated as unresolved, not adjudicated here**: Garris and Mathis both read Mize as denying creational, hierarchical headship; Mize's own "organic and mystical" language suggests he sees himself as *relocating* headship into a union-category, not eliminating male headship as a concept. Whether Mize's view amounts in practice to affirming or denying any real created asymmetry is exactly the kind of question this project shouldn't resolve from Mathis's paraphrase alone.

## Rachel Green Miller, *Beyond Authority and Submission* (P&R, 2019) — now fully researched

CONFIRMED: Miller is an **OPC member** (Houston, TX; B.A. History, Texas A&M — a researcher/blogger, not a minister or academic theologian; the earlier "OPC-adjacent" flag should be tightened to this).

WELL-ATTESTED (near-verbatim across 4+ independent reviews — OPC, CBMW, Mere Liberty, Torrey Gazette): her thesis is that conservative churches have let "authority and submission" become the single controlling lens for all male-female relationships, causing "considerable damage to relationships between men and women in the home, church, and in our societies"; her constructive alternative widens the lens to "unity, interdependence, and service." She critiques by name **John Piper, Wayne Grudem, Mary Kassian, Dorothy Patterson, and Douglas Wilson** — mainstream CBMW-associated complementarianism as much as or more than the harder Garris/Wilson-style "patriarchy movement." She specifically targets the Eternal Subordination of the Son argument (the same Knight-lineage argument this project's own Knight note flags as a hazard) and Susan Foh's reading of Gen 3:16 as a hostile bid for control.

**A genuine, worth-preserving intramural tension**: Garris himself reviewed Miller's book and concluded, CONFIRMED direct quote: "At root, Rachel Green Miller is an egalitarian" — despite her own profession of male headship and male-only ordination — faulting her for not showing complementarians actually *derived* their views from pagan sources (only that parallels exist), under-engaging 1 Tim 2:8-15 and 1 Cor 14:34-35 directly, and "equat[ing] femininity with merely being a woman." Against this, a Mere Liberty review (Kerry Baldwin) calls the book "faithful to historic Reformed teaching on Scripture while simultaneously clearing away so much false teaching." The OPC's own review (Michael Chartowich, an OPC ruling elder) lands in the middle: "we would do well to listen to our sister and take to heart the concerns she raises" while noting "questionable interpretation of some key texts (and her neglect of others)" — probably the fairest single secondary source available. **Present this as unresolved disagreement between two named contemporary Reformed voices, sourced on both sides, per house rule 5 — not adjudicated by this project without its own independent exegetical work.**

**A specific, checkable accuracy concern, not yet independently verified**: a CBMW review (Steven Wedgeworth) documents citation errors it calls "frequently egregiously inaccurate" — e.g., claiming Miller misrepresents Wilson (*Reforming Marriage*, p.164) as saying men should control finances "because women will spend too much," when the cited page allegedly discusses both spouses' potential overspending; a similar claim re: Voddie Baucham on childcare. **UNVERIFIED — before this project repeats any Miller characterization of Wilson or Baucham, independently check the underlying primary-source page.**

Bearing on this project's own house rule 8 (wife's-side balance): Miller's whole project is itself a corrective in exactly this direction — she is a genuinely useful source *for* that discipline, even where the project may end up disagreeing with how far she takes the critique.
