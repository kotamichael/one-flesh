# Seceder & Covenanter sources on marriage/domestic relations

**Strand:** strand-4-seceder-covenanter

**Researched on:** 2026-08-06 (via background research agent, WebSearch/WebFetch — Log College Press, PRDL, Internet Archive, Blue Letter Bible, Monergism, TrueCovenanter.com)

**Method note carried over from the research pass:** several primary texts exist only as scanned PDFs/DjVu files too long for fetch tooling to return in full, capping how much verbatim text could be pulled even once the right document and page were located. Flagged explicitly below rather than guessed at.

## Adam Gib (1714-1788)

UNVERIFIED for marriage content; CONFIRMED bibliography only. Works: *Kaina kai Palaia: Sacred Contemplations, in Three Parts* (Edinburgh, 1786); *A Display of the Fraudulent and Gross Abuses Committed upon the Secession-Testimony* (1780); *A Warning Against Countenancing the Ministrations of George Whitefield* (1742). *Sacred Contemplations* Part 1 is characterized as "A View of the Covenant of Works" — covenant-of-works meditation, not household ethics. No excerpt or citation tying any part of this work to Eph 5/Col 3/1 Pet 3/domestic duties was located. No dedicated Log College Press author page for Gib exists (that site focuses on American Presbyterianism). Plausible genuine dead end, not a disproof — the content may sit in an untranscribed, unindexed part of the source.

## John Brown of Haddington (1722-1787)

Mixed result, with an important false lead caught and excluded: Blue Letter Bible hosts an Ephesians commentary attributed to "John Brown" that initial searches suggested was Brown of Haddington's Self-Interpreting Bible notes on Eph 5:18-33 — verified instead that this is a **contemporary Calvary Chapel pastor** ("Senior Pastor of Calvary Chapel Central Phoenix... since 1986"), not the 18th-century divine. **Discard this source entirely if it resurfaces** — citing it as Brown of Haddington would be a real error.

Confirmed bibliography (title-level): *The Self-Interpreting Bible* (1778) — running annotations on every chapter almost certainly cover Eph 5/Col 3/1 Pet 3, but the actual annotation text was not retrievable in this pass (no clean full-text/searchable edition found beyond the false-lead site). *A Dictionary of the Holy Bible* (1769). *The Systematic Theology of John Brown of Haddington* (posthumous, based on student lecture notes, seven "Books") — two PDF fetch attempts returned only front matter; genre suggests a Decalogue-based marriage treatment under the seventh commandment is plausible but **not confirmed**. No work titled "The Christian Journal" was located under his name — could not confirm this as a real title.

**Conclusion:** strong prior probability of relevant content given the genre, but not confirmed by direct text. UNVERIFIED — flag for a follow-up pass with better tooling access (a proper scanned-book reader, not fetch-tool summarization).

## William Symington (1795-1862)

UNVERIFIED — and this reads as a genuine negative finding, not a research gap. Confirmed works (*Messiah the Prince*, *On the Atonement*, *On the Intercession of Jesus Christ*) are uniformly Christological/soteriological (kingship, atonement, intercession) — his known scholarly register as RP professor of theology. No sermon, lecture, tract, or minor work on marriage, domestic relations, or household duties surfaced under any search combination. As anticipated going in, Symington's corpus appears to simply not address this topic.

## RPCNA Testimony — "Reformation Principles Exhibited" — the strongest positive lead in this strand

WELL-ATTESTED at the structural level; CONFIRMED that a dedicated marriage chapter exists and its exact location; full text NOT yet retrieved (a tooling limitation, not an absence).

Full citation: *Reformation Principles Exhibited: Being the Declaration and Testimony of the Reformed Presbyterian Church in North America*, RPCNA General Synod, 6th edition (1871), Internet Archive identifier `reformationpr00refo`.

CONFIRMED via the document's own table of contents: **Chapter XXVIII — "Of Marriage"** (beginning p.108), sitting between Chapter XXVII "Of Oaths and Covenants" and Chapter XXIX "Of Civil Government" — a placement logic that parallels WCF 24's own position near civil-government-adjacent material. This confirms the RPCNA Testimony treats marriage as a distinct covenantal/social-order topic worth its own chapter, structurally continuous with the Westminster Confession's own approach.

**What could not be confirmed:** the actual text of Chapter XXVIII. The DjVu full-text file was too large for the fetch tool, which truncated before reaching p.108 (stopping around Ch. XVI, "Of Repentance"). A second, earlier 1807 printing (Hopkins and Seymour, Columbia University collection, `bub_gb_WB9MAAAAYAAJ`) and a Google Books snippet search both failed to yield extractable text. TrueCovenanter.com's RPE page hosts a different portion of the Testimony (historical-narrative "Books," not the doctrinal chapters).

Given the Testimony's general pattern (each chapter restates and expands a corresponding WCF chapter with proof-texts annexed), Chapter XXVIII "Of Marriage" almost certainly follows WCF 24-style propositions with its own proof-texts — but this is an informed inference, not a verified fact.

**Recommended follow-up (not completed):** direct archive.org page-image access (not fetch-tool-mediated) to pull pp.108-112 of `reformationpr00refo` — a productive, citable primary source once retrieved, since its existence and location are now confirmed.

## David Steele / Renwick catechisms — a disambiguation, not a finding

David Steele (1803-1887, "Steelite" RP Covenanter minister) — no household/marital-duties writings surfaced, only biography. **Important disambiguation**: search results repeatedly surfaced Richard Steele (1629-1692), an English Puritan (not Scottish Covenanter), author of *The Duties of Husbands and Wives* (1672, apuritansmind.com) — a real, well-known Puritan household-duties work, but belonging to Strand 2 (English Puritan genre), not this strand — see `strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan/household-duty-writers-overview.md`, which also flags that apuritansmind.com mislabels this Richard Steele with the dates of the unrelated Whig essayist Sir Richard Steele (1672-1729). Three different "Steele"s in total across this project's sources — do not conflate any of them. No James Renwick-tradition Covenanter catechism with marriage/household content was located either.

## A contemporary bridge worth pursuing: George Scipione

Not yet researched — flagged by the project owner as a possible RPCNA-affiliated contemporary figure. George C. Scipione was RPCNA-ordained and worked within the nouthetic/biblical-counseling tradition (associated with NANC and CCEF, taught at Westminster Theological Seminary and Reformed Presbyterian Theological Seminary) — if his marriage-counseling material can be located, he would be a genuine bridge between this strand's institutional home (RPCNA) and Strand 6's practical-counseling material (alongside Jay Adams). This belongs with Strand 6 by content (contemporary, counseling-oriented) even though his ordination ties him to this strand's tradition — flagged here for cross-reference, full research pass still needed.

## Bearing on the project

The Scottish Secession/Covenanter tradition appears to have genuinely under-produced a dedicated "household duties" literature compared to the English Puritan genre (Gouge, Baxter, R. Steele) — consistent with its own self-understanding as primarily a testimony/covenant-continuity/ecclesiology-focused movement. The one substantive, confirmed positive lead — RPCNA Testimony Ch. XXVIII — is a real and worthwhile target for follow-up, not a dead end.
