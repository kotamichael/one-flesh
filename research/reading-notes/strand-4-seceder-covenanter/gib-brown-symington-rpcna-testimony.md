## John Brown of Haddington (1722-1787) — CONFIRMED on all three target passages

Upgraded from UNVERIFIED. Full citation: *The New Self-Interpreting Bible*, Vol. 4 (New Testament epistles), Internet Archive `TheNewSelf-interpretingBibleVol.4`. **Edition caveat**: this specific scan is explicitly labeled "a revised edition" of Brown's 1778 original with later editors' footnotes marked "—C."/"—P." — the quotations below are from the unmarked running paraphrase (Brown's own exposition, carried into this printing), but are **not confirmed verbatim-identical to the 1778 first edition**. Spelling here is modernized, not 1778 orthography — check an actual 1778 printing before treating exact wording as settled.

**Ephesians 5:22-33**: chapter-argument heading states the text "descendeth to the particular [conjugal] duties, how wives ought to obey their husbands... and husbands ought to love their wives, even as Christ doth his church." On v.25 (husband): "to procure the kind submission of your wives, see that you husbands never tyrannize over them, or be passionate or bitter against them." On v.33 (wife, positive statement of her own duty, not merely absence of resistance): "every wife ought, with a conjugal love, to pay all becoming respect, honour, and obedience to her husband." (v.22's paraphrase has a genuine, unrecoverable OCR/scan gap in this copy.)

**Colossians 3:18-19** (both verses paraphrased as one continuous sentence — a substantive finding): "see that ye Christian wives behave in a meek and submissive manner to your lawful husbands, **as far as consists with your superior relation and obedience to Christ**. And ye, Christian husbands, treat your wives with all possible tenderness, kindness and affection... and never exercise a severe and arbitrary lordship over them." **Brown explicitly bounds wifely submission by a prior, superior obligation to Christ** — worth flagging to the systematic-theologian and pastoral-practical lanes as a specific 18th-century data point on how far household submission was understood to extend.

**1 Peter 3:1-7**: wife's side (vv.1-2, positive framing, not just "her submission"): "do you Christian wives, behave with the most obliging and affectionate submission to your husbands **in all things lawful**" — the same bounding qualifier as the Colossians passage. Husband's side (v.7, opening clause lost to scan gap, recoverable continuation): "providing honourably for [wives], putting due confidence in them, and sympathizing with and helping them under their manifold infirmities, that... they may be honoured and encouraged, as heirs of eternal happiness, equally with yourselves."

**Bearing on the project**: this satisfies the project's wife's-side discipline directly from the primary text — Brown gives the wife's "conjugal love" and "becoming respect, honour" as a positive duty she owes, not merely an absence of resistance, on the same terms as the husband's duty. The repeated "as far as consists with... obedience to Christ" / "in all things lawful" qualifier on wifely submission, appearing independently in both his Colossians and 1 Peter comments, is a genuine and citable historical data point for how this tradition bounded the doctrine.

## RPCNA Testimony — "Reformation Principles Exhibited," Chapter XXVIII "Of Marriage" — CONFIRMED, full text retrieved

Full citation: *Reformation Principles Exhibited: Being the Declaration and Testimony of the Reformed Presbyterian Church in North America*, RPCNA General Synod (New York: Lee, Shepard and Dillingham, 1871), pp.108-112. Internet Archive `reformationpr00refo`. (The item's own metadata doesn't separately state an edition number — "6th ed." from the first pass's TOC-based finding is carried over, not independently reconfirmed here.)

**Structure**: seven numbered propositions with annexed Scripture proof-texts — the same genre as the Confession/Catechisms — followed by a list of condemned errors.

- §1: "Marriage is an ordinance of God, instituted in the law of nature, and recognized in Divine Revelation" (Matt 19:4-5).
- §2 (purposes, closely parallel to WCF 24.2): "Marriage was ordained for the mutual help of husband and wife — the population of the world... and the prevention of uncleanness" (Gen 2:18, Gen 1:28, 1 Cor 7:2,14, Heb 13:4).
- §3 (duties, framed symmetrically in the propositional text itself): "the parties... are bound also to cultivate mutual love, bear with, and cover each other's infirmities, keep the marriage oath inviolate... and carefully avoid all mutual recrimination."
- §4-7: consanguinity/affinity prohibitions (Lev 18); the requirement of "duly qualified ecclesiastical or civil officers" for celebration; the civil magistrate's regulatory role; divorce restricted to adultery and "such wilful desertion as admits of no civil remedy" (Matt 19:3-9, 1 Cor 7:15). Condemned errors: polygamy, mandatory clerical celibacy, and any broader ground for divorce.

**Where headship/submission actually surfaces — exactly the WLC 123-133 pattern**: the Testimony's own numbered propositions never differentiate husband's and wife's duties by role — §3's language is entirely symmetrical. **The asymmetry appears only in the annexed proof-texts**: Col 3:19 and 1 Pet 3:1-4 ("the ornament of a meek and quiet spirit") are cited for §3, doing the role-differentiating work the propositional text itself doesn't state outright. This is the identical structural move CLAUDE.md already documents for the Westminster Standards — confirms it's not a Westminster-specific quirk but a broader confessional convention (state mutual duties plainly, let the proof-texts carry the role-specific content).

**A genuinely notable negative finding, confirmed by direct search of the full chapter text: Chapter XXVIII never cites Ephesians 5 anywhere.** The RPCNA Testimony's marriage doctrine is built on Gen 1-2 and 1 Corinthians 7's mutuality language plus Col 3/1 Pet 3 for role content — not on the Eph 5 head/body/Christ-and-church typology this project treats as central, and not on the household-duty tradition's characteristic Ephesians-centered exposition (contrast Gouge's 133-section Ephesians exposition in Strand 2). **This is a real, citable point of emphasis-difference within the project's historical synthesis**: Strand 4's confessional marriage doctrine is not Ephesians-shaped the way the Puritan household-duty tradition's is.

## George Scipione (1946-2020) — researched, with an important correction to the project's working assumption

**Correction, stated directly: Scipione was NOT RPCNA-ordained.** He was **ordained in the Orthodox Presbyterian Church (OPC)** — installed May 31, 1974, as pastor of Calvary Community Church (OPC), Phillipsburg, NJ, serving until 1982 (CONFIRMED, OPC's own historical record). His RPCNA connection is institutional, not a matter of his own ordination: 1982-2006, Director of the Institute for Biblical Counseling and Discipleship (San Diego), adjunct/associate professor of practical theology at **Westminster Seminary California** (not the original Philadelphia seminary — correct this if it appears elsewhere); 2008-2018, Director of the newly-established Biblical Counseling Institute at **Reformed Presbyterian Theological Seminary (RPTS)**, Pittsburgh — RPCNA's own seminary. This RPTS directorship is the real, confirmed RPCNA-institutional bridge, just not via ordination. Doctorate, Whitefield Theological Seminary; long NANC/ACBC association alongside Jay Adams. Died January 22, 2020.

**His one dedicated marriage/family book**: *The Battle for the Biblical Family* (Crown & Covenant Publications — RPCNA's own publishing house — 2018, 284pp). Joel Beeke's cover endorsement calls it "a concise manual to understand the Creator's plan for family life." **UNVERIFIED for actual headship/submission content** — no table of contents, excerpt, or preview was accessible through any tool tried (Google Books, Goodreads, publisher page all lack a viewable TOC). A sermon, "Sanctified Sexuality in Marriage" (SermonAudio, speaker confirmed as Scipione), is also correctly attributed but its audio transcript wasn't accessible. **`[VERIFY: obtain a copy of *The Battle for the Biblical Family* directly before citing Scipione on headship/submission substance]`** — his identity, credentials, and this book's existence are confirmed; its content is not.

## Bearing on the project

The Scottish Secession/Covenanter tradition's confessional marriage doctrine (the RPCNA Testimony) now has real, confirmed positive content — and shows the same "mutual duties stated plainly, role content carried by proof-texts" structure as the Westminster Standards, plus a genuinely distinctive non-reliance on Ephesians 5. Brown of Haddington now supplies confirmed, positive wife-side content with a notable "bounded submission" qualifier repeated across two separate passages. Scipione is confirmed as a real RPCNA-institutional bridge to contemporary biblical counseling (alongside Jay Adams), but his own substantive position on marriage roles remains unverified pending direct access to his book.
