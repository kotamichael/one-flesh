# Individual Westminster Divines, and the two Directories, on marriage roles

**Strand:** strand-3-westminster-divines

**Researched on:** 2026-08-06/07 (background research agent; primary text of the Directory for Family Worship independently re-verified 2026-08-07 against the full raw text, not just a secondary summary)

**Context:** WCF 24 and WLC 123-133 (with their proof-texts) are already verified and documented in `.claude/agents/confessional-reviewer.md` and `CLAUDE.md`. This note covers what remained: individual seated Divines' own writings, and the two Directories.

## The single most significant new finding: the 1645 marriage vow's asymmetry

CONFIRMED, verbatim, from the Directory for the Publick Worship of God (1645), "The Solemnization of Marriage":

> Husband's vow: "I N. do take thee N. to be my married wife, and do, in the presence of God, and before this congregation, promise and covenant to be a loving and faithful husband unto thee, until God shall separate us by death."

> Wife's vow: "I N. do take thee N. to be my married husband, and I do, in the presence of God, and before this congregation, promise and covenant to be a loving, faithful, **and obedient** wife unto thee, until God shall separate us by death."

The wife's vow alone adds "obedient." This is a load-bearing, Assembly-sanctioned textual data point independent of the WLC 127/129 proof-texts — worth citing alongside them in the manifesto's confessional anchor.

## Directory for Family Worship (1647) — re-checked against the full primary text directly, not a summary

The project owner asked whether this document specifies a husband-first, wife-in-his-absence succession for household worship leadership, with fitness for reading/teaching as the criterion. I pulled the full raw text (not an AI-summarized fetch) to check. The relevant sections:

> §III: "in every family where there is any that can read, the holy scriptures should be read ordinarily to the family... In all which **the master of the family** is to have the chief hand; and any member of the family may propone a question or doubt for resolution."

> §IV: "seeing the ordinary performance of all the parts of family-worship belongeth properly to **the head of the family**, the minister is to stir up such as are lazy... And in other families, where **the head of the family is unfit**, **that another, constantly residing in the family, approved by the minister and session, may be employed** in that service."

**Finding:** the document does establish a default office-holder ("the head/master of the family") and a fallback provision when that person is unfit — but the text itself is gender-neutral at exactly the point that would need to specify a wife: "another... may be employed," not "his wife" or "the mother of the family." It also doesn't state fitness-for-reading as an explicit succession criterion — that's a synthesis reading §III's "any that can read" into §IV's succession clause, which the text doesn't actually do itself. The document presupposes a patriarchal household order (17th-century "master/head of family" language assumes a male householder as the default case) without textually arguing for or explicitly gendering the succession rule.

**Follow-up pass, 2026-08-08 — the preface question, closed as a negative finding.** Checked two further independent transcriptions (truecovenanter.com; reformedbooksonline.com's transcription of the related but textually distinct Adopting Act, Church of Scotland General Assembly, 24 Aug 1647, Sess. 10). No extended theological "reasons annexed" preamble exists anywhere — the Directory's own preface is the same two sentences already found. The Adopting Act, which some kirk-session printings bound alongside the Directory (plausibly the source of any "longer preamble" memory), is not a theological justification at all — it's an enforcement mechanism specifying escalating discipline for neglect (private admonition → session reproof → suspension from the Lord's Supper), consistently using masculine pronouns for the disciplined office-holder ("the head of the family... he is to be... reproved... let him be... suspended") — incidental confirmation of the assumed-male-householder default even though the succession clause itself stays gender-neutral. WELL-ATTESTED across five independent transcription sources now (not CONFIRMED against an original 1647 page-image scan) — recommend treating this as closed rather than pursuing further.

## William Gouge's Assembly status

CONFIRMED: Gouge was a seated member of the Westminster Assembly (named by the 12 June 1643 ordinance), attended with unusual regularity into his late 60s, chaired a drafting committee in 1644, and often occupied the moderator's chair in Twisse's absence. He died in December 1653, well after the Confession's main drafting period. *Of Domesticall Duties* (1622) predates the Assembly by two decades — Gouge-the-seated-Divine and *Of Domesticall Duties*-the-1622-treatise are both true and both worth citing, but keep the dates straight.

## The succession clause — found, exactly where predicted: Gouge, not the Directory

CONFIRMED, 2026-08-08 follow-up. The clause lives in Gouge's *Of Domesticall Duties* (1622), Treatise II ("Of Husband and Wife"), Part II, §40, "Of husbands and wives' joint care in governing the family" (EEBO-TCP text A68107, old-spelling, cross-checked against a modernized-spelling reprint):

> "as the man especially is to performe the very actions of prayer, reading the word, catechizing... so the wife may be a great helpe... yea, in praying, reading, teaching, and performing like exercises her selfe, so farre as she is able, when her husband is absent, or negligent and carelesse, and will not himselfe doe them; or it may be, is not able to doe them: or if she perform them not her selfe, in getting some other to performe them."

This is a genuine **three-tier** succession, more granular than a simple husband→wife binary: (1) default — the husband performs prayer, reading, catechizing as head; (2) first fallback — **the wife herself**, triggered by three distinct named conditions (husband absent, negligent/unwilling, or unable), qualified by an explicit fitness criterion, "so farre as she is able"; (3) second fallback — "some other," if she doesn't perform them herself. This confirms the original hypothesis and supersedes any impulse to read this logic into the Directory's gender-neutral clause — two different documents, twenty-five years apart, only Gouge's is wife-named and fitness-qualified.

Section 40 as a whole is also Gouge's fullest statement of "joint government": he calls husband and wife "joint-governours" of the family, cites Abigail's evident hand in governing Nabal's house, and notes the wife may be "a great help in putting her husband in mind" of these duties even under ordinary (non-absent) circumstances — real wife's-side positive content in its own right, not merely an emergency-succession footnote; see the parallel note in `strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan/household-duty-writers-overview.md`.

## Rutherford, Gillespie, Burgess, Vines — thin or absent, as expected

- **Samuel Rutherford**: no dedicated marriage-roles writing found; his *Letters* use marriage as a spiritual metaphor for Christ/the church, not domestic-ethics instruction. UNVERIFIED/absent.
- **George Gillespie**: no marriage-roles writing found; his *Notes of Debates* record the WCF 24 floor debates on divorce grounds — Confession-drafting process, not his own doctrinal position on roles. UNVERIFIED/absent.
- **Anthony Burgess**, **Richard Vines**: no marriage-roles content located. UNVERIFIED/absent.
- **Herbert Palmer**: CONFIRMED, both halves, 2026-08-08 follow-up (fullest available edition checked: *Memorials of Godliness and Christianity*, 1670, 10th ed., EEBO-TCP/Bodleian OTA, Wing P240). **Negative finding on doctrinal content, now confirmed rather than inferred**: none of the book's three parts (personal piety/minister's calling; Christian paradoxes and watchfulness; daily/Sabbath conduct rules) contains a household-government or husband/wife-duties section — checked by full-text search for "family," "household," "husband," "wife" throughout; every hit is incidental vocabulary or belongs to the prefixed biography, not Palmer's own doctrinal prose. **The practice claim is upgraded to CONFIRMED**: the book's own prefixed "Brief Account of the Life of Mr. Herbert Palmer" states, in language nearly identical to Brook's later account (likely Brook's actual source): "His Domestick or Family Government was as pious, as his Ecclesiastick or Church care was painful... He read the Scriptures, and prayed, twice every day in his family, Catechised twice every week." Keep the distinction explicit: this documents what Palmer *did*, not what he *taught* about marriage roles — it's a biographical memorial by another hand, not his own authorial voice.
