## What this project is

**Constructive, not polemical.** This project reconstructs and states clearly the Reformed Presbyterian doctrine of marriage and marriage roles — the exegetical, historical, and systematic case for the God-given order of headship and submission within marriage, and the mutual duties of husband and wife — so that Christian men and women can see that order plainly, from Scripture read on its own terms and confirmed by the best of the Reformed exegetical tradition, and be equipped to actually live it out. It is a **positive vision** project: reconstructing a whole, coherent account first, before (and in service of) any engagement with rival readings.

**Not a triangulation between modern labels.** Clarified 2026-08-14: this project does not position itself as "complementarianism, avoiding the twin errors of egalitarianism and hard patriarchy." All three of those modern labels are judged by the same standard this project holds itself to — exegetical grounding, rootedness in the historical/confessional strands, and pastoral seriousness — and none of them gets treated as a safe default just for being the modern middle option. The method is ground-up reconstruction from Scripture, the five historical strands, and the confessional standards; that this keeps converging with what writers like Gouge and Adams already held is a finding of that process, not a starting assumption being defended. See the house rule below on giving both roles full, symmetrical treatment — not "wife gets protective/positive framing, husband gets warned against abuse of his."

The vehicle for that reconstruction is close exegesis of the full canonical witness (Genesis 1–3; 1 Cor 11:3-16, 14:34-35; Eph 5:22-33; Col 3:18-19; 1 Tim 2:11-15; Titus 2:3-5; 1 Pet 3:1-7; Prov 31; and the wisdom, narrative, and typological material that surrounds them), read together with — not against — its reception across five historical strands: continental Reformation exegesis, the Reformed-orthodox/Puritan household-duty tradition, the Westminster Standards and Divines, the Seceder and Covenanting churches, and the early American and Southern Presbyterians. Contemporary Reformed interlocutors (Zachary Garris, Jay Adams, Shawn Mathis, and others in the same vein) are engaged as living voices continuing this same tradition, credited for convergence and read honestly where they diverge — not treated as a debate this project exists to win.

See `00-foundations/manifesto.md` for the full statement.

## How this project differs from its sibling project (pro-rege)

This project shares its lane structure and production discipline with the `pro-rege` project (natural law / covenant of works and grace) in the same parent directory, but the working relationship is different: **Claude Code takes the primary drafting role here.** The project owner directs, corrects, supplies source leads (like the theologian list that seeded Strands 1–2), and reviews — but the first-pass research, reading-note production, and drafting of manifesto/series prose is Claude Code's job, done with real sourcing and confidence-marking discipline (see below), not held back pending the owner's own reading. Treat every draft as reviewable-and-correctable, not as a finished position — the owner's correction is expected and welcome, especially on anything marked `[VERIFY]` or UNVERIFIED.

## Provisional house rules (supersede with `style-guide.md` once it exists)

- **Scripture quotation:** KJV by default, matching the sibling project's convention and the register of the primary sources being engaged. Confirm with the project owner before treating this as locked.
- **Confessional citation:** always cite the original Westminster Confession (1646) / Larger and Shorter Catechisms (1647) wording specifically, plus the 1647 Directory for Family Worship (Church of Scotland) where relevant. Two distinct loci, don't conflate them: **WCF 24** ("Of Marriage and Divorce") addresses the *institution* of marriage — purposes, who may marry, divorce — and never names headship or roles. **WLC 123-133** (the fifth commandment's "superiors, inferiors, or equals" material) is the real confessional anchor for domestic authority, and — this took verifying, don't assume it from memory — the Assembly's own annexed proof-texts explicitly place the wife on the inferior side and the husband on the superior side of that framework: Q127 cites 1 Pet. 3:6 (Sarah "obeying Abraham, calling him lord") for an inferior's honor, and Q129 cites Col. 3:19 and 1 Pet. 3:7 for a superior's duty. See `.claude/agents/confessional-reviewer.md` for the full citation discipline this requires, and note the Assembly minutes (Van Dixhoorn edition) are still worth consulting for finer detail on how the Divines themselves debated this. Where later American revisions differ, state the difference explicitly. **Correction, 2026-08-14**: one has now turned up — the PCUSA's 1887 revision removed WCF 24.4's final sentence (the deceased-wife's-sister-marriage/kindred prohibition: "The man may not marry any of his wife's kindred nearer in blood than he may of his own, nor the woman of her husband's kindred nearer in blood than of her own"), confirmed by direct comparison against the original text plus a corroborating secondary source. This is an affinity/kindred-marriage question, not a headship/roles one, so it doesn't touch this project's central claims, but the blanket "none have yet turned up" statement was wrong and is corrected here. No revision has yet turned up in WLC 123-133 specifically — that part still stands.
- **Uncertainty discipline:** never assert a specific claim about what a named historical or contemporary author held without a verifiable source. If a claim can't be confirmed, say so plainly with an inline `[VERIFY: ...]` marker rather than smoothing it into confident prose. This matters more here than in most projects — this topic attracts confident overstatement on all sides, and the project's credibility depends on not doing that.
- **Pre-19th-century primary sources:** preserve original spelling/orthography in direct quotation (e.g., "stedfastly," "shew"); modernize only in paraphrase, and mark paraphrase as paraphrase.
- **Living authors** (Garris, Adams — recently deceased 2020, treat with the same sourcing care as a living author — Mathis, and others) are engaged as Reformed brothers continuing a shared tradition, not as opponents. Where this project's reading of a text or historical figure differs from theirs, say so as a difference among allies, not a takedown.
- **Historical diversity is a feature, not noise.** The five historical strands do not say identical things — e.g., how strongly a given writer grounds headship in creation order (1 Cor 11) versus the fall (1 Tim 2), how much weight is given to mutuality-of-duty language, how "helper" (Gen 2:18, *ezer kenegdo*) is handled. Track real differences of emphasis explicitly; don't flatten five centuries into "the Reformed view."
- **Register:** rigorous and exegetically serious, but the project's own stated goal is practical — every series should eventually cash out in "how does a husband/wife actually live this," not stop at doctrine stated abstractly. See the pastoral-practical lane.
- **Both roles get equal, positive research weight — checked explicitly, not assumed.** Caught 2026-08-06: an early batch of research notes documented the husband's headship at real depth and reduced the wife's side to "submits" / "his submissive helper," even though the source material (Prov 31, Titus 2:3-5, 1 Pet 3:1-6, and the household-duty writers themselves) has substantial positive content on a wife's own duties, competence, initiative, and calling — not merely what she owes her husband's authority. This is a research-coverage failure, not a feature of the sources. Every reading note and every series article must ask the wife's-side question with the same specificity as the husband's-side question before it's considered complete; a note that's rich on headship and thin on the wife's own positive role is unfinished, not balanced by nature of the topic.
- **Both roles get the FULL treatment, symmetrically — not "wife gets protection, husband gets warned."** Clarified 2026-08-14, refining the rule above: equal weight isn't satisfied by giving the wife positive/competence content while giving the husband cautionary/abuse-of-authority content, as if that were the natural division of labor. For *both* husband and wife, pursue: (1) the role itself, (2) responsibilities/duties, (3) the characteristic sins, temptations, and warnings Scripture and the sources attach to *that* role specifically (a wife's besetting sins — contentiousness, usurping authority, folly — get the same direct treatment as a husband's — tyranny, abdication, harshness), and (4) pastoral/nouthetic counsel addressed to that person, in their own right. Apply this to whatever degree Scripture and a given source actually supports it — don't force a claim the text doesn't make — but ask the question for both roles, every time, the same way the wife's-positive-content rule above already requires.
- **Surface who/what a source is actually arguing against.** Added 2026-08-14: a source's emphases, silences, and rhetorical intensity usually make more sense once you know its occasion — what controversy, opponent, or cultural moment it was written into. Musculus's anti-sacramental-marriage argument only fully lands once Rome's sacramental view is named as the target; Adams's ch. 1 "sin, not sickness" framing in *Christian Living in the Home* (1972) is a direct reaction to the Rogerian/psychoanalytic pastoral-counseling model dominant at the time; Bucer's unusually permissive divorce argument sits against medieval canon law. Note the interlocutor/occasion explicitly where it's known, and flag it as unknown rather than silent where it isn't — this is a distinct question from whether the source's own position is correct, and distinct from the confessional/contemporary-interlocutor lanes' job of relating the source to *this project's* argument.
- **Follow each source's own structure; don't impose a uniform external grid.** Different writers break the topic down differently — general theology of marriage, then roles, then within each role its duties and prohibitions, sometimes with further sub-distinctions — and a reading note should surface *that source's own* categories and words rather than flattening every source into an identical template (see "Historical diversity is a feature, not noise" below, which this extends specifically to the roles/duties/warnings breakdown). The point of the rule above is to make sure the same *kinds* of questions get asked of every source, not to make every note look the same shape.

## The seven editorial lanes

This project divides labor across seven Claude Code subagents in `.claude/agents/`. **Auto-invocation is unreliable in practice** — Claude often just does the work in the main session instead of delegating, even when a matching subagent exists. Invoke explicitly: *"Use the exegete subagent to draft the Ephesians 5 section of II.3"* rather than assuming Claude will route there on its own.

| Lane | File | Job |
|---|---|---|
| A | `exegete.md` | Biblical-theological spine every systematic and historical claim answers to |
| B | `historical-researcher.md` | Primary-source accuracy: Reformation, Reformed Scholastics/Puritans, Westminster Divines, Seceder/Covenanter, early American & Southern Presbyterians |
| C | `systematic-theologian.md` | Philosophical/systematic synthesis: theological anthropology, nature-grace, history of redemption, the *telos* of marriage |
| D | `confessional-reviewer.md` | Checks drafts against 1646 WCF/LC/SC and the 1647 Directory for Family Worship specifically |
| E | `contemporary-interlocutor.md` | Fair, sourced representation of Garris, Adams, Mathis, and other living Reformed voices |
| F | `pastoral-practical.md` | The "so what" — how headship and submission are actually practiced, wisely, in an ordinary marriage |
| G | `editor-style.md` | House style enforcement and final read-through |

Default handoff order for a new article: **A + C** (gather exegetical and systematic-theological material) → **B** (historical confirmation/complication) → **D** (check against confessional text) → **E** (only for articles engaging contemporary positions) → **F** (application) → **G** (final pass). Revise per-article as needed; it's a default, not a rule.

## Non-goals

- **Not a "hard patriarchy" or grievance project, and not a defense of "complementarianism" as a modern label either.** The goal is a positive, whole account of what Scripture and its best interpreters actually give us, built from the ground up — not a reaction to feminism or egalitarianism for its own sake, not licensing an authoritarian distortion of headship, and not simply restating mainstream complementarianism because it's the safer-sounding modern option. See "Not a triangulation between modern labels" above. If a draft's polemical energy outweighs its constructive statement of the positive doctrine, that's drift.
- Not a general-audience marriage-advice site — this is exegetically and historically grounded first.
- Not flattening five centuries of Reformed reflection into a single undifferentiated "the Reformed view" — real internal variety gets tracked, not smoothed over.
- Not treating Garris, Adams, Mathis, or other living Reformed interlocutors as opponents — see `.claude/agents/contemporary-interlocutor.md`.
