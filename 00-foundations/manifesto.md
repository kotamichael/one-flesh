# One Flesh: Manifesto

*Working title — Gen 2:24 / Eph 5:31. Drafted 2026-08-07, first pass. Confirm before treating as locked.*

## Purpose

This is a constructive project before it is a critical one. Its aim is to reconstruct and state clearly the Reformed Presbyterian doctrine of marriage and marriage roles — the God-given order of headship and submission within marriage, and the mutual, positive duties of husband and wife — from close exegesis of Scripture, confirmed and sharpened by five centuries of its best Reformed interpreters, so that Christian men and women can see that order plainly and be equipped to actually live it out. It is a whole account before it is a defended one: egalitarian readings and a distorted, authoritarian "hard patriarchy" are both real errors, but this project's first task is not refuting either — it is reconstructing, positively, what Scripture and its faithful interpreters actually give us.

## Thesis

Marriage is a creation ordinance (Gen 2:18-24) in which God has ordered husband and wife toward one another under a real, non-interchangeable structure — the husband as head, the wife as his helper and, under that headship, his glory (1 Cor 11:7-9) — without either party ceasing to be, equally and fully, the image of God (Gen 1:27). This order is not a bare positive law imposed on otherwise-undifferentiated persons; it is grounded in creation itself (1 Cor 11:8-9; 1 Tim 2:13), confirmed and intensified in its distortion by the Fall (Gen 3:16), and finally re-purposed and glorified in Christ, who fills headship and submission with a content neither could have had before the incarnation: the husband's headship patterned on Christ's own headship over the church — a headship that loves at the cost of self (Eph 5:25-29) and that rules, corrects, and defends (WSC 26, WLC 45) — and the wife's submission patterned on the church's own glad, secure submission to a Lord who gave himself for her. **Both halves of this thesis have to be held at once, or the doctrine breaks**: a headship without self-giving love degenerates into domination; a submission without the wife's own full dignity, competence, and active governance within her sphere (Prov 31; WLC 129's "protecting, and providing... for soul and body" cuts both ways) degenerates into a caricature neither Scripture nor this project's own sources will bear.

## What is at stake

Getting this wrong fails in one of two directions, and this project treats both as live errors, not one as real and the other as a strawman.

Deny the order altogether — read Eph 5:22-33, 1 Cor 11:3-16, Col 3:18-19, 1 Tim 2:11-15, and 1 Pet 3:1-7 as culturally local accommodations with no abiding creational warrant — and Paul's own appeal to *creation*, not merely custom, as the ground of headship (1 Cor 11:8-9, "For the man is not of the woman; but the woman of the man... for this cause ought the woman to have power on her head, because of the angels"; 1 Tim 2:13, "For Adam was first formed, then Eve") becomes an argument that has to be explained away rather than answered. This is the egalitarian failure.

Overclaim the order — treat headship as entitling a husband to functional control over a wife's conscience, competence, or person, or treat submission as requiring silence in the face of sin or abuse — and the result betrays the very pattern it claims to imitate. WLC 130 names this precisely as a sin *of superiors*: "an inordinate seeking of themselves, their own glory, ease, profit, or pleasure... correcting them unduly... provoking them to wrath... by an unjust, indiscreet, rigorous, or remiss behavior." A husband who governs this way has not exceeded Christ's pattern of headship — he has inverted it. This is the hard-patriarchy failure, and this project treats it as a real and present danger in contemporary complementarian circles, not a merely hypothetical one to wave off (see "Relation to contemporary treatments" below).

Getting this right constructively is the goal; getting it right *practically* — "wise as Solomon and Christ" — is equally the goal, not an afterthought appended once the doctrine is settled.

## Confessional anchor

Two distinct loci in the Standards, and this project keeps them distinct rather than running them together:

- **WCF 24** ("Of Marriage and Divorce") addresses the *institution* of marriage: one man and one woman (24.1); its three purposes — "the mutual help of husband and wife, for the increase of mankind with legitimate issue, and of the church with an holy seed; and for preventing of uncleanness" (24.2); who may lawfully marry (24.3-4); and divorce (24.5-6). It says nothing about headship, submission, or roles.
- **WLC 123-133** (the fifth commandment — "Honour thy father and thy mother") is the real confessional locus for domestic authority, and it is substantial. Q124 defines "father and mother" to mean "not only natural parents, but all superiors in age and gifts; and especially such as, by God's ordinance, are over us in place of authority, whether in family, church, or commonwealth." Q126 gives the commandment's "general scope" as "the performance of those duties which we mutually owe in our several relations, as inferiors, superiors, or equals." **The Assembly's own annexed proof-texts — not merely a later tradition's inference — place the wife on the inferior side and the husband on the superior side of this framework**: Q127 ("the honour that inferiors owe to their superiors") cites 1 Pet 3:6, Sarah "obeying Abraham, calling him lord"; Q129 ("what is required of superiors towards their inferiors") cites Col 3:19 and 1 Pet 3:7, the husband's own duty to his wife. Q129 and Q130 — respectively the positive duties and the sins of a superior — are this project's single most load-bearing confessional text for what headship should and should not look like in practice (see `.claude/agents/confessional-reviewer.md` and `.claude/agents/pastoral-practical.md` for the full citation discipline this requires).
- **The Directory for the Publick Worship of God (1645)**, "The Solemnization of Marriage," adds a third, independent data point: the wife's vow alone includes "obedient" ("a loving, faithful, and obedient wife," against the husband's "a loving and faithful husband") — a directly Assembly-sanctioned liturgical embedding of the same asymmetry.
- **The Directory for Family Worship (1647)** governs household worship leadership under gender-neutral "head/master of the family" language, and — checked directly against the full primary text — does *not* itself specify a husband-then-wife-in-his-absence succession; it presupposes a patriarchal household order without arguing for or explicitly gendering it. The more specific succession logic lives, as predicted, in Gouge's own casuistry rather than this Directory: *Of Domesticall Duties* (Treatise II, Part II, §40) gives a genuine three-tier succession for family prayer, reading, and catechizing — the husband by default; **the wife herself**, explicitly named, triggered by his absence, negligence, or inability, and qualified by "so farre as she is able"; and "some other" as a final fallback if she does not perform them herself. This is also, independently, real positive wife's-side content: Gouge grounds her own initiative in these duties, not merely her filling in for an absent husband.

These citations are checked against the original (1646/1647) text, not later American revisions — none have yet turned up affecting either WCF 24 or WLC 123-133, but this is confirmed rather than assumed, per standing house rule.

## Historical method

This project reads five historical strands as a living conversation, not a single flat "the Reformed view" — real internal variation is a feature to be tracked, not smoothed over.

**Strand 1 — Reformation-era continental exegesis** (Oecolampadius, Musculus, Vermigli, Bullinger, Calvin) is a genuine internal spread on the single question of where headship's authority originates. Musculus draws the sharpest line: his taxonomy of divinely-given "laws" places *no* rule-language in the pre-Fall "law of wedlock" (Gen 2:24) at all, introducing the wife's subjection only as a distinct, later, post-Fall law (Gen 3:16). Oecolampadius similarly locates subordination-language after the Fall, against a backdrop of pre-Fall "equal dignity," though he registers a "made for him" teleology even in Eden. Calvin and Vermigli both hold a continuity view instead — Calvin's own formula is the strand's clearest statement of it: "she had, indeed, previously been subject to her husband, but that was a liberal and gentle subjection; now, however, she is cast into servitude" (Gen 3:16 comm.) — a real, pre-Fall headship that the Fall intensifies rather than originates; Vermigli reports this as a live patristic dispute (citing Chrysostom's denial of any pre-Fall subjection) before landing on a similar continuity position, distinctively argued through Aristotelian political categories — headship as "the Dominion of Aristocratia," rule by "the chief, which excelleth in virtue and worthiness," explicitly excluding "tyranny." Calvin alone integrates this exegesis into an operative civil-legal reform program (Geneva's 1546 Marriage Ordinance) and gives the strand's strongest mutuality formula: "Let the man perform to the woman the office of the head... and let the woman perform to the man the office of the body" (1 Cor 11 comm.). Bullinger's *Christian State of Matrimony* was the single most-circulated marriage manual of the Reformation in England; a second pass located his own headship language — "let the husband be the head of the wife, to wit, her adviser and counsellor, her ruler and guide" — grounded in the creation of Eve "to be his help & companion," plus a direct 1 Pet 3:1 citation, though 1 Cor 11 and Col 3:18-19 are now checked, confirmed absences from his corpus rather than merely unlocated. See `research/reading-notes/strand-1-reformation-continental/five-reformers-overview.md`.

**Strand 2 — Reformed-orthodox and Puritan household-duty writers** (Zanchi, Cartwright, Perkins, Gouge, Cleaver & Dod, Baxter, Rogers, Richard Steele, and the later evangelical continuation in John Angell James). This strand is the best-documented, and shows real, live internal disagreement worth preserving rather than flattening: Perkins and Rogers ground headship in creation (1 Cor 11:7's "superior sex" language; Rogers's striking move of grounding headship directly in the man's especially resembling "the image of God"); Cleaver & Dod ground it explicitly in Gen 3:16 and the Fall; Richard Steele and John Angell James both hold creation-order and fall-confirmation together deliberately, stacking 1 Tim 2:13, 1 Cor 11:8-9, and Gen 3:16 as mutually reinforcing rather than choosing one. Gouge himself, on his own fullest resolution of the tension (Treatise I), holds both together too: Gen 3:16 is "a law... for trial of her obedience" *and* an intensification of an order already implied by the woman's having "been made before the fall, that the man might rule over her" — the creation-argument doing the heavier structural work, the fall-argument supplementing it. Every figure in this strand pairs a husband's authority-duty with a corresponding love-duty and treats the reverse failure — a husband's "abuse of his authority" (Gouge) — as the primary source of marital misery, not a wife's insubordination.

Gouge's *Of Domesticall Duties* is this strand's anchor text precisely because of its casuistic specificity: he itemizes real disputed cases (which household expenditures need a husband's consent, which don't, what a wife's standing is even where she lacks independent property, and — per §40 — exactly when and how she takes over the family's devotional leadership) the way a counselor handles real marriages, not the way a system-builder states doctrine. John Angell James, writing two centuries later outside any of this project's confessional strands proper (English Congregationalist, not Presbyterian) but squarely within this genre, supplies this strand's most distinctive positive-content addition: an explicit argument that a wife should have independent financial and charitable discretion in her own name — "why should she have no sphere of benevolent effort?" — calling its denial itself "degrading." See `research/reading-notes/strand-2-reformed-orthodox-puritan/household-duty-writers-overview.md` and `john-angell-james.md` for the full sourced detail, including Gouge's "though the man be as the head, yet is the woman as the heart," his "joint governor" language, his "love is as sugar... respect is as salt" pairing, and Cartwright's own Proverbs 31 commentary, which reads the wife's praise as grounding a reciprocal husbandly duty rather than standing alone.

**Strand 3 — The Westminster Divines and Standards.** Beyond the confessional anchor above and the Gouge succession-clause finding folded in there, William Gouge was himself a seated member of the Assembly (named by the 12 June 1643 ordinance, chairing a drafting committee in 1644) even though *Of Domesticall Duties* predates the Assembly by two decades. Rutherford, Gillespie, Burgess, and Vines — despite being seated Divines — show no located marriage-roles writing distinct from their better-known work in political and church-government theology; this is reported as a genuine negative finding, not an unfinished search. Herbert Palmer is a partial exception in kind, not content: his *Memorials of Godliness* (checked directly, fullest 1670 edition) contains no household-duty doctrine at all, but the volume's own prefixed biography confirms his practice — twice-daily family prayer and Scripture reading, twice-weekly catechizing — a data point about what he *did*, not what he *taught*.

**Strand 4 — Seceder and Covenanting churches.** Thinner than the mainstream Puritan corpus, consistent with this tradition's own self-understanding as primarily testimony/ecclesiology-focused rather than household-duty-focused. Adam Gib and William Symington yielded no confirmed marriage-roles content (Symington's silence reads as a genuine negative finding — his corpus is uniformly Christological). Two real, substantive finds now stand in for what was a thin strand: the RPCNA's *Reformation Principles Exhibited* (1871) Chapter XXVIII, "Of Marriage," now retrieved in full, shows the identical structural move already documented in WLC 123-133 — its own propositions state husband/wife duties symmetrically ("mutual love," "mutual recrimination"), with the role-asymmetry carried entirely by its annexed proof-texts (Col 3:19, 1 Pet 3:1-4) — and, distinctively, **never cites Ephesians 5 anywhere**, unlike the Puritan household-duty tradition's characteristically Ephesians-centered exposition. John Brown of Haddington's Self-Interpreting Bible, now confirmed on Eph 5, Col 3, and 1 Pet 3 directly, supplies a genuinely load-bearing qualifier repeated across two separate passages: wifely submission holds "as far as consists with... superior relation and obedience to Christ," and "in all things lawful" — bounding the doctrine explicitly, a specific 18th-century data point worth carrying into the systematic-theologian and pastoral-practical lanes' own treatment of limits. George Scipione, initially misidentified as RPCNA-ordained, was in fact **OPC-ordained** with a confirmed RPCNA-institutional bridge (Director of the Biblical Counseling Institute at RPTS, 2008-2018) — his one dedicated book, *The Battle for the Biblical Family* (Crown & Covenant, 2018), remains `[VERIFY]` for actual content pending direct access.

**Strand 5 — Early American Presbyterians and Southern Presbyterians** (Alexander, Miller, Thornwell, Dabney, Palmer) contains this project's most important internal fork, and it sits between two men who knew each other's work. Both Old School Princeton figures, Archibald Alexander and Samuel Miller, produced no doctrinal marriage-roles treatise — Alexander explicitly named this a real gap in American Presbyterian literature and declined, for reasons of scope, to fill it himself. James Henley Thornwell, despite his prominence as a theorist of "social relations" (in defense of slavery), likewise produced no marriage-roles doctrine on full-text verification of his *Collected Writings* — crediting him with one would be a misattribution the project should actively guard against. It falls to **Robert Lewis Dabney** and **Benjamin Morgan Palmer** to do this strand's real constructive work, and they do not agree on the same question Strand 1 splits on: Dabney holds that the wife's subjection is in part **punishment for Eve's sin, imputed to all women since** ("the sentence... has been extended, by imputation, to all her daughters"), while Palmer holds headship/subordination is a **creation ordinance in its content**, with the Fall changing only its *mode of enforcement* — from voluntary harmony to "positive statute" — never its substance. Dabney alone stacks all five canonical proof-texts into a single argument and extends it into a "ruling elder" syllogism reaching church office and civil suffrage; Palmer alone develops Eph 5:22-33 as marriage's own positive Christological symbol ("the Symbolical Mystery of Marriage") rather than only a hierarchy proof-text — stating directly that "St. Paul is not here explaining union with Christ by marriage... but he is illustrating marriage by union with Christ," marriage itself "the hieroglyph over the very portals of marriage, not to be deciphered until the key was furnished in the work of redemption" — and is the strand's most emphatic voice that submission's voluntariness is itself theologically load-bearing. This is a real disagreement between two major Southern Presbyterians, not a single "Southern Presbyterian view."

A closer reading of Palmer turned up this strand's richest positive wife's-side material anywhere in the project, built entirely from Gen 2, Eph 5, and Col 3 without ever invoking Prov 31: husband and wife are "the joint rulers over the domestic state," and "within her sphere the wife wields an **original authority**, which she acquires through her submission... it belongs to her station, and cannot be divorced from it" — drawn to an explicitly positive conclusion, not a consolation prize: "in her office she has gained dominion and power. It is not a sacrifice without compensation. She resigns independence, but secures control." This is a genuine, independent convergence with Gouge's "joint governor" language in Strand 2 — two figures a century and continent apart reaching for the same category by different textual routes. Dabney's parallel material is real but markedly thinner — a single "queen of his home" image in his 1871 suffrage essay, and, in his 1879 preaching essay, an explicit disclaimer that the preaching-exclusion rests on inferior capacity, paired with praise of named New Testament women — but neither develops into a doctrine of the wife's domestic competence the way Palmer's does. See `research/reading-notes/strand-5-american-southern-presbyterian/five-figures-overview.md`.

**A cross-strand convergence worth naming directly, independent of the creation-vs-fall question the strands above split on**: Zanchi's *De Spirituali Connubio* ("our entire salvation consists in this spiritual marriage," Eve's creation read as a type of the Church's own origin from Christ's pierced side), Palmer's Eph 5 exposition (above), and Jay Adams's opening move in *Christian Living in the Home* (a Christian home is defined by having its resources — the admission of sin, precepts for addressing it, the means of grace — "centered around the person of Christ," Col 2:3) all run the same direction independently: marriage does not explain Christ; Christ explains, and supplies, what marriage needs. Three genres wide apart — 16th-century Reformed typological exposition, 19th-century Southern Presbyterian systematics, 20th-century nouthetic counseling — arriving at the same directional claim without depending on one another is itself evidence for this project's method: convergence discovered across independent strands, not assumed going in (see CLAUDE.md).

## Relation to contemporary treatments

*Working section, drafted 2026-08-07 from a first research pass — confidence markers follow the historical-researcher convention: CONFIRMED, WELL-ATTESTED, UNVERIFIED.*

The contemporary Reformed field on marriage roles is not a single position. **Zachary Garris** (*Masculine Christianity*, knowingscripture.com) argues male headship as creation-ordered rule, taking a harder line than mainstream complementarianism on the extent of a husband's actual, exercised authority and on public-worship restrictions (1 Cor 14:34-35) — a sympathetic review of his own book notes he does comparatively little direct exegesis of Eph 5's self-sacrificial-love clause, a real and worth-naming asymmetry in his published argument (CONFIRMED). **Jay Adams**, as a working nouthetic counselor rather than a systematician, reduces headship and submission to direct, livable counseling directives — his practical specificity is a genuine asset this project draws on directly (see `.claude/agents/pastoral-practical.md`). A follow-up pass located the wife-addressed parallel this project's first pass had missed entirely — *Christian Living in the Home*'s ch. 6, "A Word to the Wives," mirroring ch. 7's "Loving Leadership" to husbands — closing what had been a real, self-acknowledged imbalance in this project's own sourcing rather than in Adams's actual corpus. **Shawn Mathis** (OPC) has engaged this question directly and recently, and both sides of the exchange are now represented on their own terms rather than through one side's paraphrase of the other. Mathis's March 2026 three-post series defends creation-order headship — stacking 1 Tim 2:12-14, the Adam/Christ typology of 1 Cor 15:47-48 and Rom 5, and Eve's formation "from Adam's side" — against **Aaron Mize**'s OPC *Ordained Servant* article (subsequently pulled from opc.org, with its own editor issuing a public apology that also disclosed a real quotation/paraphrase conflation in the piece — a cautionary parallel to this project's own sourcing discipline). Mize's own argument, so far as it survives in secondhand reproduction (the primary text is currently unobtainable), is not a flat denial of headship but a relocation of it: he distinguishes Christ's *kingship* ("hierarchical or official," which he associates with fallen categories) from Christ's *headship* ("organic and mystical," a body-unity category), arguing Eph 5 borrows the second for marriage — a specific, arguable reading of κεφαλή, not simply "wives don't submit," and one this project should engage on those terms if it engages Mize at all. Neither Mathis nor Mize, on current evidence, develops anything positive about the wife's own role specifically — a real, stated gap on both sides of this particular exchange, not a point scored against either.

A further intramural voice worth tracking in the same OPC orbit is **Rachel Green Miller** (*Beyond Authority and Submission*, P&R 2019), an OPC member whose book argues the tradition has let "authority and submission" become the sole controlling lens for male-female relationships, at the cost of "unity, interdependence, and service." Garris himself reviewed her and concluded "at root, Rachel Green Miller is an egalitarian" despite her own professed male-headship and male-only-ordination views; other reviewers (Mere Liberty; an OPC ruling elder's review) read her far more charitably. **This project states that disagreement as unresolved between two named, sourced contemporary voices — it is not this project's job to adjudicate it without independent exegetical work of its own** — but notes that Miller's stated project is itself an attempt at exactly the wife's-side correction this project's own house rules require, whatever one concludes about how well she executes it. **C.R. Wiley**, **Douglas Wilson**, and **Kevin DeYoung** occupy a real range within confessional and quasi-confessional Presbyterian circles from harder to more moderate complementarianism. A further pass identified more academically substantial Presbyterian scholarship: **George W. Knight III** (OPC/PCA; *The Role Relationship of Men and Women*, 1977/1985; the NIGTC *Pastoral Epistles*, 1992) grounds headship in creation order and in a word-study of *authenteō* (1 Tim 2:12) arguing the "authority" in view is legitimate, not domineering — but his corpus also contains this project's clearest cautionary tale: Knight was the first (1977) to analogize male headship/female submission to the Father-Son relation in 1 Cor 11:3, a move that became, decades later, the seed of the 2016 Eternal Functional Subordination controversy (Grudem, Ware, vs. Trueman, Goligher, and other confessional critics) over whether it compromises Nicene Trinitarian orthodoxy. **This project will not adopt the Trinity-analogy argument** — it is a live warning about where an otherwise-legitimate creation-order argument can go wrong, not a resource to draw on. **J. Ligon Duncan III** (PCA; RTS Chancellor) explicitly distinguishes "male headship" from "male domination" ("The husband is not a tyrannical monarch"), limits submission's scope directly ("Your husband tells you to sin... the answer is 'No!'"), and engages Prov 31 to argue a submissive wife "offers suggestions and advice and counsel, and even correction to her husband." **Richard D. Phillips** (PCA; *The Masculine Mandate*) writes primarily to men about a husband's own calling — leadership that "typically initiates and always leads shared discussions" while "the wife's opinion is vitally important" — and this project's own research pass did not turn up comparably developed material from him on the wife's own competence and calling in her own right; that may reflect the book's stated scope (an address to men) rather than a defect in it, and should be represented as a research-coverage note, not a verdict on the work. **Wayne Grudem does not belong in this bibliography**: he is a confessing credobaptist ("Reformed Baptist"), not Presbyterian, though his *Evangelical Feminism and Biblical Truth* is where Knight's Trinity-analogy argument became mainstream and was later publicly contested — worth a footnote on that lineage, not inclusion as a confessional-Presbyterian source.

This project's own distinctive is not that no one else is doing any part of this work — Garris, Adams, Mathis, Wiley, Wilson, and DeYoung each already occupy a piece of it. It is that the whole synthesis — Reformation-era continental exegesis, the Reformed-orthodox/Puritan household-duty tradition in its real internal variety, the Westminster Standards read with confessional-citation precision (proof-texts included, not just headline answers), the Seceder/Covenanter and American/Southern Presbyterian reception, and the contemporary field represented fairly across its own internal range — has not yet been put in one place, argued from the text outward, and carried through to genuinely practical, "wise as Solomon and Christ" application rather than stopping at doctrine stated abstractly.

## Scope

- **Series I — Anthropology & Foundations.** The image of God, sexual differentiation, the creation ordinance of marriage and its threefold purpose (WCF 24.2), and the nature-grace question: is this order knowable in principle from general revelation alone, or only rightly recoverable in Christ?
- **Series II — Exegetical Core.** The canonical headship/submission texts worked closely and individually — Gen 1-3, 1 Cor 11:3-16, Eph 5:22-33, Col 3:18-19, 1 Tim 2:11-15, 1 Pet 3:1-7, Prov 31 — with live exegetical disputes (creation vs. fall grounding; κεφαλή's semantic range; the Eph 5:22 ὑποτάσσω supplied from v.21) named honestly rather than resolved by fiat.
- **Series III — Historical Reception.** The five historical strands synthesized, real internal variation preserved.
- **Series IV — Contemporary Engagement.** Garris, Adams, Mathis, Wiley, Wilson, DeYoung, Knight, Duncan, Phillips, and other living voices, engaged as intramural brothers per `.claude/agents/contemporary-interlocutor.md`.
- **Series V — Practice.** The pastoral, practical "so what" — ordinary daily life and decision-making, premarital formation, and marriages under strain — grounded first in WLC 129-130's own concrete language for a superior's duties and sins, then in Gouge's and Adams's practical casuistry together (see `.claude/agents/pastoral-practical.md`).

## Non-goals

- **Not a "hard patriarchy" or grievance project**, and not a reaction to feminism or egalitarianism for its own sake. If a draft's critical energy outweighs its constructive statement of the positive doctrine, that is drift from this project's actual purpose.
- **Not a project that documents male headship at length and treats the wife's side as an appendage.** This failure mode surfaced concretely during this project's own first research pass (see CLAUDE.md) and is treated as a standing discipline every lane must actively guard against, not a risk already handled.
- Not a general-audience marriage-advice site — exegetically and historically grounded first, practical second (but genuinely second, not omitted).
- Not flattening five centuries of Reformed reflection, or a genuinely diverse contemporary field, into a single undifferentiated position.
- Not treating any living contemporary interlocutor as an opponent — see `.claude/agents/contemporary-interlocutor.md`.

## Closing

The reason to get this right is not architectural elegance, and it is not victory in a contemporary argument. It is that two people, standing before God in a covenant neither fully chose in advance to understand, are being told — in the plainest terms Scripture and its best interpreters can give — how to actually love each other for the whole of a life: the husband called to a headship that gives itself away rather than extracts, the wife called to a submission that is glad and secure rather than coerced, both together the visible sign, however imperfectly kept, "concerning Christ and the church" (Eph 5:32) — a mystery neither of them invented and neither of them may finally take credit for keeping, but which either of them, by God's ordinary grace, may actually live.
